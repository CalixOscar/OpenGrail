# Donyi-Polo

## Historical context

Donyi-Polo (literally "Sun-Moon") is the traditional indigenous religion of the Tani ethnic group—encompassing the Adi, Apatani, Nyishi, Galo, Tagin, and Mishing peoples—inhabiting the eastern Himalayan state of Arunachal Pradesh and upper Assam in Northeast India. For centuries, Tani religious life was decentralized, transmitted orally through hereditary shamans (*Nyibu* or *Miri*) who chanted monumental genealogies and creation myths connecting human clans directly to cosmic ancestors.

During the late 1960s and 1970s, as Christian missionary activity expanded rapidly across Northeast India alongside growing cultural assimilation pressures from lowland Hindu institutions, prominent Tani intellectuals and community leaders—foremost among them Talom Rukbo (1937–2001), revered as the father of modern Donyi-Poloism—initiated an organized religious reformation. In 1968, Rukbo helped establish the *Donyi-Polo Mission* and subsequently the *Donyi-Polo Yelam Kebang* (1986). This movement formalized the ancestral oral tradition into an institutional religion with standardized congregational liturgies, permanent prayer halls (*gangging*), written hymns (*Agam*), and institutional schools, effectively preventing widespread cultural erasure and asserting indigenous religious autonomy within modern India.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

In Tani philosophy, *Donyi* (the Sun, associated with motherly warmth, illumination, and life-giving energy) and *Polo* (the Moon, associated with fatherly vigilance, nocturnal protection, and cosmic rhythm) are not worshiped as physical celestial bodies, but as the visible, twin manifestations of the supreme, unmanifested transcendent creator (*Sedi-Melo* or *Pedong Nane*). Donyi-Polo represents the supreme cosmic principle of divine omniscience, moral truth, and universal justice (*dharma* / *tulu*), acting as the eternal witness to all human actions and speech.

Key elements of modern and traditional Donyi-Polo practice include:
- *The Gangging*: Community prayer halls constructed with indigenous architectural features, where congregation members gather on Sundays for collective prayer, moral discourses, and singing of standardized devotional hymns.
- *The Nyibu (Shaman)*: Traditional shamans who retain critical roles for healing, divination (such as inspecting chicken livers or egg yolks for omens), and presiding over major rites of passage by chanting the sacred *Aabang* (monumental oral epics recounting the genealogy of creation).
- *Agrarian Festivals*: Communal celebrations tied to agricultural cycles and spirit propitiation, including *Solung* (harvest festival of the Adi), *Mopin* (spring fertility festival of the Galo), *Dree* (agricultural festival of the Apatani), and *Nyokum* (pre-harvest harmony festival of the Nyishi).
- *Ethical Harmony (Donyi-Polo Ane)*: Moral behavior is governed by strict adherence to truthfulness, communal solidarity, respect for nature spirits (*Wiyu*), and customary tribal jurisprudence (*Kebang* council system).
