# Sanamahism

## Historical context

Sanamahism is the ancestral, indigenous religion of the Meitei (Manipuri) people of the Imphal Valley in Northeast India (historically the Kingdom of Kangleipak). Rooted in deep antiquity and documented in thousands of archaic Meitei-script palm-leaf and paper manuscripts known as *Puyas*, the tradition served as the official state religion of Manipur for centuries.

In the early eighteenth century, during the reign of King Pamheiba (Garib Niwaj, r. 1709–1748), Bengali Gaudiya Vaishnavism was introduced to the royal court by the guru Shantidas Goswami. In 1729, the monarchy declared Gaudiya Vaishnavism the state religion and ordered the systematic confiscation and public burning of sacred Meitei Puya texts—a traumatic historical event remembered as the *Puya Meithaba*. Despite intense state-mandated Hinduization, Meitei families steadfastly preserved Sanamahi worship within domestic spaces and clan sanctuaries.

In the mid-twentieth century, organized cultural revivalist movements—notably the *Sanamahi Sanstha* and the *Meetei Marup* (founded in 1945)—launched a vigorous renaissance. This revival championed the restoration of the original Meitei Mayek script, the re-consecration of historical temples at the ancestral royal seat of Kangla, and the formal disaffiliation of Meiteis from Vaishnavite Hinduism in favor of authentic indigenous Sanamahism.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Sanamahism possesses a rich cosmological and theological structure:
- *The Divine Triad and Creator*: The transcendent source of all existence is *Yaibirel Sidaba* (the Immortal Supreme Being), who manifested the universe through *Leimarel Sidabi* (the Primordial Mother Earth) and her two divine sons, *Lainingthou Sanamahi* and *Pakhangba*.
- *Lainingthou Sanamahi*: The solar creator deity and sovereign protector of human dwellings. Every traditional Meitei home maintains a consecrated domestic altar in its southwestern corner (*Sanamahi Kachin*), where daily prayers and food offerings are presented to Sanamahi and Leimarel.
- *Pakhangba*: The younger divine brother, envisioned as an omniscient ancestral monarch and divine dragon-serpent (*Paphal*), who rules over the political realm and sovereign governance.
- *Umang Lai*: Hundreds of sacred sylvan deities inhabiting ancient sacred forest groves (*Umang Lai Khubam*) throughout the valley, representing clan ancestors and regional protectors.

The pinnacle of Sanamahi communal liturgical life is the *Lai Haraoba* ("merry-making of the gods"), an elaborate multi-day ritual festival celebrated annually at village shrines. The festival is directed by *Maibis* (spiritual priestesses/shamans who experience ecstatic trances and voice the prophecies of the gods) and *Maibas* (ritual priests, scholars, and healers). Through intricate, choreographed dances (*Jagoi*), the Maibis ritually re-enact the entire Meitei cosmogony: the emergence of the cosmos from primordial waters, the building of the earth, the creation of human anatomy, the development of agriculture and weaving, and the cyclical renewal of life.
