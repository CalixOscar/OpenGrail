# West African Vodun

## Historical context

Vodun traditions are practiced especially in Benin, Togo, Ghana, and adjacent regions among communities with distinct languages and political histories. Royal institutions, family shrines, markets, healing lineages, colonial intervention, Christian and Muslim contact, and modern state recognition have all shaped them. “Vodun” does not name one standardized pantheon or a fossilized survival outside modern life.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Devotees cultivate reciprocal relationships with particular *vodun*, ancestors, and community powers through initiation, offering, possession, divination, music, and festival. Authority is distributed among temples, priestly lineages, families, and local traditions. Haitian Vodou preserves important Fon- and Ewe-related inheritances but developed under Atlantic slavery in dialogue with Kongo, Yorùbá, Catholic, Indigenous Caribbean, and other sources; the names should not be treated as synonyms.
