# African Indigenous Traditions (Comparative)

## Historical context

Africa contains extraordinary religious diversity shaped by language families, polities, migrations, ecologies, and exchange with Christianity, Islam, and other traditions. Scholars once placed this diversity under evolutionary labels such as “primitive religion”; contemporary work instead prioritizes named peoples, institutions, and historical change. Many communities maintain Indigenous practices while also identifying with a world religion.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Ancestors, divination, healing, sacred authority, land, moral personhood, and relations with more-than-human beings recur, but never form one African creed. This node is explicitly an analytical map layer. Its placement under an animist framework signals one comparative vocabulary and remains contestable; it does not assert that communities self-identify as “animist” or share one origin.
