# Lucumí / Regla de Ocha

## Historical context

Lucumí, also widely called Santería or Regla de Ocha, took shape in colonial and postcolonial Cuba among Yorùbá-speaking captives and descendants, in interaction with other African nations, Catholic institutions, Spiritism, and Cuban social life. Cabildos, ritual houses, kinship, migration, and later transnational networks sustained distinct lineages. Many practitioners prefer Lucumí or Ocha because “Santería” began as an outsider label.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Initiation, divination, drumming, sacrifice, spirit possession, and reciprocal service to the *oricha* organize practice. Catholic saints may correspond to oricha in historically layered ways, but the beings are not simply identical. Lucumí retains Yorùbá language and concepts while possessing its own Cuban canon of ritual precedent. It should be compared with, not collapsed into, Brazilian Candomblé or contemporary Yorùbá religion.
