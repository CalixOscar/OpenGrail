# Kemetism

## Historical context

Kemetism includes modern movements that revive or reconstruct religions of ancient Egypt, with organized communities developing especially from the 1970s onward. Practitioners use Egyptology, translated texts, museum collections, personal devotion, and new ritual composition. Kemetic Orthodoxy, independent reconstructionists, and groups linking Egypt to African diaspora identity differ significantly in authority, theology, and cultural framing.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Devotion to Netjeru, offerings, shrine practice, ancestor reverence, and ethical commitment to *ma'at* commonly appear. Modern life lacks the ancient temple economy and divine kingship, so all revival involves selection and adaptation. The branch relation denotes intentional historical reception, not unbroken priestly succession. Responsible practice also navigates colonial histories of Egyptology and debates over Egypt's African and Mediterranean identities.
