# Trinidad Orisha

## Historical context

Yorùbá-speaking migrants and formerly enslaved communities sustained Orisha worship in nineteenth-century Trinidad under colonial restriction. The tradition interacted with Catholicism, Protestantism, Spiritual Baptism, and the island’s wider plural society. Twentieth-century practitioners pursued public recognition and varying forms of Africanization, including renewed ties with West African teachers and vocabulary.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Shrines honor multiple Orisha through drumming, song, offering, divination, healing, and possession. “Shango” historically became a broad popular name, but contemporary communities may prefer Orisha. The tradition is neither a frozen Yorùbá survival nor a random mixture: it is a coherent Caribbean religious history with internal lineages and active debate about heritage.
