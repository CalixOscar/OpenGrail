# Palo

## Historical context

Palo formed in colonial Cuba among Central African-descended communities who recreated Kongo ritual knowledge under slavery and urban association. Branches commonly named Mayombe, Briyumba, and Kimbisa developed different liturgies and relationships with Catholicism, Spiritism, and other Afro-Cuban religions. Migration carried houses beyond Cuba while initiation retained lineage authority.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Ritual centers on an empowered vessel often called *nganga* or *prenda*, assembled through lineage-specific knowledge to mediate spirits, protection, healing, and justice. Songs preserve Central African language and cosmology in transformed form. Sensational labels such as “black magic” erase ethics, community, and religious discipline; protected initiatory details should not be reconstructed from outsider curiosity.
