# Winti

## Historical context

Winti is the traditional Afro-Surinamese religion forged during the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries in the plantation colony of Suriname (then Dutch Guiana). It was created through the synthesis of West and Central African religious traditions brought by enslaved peoples—primarily Akan, Fon, Ewe, Yoruba, and Kongo—intermingling with indigenous Amazonian (Carib and Arawak) plant lore. The religion developed along two interconnected paths: among the urban and plantation enslaved population (Creoles) and within the independent Maroon (*Bushinengue*) communities (such as the Ndyuka, Saramaka, and Matawai) who escaped into the interior rainforests.

Under Dutch colonial rule, Winti was strictly outlawed in 1874 and subjected to intense Christian missionary demonization. Practitioners maintained their rites clandestinely for generations until legal prohibition was officially repealed in 1971. Today, Winti is recognized as an integral component of Surinamese national heritage and is actively practiced across Suriname as well as within the significant Surinamese diaspora in the Netherlands.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Winti cosmology begins with *Anana Keduaman Keduampon*, the transcendent, unknowable supreme creator God who does not intervene directly in daily human affairs. Instead, cosmic power is mediated through *Winti*—divine nature spirits and intermediary beings organized into four primary elemental pantheons (*futu*):
- *Tapu Winti* (Sky Pantheon): High spiritual entities such as *Tata Aisa* and celestial powers.
- *Gron Winti* (Earth Pantheon): Deities presiding over the land, agriculture, and fertility, foremost among them *Mama Aisa*, the beloved mother earth goddess who provides stability and nurturance.
- *Wata Winti* (Water Pantheon): Spirits of rivers, creeks, and oceans, including *Wata Mama* and *Wata Ingi* (often manifesting through indigenous riverine imagery).
- *Busi Winti* (Forest Pantheon): Powerful spirits of the deep jungle, including *Kromanti* (fierce warrior and healing spirits of Akan heritage) and *Ampuku* (forest trickster and guardian spirits).

In addition to the Winti, the cosmos includes *Jorka* (spirits of deceased human ancestors) and *Kabu* (venerable founding ancestors of a family clan). Human anthropology in Winti posits that every individual possesses a *Kra* (the divine spark or inner spiritual soul received from God) and a *Dyodyo* (spiritual parents/guardians, typically a male and female Winti that protect the person).

Ritual life centers on the *Winti Prei*, an all-night community festival where polyrhythmic drumming, clapping, and singing in ancestral languages induce spirit possession. Initiated priests and healers (*duman* and *lukuman*) guide trances, diagnose spiritual imbalances, prepare sacred herbal baths (*was-was*), and perform reconciliations (*pasi*) between individuals and their ancestral spirits.
