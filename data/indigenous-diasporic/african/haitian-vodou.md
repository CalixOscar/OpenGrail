# Haitian Vodou

## Historical context

Haitian Vodou formed in Saint-Domingue and Haiti as enslaved and free Africans, their descendants, and local communities rebuilt religious life under brutal colonial conditions. Fon/Ewe, Kongo, Yorùbá, and other inheritances entered new *nanchon* and house lineages alongside Catholic images, prayers, and calendars. The religion has remained adaptive through revolution, migration, legal suppression, and transnational community.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Service to the *lwa* includes song, drumming, dance, offering, possession, healing, initiation, and care for ancestors. Houses and lineages differ; there is no universal catechism. Catholic correspondences are lived translations, not evidence that Vodou is disguised Christianity. Popular media depictions of zombies, dolls, or “black magic” erase ordinary devotion and reproduce racialized colonial stereotypes.
