# Umbanda

## Historical context

Umbanda emerged publicly in early twentieth-century Brazil from a wider field of Afro-Brazilian temples, Kardecist Spiritism, Catholic devotion, Indigenous symbolism, healing, and urban change. Narratives of a single 1908 founding are important to some communities but do not explain the movement's multiple roots. Umbanda houses range from strongly African-oriented to esoteric or Spiritist forms.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Mediums incorporate spirit lines often named as *caboclos*, *pretos velhos*, children, or other social figures, offering counsel and healing through song, smoke, gesture, and prayer. These figures carry complex Brazilian histories and should not be read as literal portraits of all Indigenous or African-descended people. Umbanda overlaps with Candomblé without being its simplified version, and each house defines cosmology and ethics differently.
