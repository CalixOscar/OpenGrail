# African Diasporic Traditions (Comparative)

## Historical context

Enslaved and free African-descended people in the Americas and Atlantic world rebuilt religious life under violent displacement. Yorùbá, Gbe, Kongo, Akan, and other inheritances interacted with Indigenous American traditions, Catholic and Protestant Christianity, Spiritism, Islam, and new social conditions. The resulting traditions are creative historical formations, not damaged copies of an African original.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Initiation, possession, divination, healing, ancestors, sacred music, and reciprocal relations with divine powers recur in different combinations. Some traditions are organized religions; others, including hoodoo, may be better described as practice complexes. This umbrella is comparative only and should never replace names such as Vodou, Lucumí, Palo, Candomblé, Winti, or Orisha.
