# Hopi Religion

## Historical context

Hopi villages maintain distinct clan histories, ceremonial societies, agricultural knowledge, and responsibilities rooted in long residence on and around the Hopi mesas. Spanish missions, United States rule, schooling, land disputes, tourism, and media intrusion have affected religious life. Much knowledge is restricted by community authority, so publicly available ethnography does not license outsiders to reproduce ceremonies or secret narratives.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Dry farming, rain, emergence histories, katsinam, pilgrimage, reciprocity, and village ceremonial cycles connect ethical life with place. Practice differs among villages and societies; “Hopi religion” is not a single public creed. New Age prophecy literature frequently detaches Hopi teachings from custodianship and sometimes invents quotations. Comparative study should honor Hopi decisions about what may be shared and avoid turning living responsibilities into generic environmental symbolism.
