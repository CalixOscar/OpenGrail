# Maya Religions

## Historical context

Maya religious histories span more than three millennia across present-day Mexico, Guatemala, Belize, Honduras, and El Salvador. Hundreds of communities speak distinct Mayan languages and experienced different city-states, colonial regimes, churches, wars, and revitalization movements. Classic inscriptions and archaeology illuminate royal ritual; colonial texts and living practices reveal other worlds, but no source represents every Maya people.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Calendrical knowledge, maize cultivation, caves and mountains, ancestors, offerings, divination, healing, cofradías, and reciprocal relations with nonhuman powers appear in locally specific forms. The K'iche' *Popol Wuj* is a profound community text, not a universal “Maya Bible.” Christian devotion and Maya ritual often coexist through centuries of negotiation. Ancient collapse narratives must not imply that Maya peoples vanished; vibrant communities continue to defend language, land, and ceremony.
