# Guaraní Religious Traditions

## Historical context

Guaraní-speaking peoples inhabit regions now divided among Paraguay, Brazil, Argentina, and Bolivia and include distinct communities such as Mbyá, Kaiowá, Ñandeva, and Ava. Jesuit missions, enslavement, settler expansion, deforestation, and national borders transformed land and ritual life. Christianity was variously adopted, translated, resisted, or kept separate.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Sacred speech, song, naming, prayer houses, dreams, cultivation, and journeys toward good or imperishable land are important in several traditions. Published collections such as *Ayvu Rapyta* document particular Mbyá teachings, not a universal Guaraní scripture. Present-day land defense is inseparable from religious continuity because forests, paths, ancestors, and divine relations are located rather than abstract.
