# Land Without Evil (Yvy Marãne'ỹ)

## Historical context

The concept of *Yvy Marãne'ỹ* (the "Land Without Evil" in Guaraní, or *Terra sem Mal* in Portuguese) represents one of the most distinctive and extensively documented religious and prophetic traditions of lowland South America. Rooted in pre-Columbian Tupi and Guaraní cosmology, it motivated sustained, multi-generational migrations led by inspired shamans (*karaí* and *paje*) across thousands of kilometers of the Amazon and Paraná-Paraguay river basins, moving toward the Atlantic coast or the Andean foothills in search of an immortal earthly sanctuary.

Historical chroniclers from the sixteenth century onward—such as Hans Staden, Jean de Léry, and Jesuit missionaries—recorded massive movements of Tupi-Guarani populations abandoning their villages under the direction of charismatic prophets who proclaimed the imminent destruction of the corrupt earth and the discovery of a terrestrial paradise beyond the sea. Anthropologist Curt Nimuendajú documented the continuation of these sacred migrations into the early twentieth century among the Apapocuva-Guaraní. Far from an extinct historical relic, *Yvy Marãne'ỹ* remains an active theological and spiritual pillar of contemporary Guaraní (Mbyá, Kaiowá, and Ñandeva) cultural identity, self-determination, and territorial defense.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

In classical Tupi-Guarani theology, the supreme deity Ñande Ru Tenonde ("Our Father the First One") created the earth as an imperfect, temporary realm that is periodically destroyed and renewed by cosmic fire or deluge (*Yvy rupa*). To escape this cycle, human beings can achieve *aguyje*—a state of spiritual lightness, perfection, and bodily immortality—not through physical death, but by purifying the body and spirit during life.

Attaining *aguyje* and discovering the Land Without Evil requires rigorous spiritual disciplines centered on the *opy* (sacred prayer house):
- Sustained collective dancing (*jeroky*) and ritual recitation of sacred poetic words (*ñe'ẽ porã*) accompanied by the *mbaraka* (sacred gourd rattle) and *takuapu* (rhythmic bamboo stamping tube played by women).
- Strict dietary and behavioral taboos (*mba'e ky'a*) to remove mortal heaviness (*pohyikue*) and prevent spiritual contamination.
- Guidance by shamans who interpret prophetic dreams (*kera*) and visionary journeys to celestial realms.

In the Land Without Evil, crops grow without human labor, game approaches hunters voluntarily, age and physical decay are abolished, and departed ancestors dance eternally alongside divine beings. In the contemporary era, indigenous scholars and community leaders emphasize that *Yvy Marãne'ỹ* functions simultaneously as a mystical horizon and an urgent ethical imperative for ecological preservation, communal solidarity, and resistance to environmental destruction in ancestral lands.
