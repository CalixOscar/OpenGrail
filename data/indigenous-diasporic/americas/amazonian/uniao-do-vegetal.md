# União do Vegetal

## Historical context

The Centro Espírita Beneficente União do Vegetal (UDV) was founded on July 22, 1961, by José Gabriel da Costa (known as Mestre Gabriel, 1922–1971) in the Amazon rainforest near the border between Brazil and Bolivia, in what was then the Federal Territory of Guaporé (now Rondônia). Gabriel, a rubber tapper and former police officer originally from Bahia with a background in regional Afro-Brazilian traditions, first encountered the sacred tea prepared from the *mariri* (*Banisteriopsis caapi*) vine and *chacrona* (*Psychotria viridis*) leaves in 1959 from an indigenous healer.

Recognizing the tea as a sacred medium for spiritual revelation, Mestre Gabriel organized a formal spiritual society in Porto Velho, defining its mission as the union of the vegetal kingdom with human consciousness to promote peace and human fraternal solidarity. Under Gabriel's leadership, the UDV established a structured institutional hierarchy, a clear ethical code, and standardized ritual practices. Following Gabriel's death in 1971, the UDV expanded steadily from Rondônia across all Brazilian states, eventually establishing branches throughout North America and Europe.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

In contrast to the dynamic, musical, all-night dancing (*bailado*) characterizing Santo Daime, the UDV conducts seated, introspective ritual sessions (*Sessões*) lasting approximately four hours. Members wear green shirts with formal insignias and sit quietly in rows of chairs within consecrated temples (*Núcleos*). The ritual space is presided over by a *Mestre*, who directs the flow of the session through spoken instruction, question-and-answer dialogues, recorded musical selections, and the chanting of *Chamadas*—sacred rhythmic calls that invoke spiritual forces, tell the mythical origins of the tea, and harmonize the group's collective concentration (*burracheira*).

The UDV presents a distinctive cosmological system transmitted primarily through oral tradition. It posits that the tea (referred to reverently as *Hoasca* or *Vegetal*) does not produce illusions or delirium, but rather clears the senses to allow the *Luz da Consciência* (Light of Consciousness) and *Concentração Mental* (Mental Concentration) to operate, revealing the true nature of God, the soul, and reincarnation. Doctrinally, the UDV emphasizes strong civic responsibility, institutional legality, marital fidelity, family harmony, and abstention from alcohol and tobacco.

The UDV has played a prominent role in establishing international legal protections for the religious use of entheogenic sacraments. In Brazil, it actively participated in governmental working groups that culminated in the 2004 and 2010 CONAD resolutions protecting sacramental ayahuasca use. In the United States, after federal authorities seized shipments of Hoasca in 1999, the UDV litigated a landmark religious freedom case that reached the Supreme Court. In *Gonzales v. O Centro Espírita Beneficente União do Vegetal* (2006), the Supreme Court ruled unanimously (8–0) that under the Religious Freedom Restoration Act (RFRA), the federal government failed to demonstrate a compelling interest in prohibiting the church's sacramental use of Hoasca.
