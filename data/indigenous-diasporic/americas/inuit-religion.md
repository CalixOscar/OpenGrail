# Inuit Religions

## Historical context

Inuit communities extend across Kalaallit Nunaat, Canada, Alaska, and Chukotka, with distinct languages, colonial histories, and local practices. Mission Christianity, forced settlement, residential schooling, relocations, wage economies, and climate disruption have transformed religious life. Early explorers and ethnographers often generalized from one region, so “Inuit religion” must remain a plural comparative category.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Hunting ethics, naming, human-animal reciprocity, weather, sea powers, helping spirits, storytelling, and the work of ritual specialists appeared in varying forms. Terms such as *angakkuq* belong to specific linguistic contexts and are not universal equivalents of a global “shaman.” Many Inuit communities are strongly Christian while preserving inherited values and practices. Popular fascination with taboos or sea-woman narratives should not eclipse contemporary sovereignty and knowledge governance.
