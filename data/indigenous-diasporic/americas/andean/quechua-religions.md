# Quechua Religious Traditions

## Historical context

Quechua became widespread through pre-Inca exchange, Inca administration, colonial mission policy, and later national histories. Communities across Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador, Argentina, and migration settings possess distinct local traditions rather than one surviving imperial religion. Colonial extirpation, Catholic confraternities, haciendas, mining, agrarian reform, and tourism all reshaped sacred landscapes.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Reciprocity with *Pachamama*, mountain beings often called *apus*, water, crops, herd animals, ancestors, saints, and the dead may organize offerings and festivals. Terms and beings vary substantially, and Christian identification is often integral rather than superficial disguise. Popular “Inca spirituality” marketed to outsiders can detach practice from Quechua communities, land rights, and ritual specialists.
