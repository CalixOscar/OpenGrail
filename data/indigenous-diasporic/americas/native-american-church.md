# Native American Church

## Historical context

The Native American Church developed in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries as peyote ceremonial traditions moved through Indigenous kin and travel networks from northern Mexico and the southern Plains. Formal incorporation helped communities defend sacramental use against prohibition. Nations and chapters maintain distinct protocols, and federal legal categories do not make the church a single pan-Indigenous institution.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

An all-night meeting may center prayer, peyote, water, fire, song, drum, cedar, testimony, and communal care under trained officers. Some lineages use explicitly Christian language, while others frame peyote through different Indigenous teachings. The syncretic link records historical interaction, not Christian ownership of the ceremony. Peyote's conservation and the sovereignty of cultivating communities are important ethical issues for outsiders interested in psychedelic practice.
