# Muscogee Ceremonial Traditions

## Historical context

Muscogee or Mvskoke peoples historically organized through towns and confederated political relationships across the Southeast. United States warfare and forced removal relocated many communities to Indian Territory, while others remained in the Southeast. Ceremonial grounds survived suppression and continue through distinct leadership, calendars, and protocols alongside Christian churches and tribal governments.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The annual Busk or Green Corn cycle renews sacred fire, community relationships, ethical order, and connection to land through fasting, medicine, dance, and deliberation. Each ground controls its own knowledge and participation. “Creek religion” can wrongly suggest one centralized system; the living reality is a network of sovereign ceremonial communities with related but specific traditions.
