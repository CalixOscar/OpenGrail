# Mexicayotl

## Historical context

Mexicayotl emerged through twentieth-century Mexican movements seeking to renew Indigenous identity, language, dance, calendar, and spirituality after centuries of colonial suppression and state mestizaje. Figures such as Rodolfo Nieva López and later cultural groups helped articulate organized forms, but participants differ in lineage claims, politics, and relations with living Nahua communities.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Ceremonial dance, Nahuatl names, calendrical observance, offerings, and devotion to Mexica deities may express decolonial identity as well as religion. Reconstruction uses colonial manuscripts and archaeology, sources that require critical interpretation. The branch edge records an explicit modern reception of Mexica traditions, not uninterrupted institutional survival. Scholars and practitioners also debate appropriation when urban or non-Nahua participants claim authority over Indigenous heritage.
