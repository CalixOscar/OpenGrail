# Diné Religion

## Historical context

Diné religious life is grounded in homelands of the Southwest and shaped by clan relationships, pastoralism, colonial violence, the Long Walk, resource extraction, and continuing sovereignty. Ceremonial knowledge is transmitted through families and trained specialists rather than a universal written canon. Some narratives and sandpaintings are context-bound or restricted; academic description should not convert them into freely reproducible content.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The aspiration toward *hózhó*—often translated through beauty, balance, order, and right relationship—guides healing and ethical practice without reducing to simple “harmony.” Blessingway and other ceremonial complexes address particular conditions through prayer, song, offering, and community participation. Diné people may also practice Christianity, peyote religion, or other traditions. Pan-Indigenous labels obscure Diné language, law, land, and ceremonial authority.
