# Mexica and Nahua Religions

## Historical context

The label “Aztec religion” often centers the Mexica of Tenochtitlan, but central Mexico contained many Nahua-speaking altepetl with their own temples, patron powers, and histories. Knowledge comes from archaeology, painted books, Nahuatl-language colonial texts, and Spanish accounts produced amid conquest and evangelization. These sources preserve Indigenous testimony while also reflecting coercion, translation, and elite viewpoints.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Calendrical rites, agriculture, warfare, pilgrimage, household offerings, divine embodiment, and reciprocal nourishment ordered relations among humans, gods, and cosmos. Huitzilopochtli, Tlaloc, Tezcatlipoca, and many other powers mattered differently across settings. Human sacrifice was real and politically significant, but sensational focus on it can erase philosophy, poetry, ordinary devotion, and colonial violence. Contemporary Nahua communities and Mexicayotl revivals are not relics of an extinct civilization.
