# Zapotec Religious Traditions

## Historical context

Zapotec-speaking peoples built long-lived urban and regional traditions in Oaxaca, including Monte Albán, while never forming one timeless culture. Spanish invasion and missionization attacked temples and calendrical specialists, yet Zapotec-language documents and community practice preserved transformed knowledge. Contemporary towns maintain distinct ritual calendars, offices, saints, lands, and migration histories.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Rain, maize, caves, mountains, ancestors, naming calendars, household offerings, communal service, and Catholic festivals interrelate differently by region. Archaeological gods cannot automatically be equated with contemporary beings or saints. “Zapotec religion” is a linguistic and historical umbrella, and community-controlled ritual knowledge should not be generalized from one valley to the Isthmus or Sierra.
