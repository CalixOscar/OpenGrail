# Lakota Religion

## Historical context

Lakota religious traditions belong within the wider Oceti Sakowin while retaining community, band, family, and ceremonial distinctions. United States warfare, reservation confinement, boarding schools, bans on ceremony, and loss of the Black Hills inflicted sustained religious violence. Elders and families nevertheless maintained and renewed knowledge. Published accounts, including famous mediated biographies, never replace community authority.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Mitákuye Oyás'i expresses expansive kinship and obligation rather than a detachable slogan. Prayer, pipe traditions, vision seeking, Sun Dance, sacred songs, and relationships with land and beings require proper preparation and sponsorship. Many Lakota are also Christian or Native American Church members. Commercial “medicine men” and pan-Indian workshops often appropriate ceremonies; careful comparison distinguishes public description from permission to perform.
