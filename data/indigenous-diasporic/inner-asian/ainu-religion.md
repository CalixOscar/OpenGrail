# Ainu Religious Traditions

## Historical context

Ainu communities in Hokkaido, Sakhalin, and the Kuril region developed religious life through hunting, fishing, household ritual, oral epic, and relations with *kamuy*. Japanese colonization, dispossession, assimilation policy, and suppression of language and ceremony severely disrupted transmission. Cultural and religious revitalization continues under Ainu leadership.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Kamuy are powerful other-than-human beings encountered through animals, fire, tools, disease, weather, and other forms. Offerings and carefully ordered ceremonies sustain reciprocity; the famous bear-sending rite should not be isolated as a spectacle from its ethical world. “Animism” can indicate relational features but is not an Ainu self-sufficient creed, and comparison with Shinto must not erase colonial difference.
