# So-Called Cargo Cult Movements

## Historical context

Anthropologists and colonial officials applied “cargo cult” to many unrelated prophetic, political, and ritual movements in Melanesia and nearby regions. These arose amid missionization, labor recruitment, racial hierarchy, war, and radically unequal access to wealth. Grouping them by an alleged desire for Western goods often made Indigenous theories of justice and sovereignty appear irrational while treating colonial extraction as normal.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Particular movements used Christian prophecy, ancestors, kastom, new ritual, military imagery, or cooperative organization in different combinations. Some expected material transformation, but “cargo” was never their single concern. The node is minority-tier because scholars increasingly criticize the category itself. Users should navigate to named communities such as John Frum and interpret each movement through its own language, leaders, and political history.
