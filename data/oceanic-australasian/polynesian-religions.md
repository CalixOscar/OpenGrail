# Polynesian Religions

## Historical context

Polynesian-speaking navigators settled islands across a vast Pacific region over many centuries, developing related languages and highly specific political, ecological, and religious systems. Tonga, Sāmoa, Tahiti, Rapa Nui, Aotearoa, Hawaiʻi, and other communities cannot be represented by one pantheon. Mission Christianity, colonial government, migration, tourism, and cultural revitalization reshaped each history differently.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Genealogy, chiefly authority, ocean and land, ancestors, sacred restriction, gift exchange, ritual expertise, and powers described through terms such as *mana* and *tapu* recur with locally different meanings. English borrowed and distorted some of those words. The umbrella exists for careful linguistic and historical comparison, not to make island traditions interchangeable. Community control over chants, names, and sacred knowledge should guide what outsiders reproduce.
