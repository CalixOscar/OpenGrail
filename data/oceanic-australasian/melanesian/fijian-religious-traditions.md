# iTaukei Fijian Religious Traditions

## Historical context

Fiji’s islands developed varied chiefdoms and ritual systems through local histories and exchange with Tonga and other Pacific societies. Nineteenth-century warfare, Methodist conversion, colonial government, and the formation of modern chiefly institutions transformed religious authority. Christianity is now integral to much iTaukei communal life, while ancestral and *vanua* responsibilities continue in changing forms.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The *vanua* joins land, people, chiefly order, ancestors, and collective obligation rather than naming land as a detachable commodity. Ceremonial exchange, yaqona, *meke*, funerals, and church life can all enact sacred relationship. “Melanesian” is a geographic-comparative placement; Fiji also has strong Polynesian connections and should not be forced into a single cultural block.
