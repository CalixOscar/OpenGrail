# John Frum Movement

## Historical context

The John Frum movement arose on Tanna under the Anglo-French colonial regime, mission pressure, plantation labor, and debates over local governance. Prophecies and public organization became especially visible around the Second World War and the arrival of American forces. Movement communities have pursued kastom, political autonomy, moral order, and expected transformation through changing historical circumstances.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Friday observance, songs, marching, village organization, volcanic and ancestral landscapes, and narratives of John Frum vary among adherents. Outsiders often reduce the movement to naïve expectation of manufactured “cargo,” ignoring its anticolonial analysis and enduring institutions. The branch relation uses a familiar scholarly category for navigation, while the parent node explains why “cargo cult” is contested and cannot exhaust Tannese meanings.
