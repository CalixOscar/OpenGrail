# Melanesian Religions

## Historical context

“Melanesia” is a colonial-era regional label covering extraordinary linguistic and cultural diversity in New Guinea, the Bismarcks, Solomon Islands, Vanuatu, New Caledonia, and Fiji. Communities developed different exchange systems, initiation, leadership, art, kinship, and relations with land and sea. Missionization and colonial labor regimes produced new Christian and Indigenous movements, not a single trajectory from “traditional” to modern.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Ancestors, spirits, gardening, exchange, secrecy, initiation, healing, and ceremonial houses matter in some communities but never define all. Classic anthropology often converted local concepts into broad theories of mana, magic, or “cargo cults.” Useful comparison begins with named peoples and languages, then traces actual contact. Restricted ritual knowledge and community-owned designs should not be treated as anonymous regional folklore.
