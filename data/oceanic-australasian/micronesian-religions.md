# Micronesian Religions

## Historical context

Micronesia includes the Marianas, Carolines, Marshalls, Kiribati, Nauru, and other island groups with different languages, settlement histories, and political systems. Long-distance navigation joined islands without making their religions uniform. Spanish, German, Japanese, and American empires, mission churches, warfare, nuclear testing, displacement, and migration have shaped contemporary religious life in sharply unequal ways.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Genealogy, chiefly or clan authority, ocean knowledge, ancestors, breadfruit and taro cycles, sacred places, and specialist healing appear in locally specific forms. Navigational knowledge can carry ethical and cosmological significance beyond technical wayfinding. Most communities are now Christian, often through thoroughly local church traditions. A regional graph node is useful only if users continue toward particular Chamorro, Carolinian, Marshallese, i-Kiribati, and other histories.
