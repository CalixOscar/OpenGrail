# Ancient Egyptian Religion

## Historical context

Ancient Egyptian religious traditions developed over more than three millennia, from early kingdoms through Ptolemaic and Roman rule. Temples maintained divine images and estates, kingship enacted cosmic order, and households honored protective and ancestral powers. Texts now called funerary “books” were changing ritual collections rather than one fixed Bible, and access varied sharply by period and social position.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Maintaining *ma'at*—truth, justice, and ordered relation—joined ritual, ethics, and political legitimacy. Ra, Osiris, Isis, Amun, Hathor, Horus, and many local gods could combine without requiring a single systematic theology. Mortuary transformation and divine reciprocity were central but not the whole religion. Later Isis worship and modern Kemetism creatively receive Egyptian materials in very different historical contexts.
