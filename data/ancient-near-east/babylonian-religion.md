# Babylonian Religion

## Historical context

Babylonian religion grew from the multilingual religious worlds of southern Mesopotamia during the second and first millennia BCE. Babylon's political prominence elevated its city god Marduk, especially in the *Enūma Eliš*, but older cult centers and gods retained authority. Temple households, learned specialists, royal patronage, neighborhood shrines, and family rites all contributed to religious practice across changing dynasties.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Festival processions, divination, exorcistic medicine, prayer, sacrifice, and maintenance of cult statues linked human society with a vulnerable cosmic order. Extensive lexical and ritual scholarship preserved Sumerian materials within Akkadian learning. The branch relation marks historical development inside Mesopotamian tradition, not a moment of institutional founding. Biblical and Greek comparisons can illuminate contact zones, but each proposed borrowing must be demonstrated textually and chronologically.
