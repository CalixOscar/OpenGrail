# Hittite Religion

## Historical context

Hittite archives from Hattusa preserve thousands of ritual, festival, mythic, and diplomatic tablets from second-millennium BCE Anatolia. Court scribes worked in Hittite, Hattic, Hurrian, Luwian, Akkadian, and other languages. The famous phrase “the thousand gods of Hatti” reflects deliberate incorporation of local and foreign cults rather than a single stable pantheon imposed throughout the kingdom.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Seasonal festivals, oath gods, royal ancestor rites, purification, sacrifice, and careful maintenance of divine images tied ritual accuracy to political welfare. Hurrian deities and narratives became especially influential at court, while older Anatolian elements persisted. The influence edge denotes documented translation and cult transfer. Comparisons between the Kumarbi cycle and Greek succession myths are valuable, although transmission routes and intermediate traditions remain debated.
