# Cult of Enlil at Nippur

## Historical setting

Nippur was never consistently a great imperial capital, yet its temple Ekur made the city a pan-Mesopotamian religious center. Sumerian and Akkadian rulers repaired the sanctuary, dedicated offerings, and sought Enlil’s authorization as a sign of legitimate kingship. Administrative tablets, building inscriptions, hymns, and school texts document a cult that changed across more than two millennia.

## Scope and interpretation

Enlil was associated with sovereign command, wind, and the ordering of the divine assembly, but ancient divine roles were not fixed doctrinal portfolios. This node represents a temple institution and its ritual network, not a separate denomination whose adherents worshiped only Enlil. Nippur’s prestige joined otherwise competing city-states through a shared sacred geography.
