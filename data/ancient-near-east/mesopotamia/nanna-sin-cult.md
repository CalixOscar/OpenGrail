# Cult of Nanna–Sîn

## Historical setting

The moon god Nanna, Akkadian Sîn, was the patron of Ur, where the Ekišnugal temple and its personnel held major economic and ceremonial roles. His cult also became deeply rooted at Harran in upper Mesopotamia. Lunar observation connected the god with calendrical order, divination, fertility, and royal legitimacy across Sumerian, Babylonian, Assyrian, and later imperial settings.

## Scope and interpretation

Ur and Harran were related centers but not identical institutions, and the cult’s practices changed substantially over time. The divine family linking Sîn with Ningal, Shamash, and Ishtar supplied a language for astral order. Claims of direct survival in much later communities are debated and require more than a shared lunar symbol or a medieval report.
