# Cult of Inanna–Ishtar

## Historical setting

Inanna was central to the Eanna precinct at Uruk by the late fourth millennium BCE. Akkadian-language traditions increasingly identified her with Ishtar, whose cult flourished at cities including Akkad, Nineveh, and Arbela. Hymns, myths, royal inscriptions, votive objects, and ritual texts show changing relationships between the goddess, kingship, war, sexuality, lament, and astral symbolism.

## Scope and interpretation

Inanna and Ishtar were substantially identified, yet local manifestations could retain different titles, rituals, and political meanings. This node maps a distributed cult complex within Mesopotamian religion rather than a freestanding faith. Modern claims that the cult had one uniform sexual institution or a single transhistorical gender doctrine exceed the uneven ancient evidence.
