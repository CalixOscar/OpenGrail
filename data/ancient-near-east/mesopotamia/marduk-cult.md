# Cult of Marduk

## Historical setting

Marduk rose with Babylon’s political fortunes, especially from the Old Babylonian period onward. Priests and scholars increasingly assigned him powers formerly associated with other great gods. In the creation epic *Enūma Eliš*, Marduk defeats Tiamat and receives supreme names; the New Year Akītu festival linked his temple Esagila, kingship, and the renewal of civic order.

## Scope and interpretation

Marduk’s elevation did not turn Babylonian religion into exclusive monotheism. Other gods retained temples, agency, and worship, while theological texts could concentrate divine attributes in different ways. The cult illustrates how political centrality, scribal reinterpretation, and ritual performance could reorganize a pantheon without abolishing its plurality.
