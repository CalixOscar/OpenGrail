# Cult of Amun at Thebes

## Historical setting

Amun rose from a Theban god to national prominence as Theban rulers reunified Egypt and founded the New Kingdom. Joined with the solar god Re as Amun-Re, he received monumental temples at Karnak and Luxor, extensive estates, priesthoods, and festival processions. His oracle and clergy remained influential even as political centers changed.

## Scope and interpretation

The cult’s theology could describe Amun as hidden, transcendent, and manifest through other gods, but this did not abolish Egyptian polytheistic practice. “Amunism” is therefore misleading if it implies a separate confessional religion. The institution combined local sacred geography, solar theology, kingship, economic administration, and popular petition.
