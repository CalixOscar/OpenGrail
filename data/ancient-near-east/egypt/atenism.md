# Atenism

## Historical setting

Pharaoh Akhenaten redirected royal patronage toward the visible solar disk Aten, founded a new capital at Akhetaten, altered divine imagery, and attacked the names and institutions of Amun and some other gods. Access to Aten was represented especially through the royal family. Soon after Akhenaten’s death, successors restored older temples and abandoned the capital.

## Scope and interpretation

Scholars debate whether Atenism should be called monotheism, monolatry, royal henotheism, or an exceptional centralization of cult. Evidence mostly reflects official monuments rather than ordinary belief, and the movement’s duration was brief. Popular claims that it directly generated Israelite monotheism remain speculative and cannot be established from chronological resemblance alone.
