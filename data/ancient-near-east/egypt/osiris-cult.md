# Cult of Osiris

## Historical setting

Osiris appears in Old Kingdom mortuary texts and became closely associated with Abydos, burial, judgment, agricultural renewal, and legitimate succession through Horus. Royal afterlife language gradually became available more broadly in funerary texts and practice. Festivals at Abydos ritually narrated conflict, death, and restoration, drawing pilgrims and dedications from across Egypt.

## Scope and interpretation

The cult did not promise resurrection in precisely the later Christian sense; Egyptian afterlife concepts joined bodily preservation, transformed existence, solar cycles, social status, and divine identification. Isis, Nephthys, Horus, and Seth were integral to Osirian narratives. Later Mediterranean cults adapted these figures, but Egyptian and Greco-Roman settings should not be collapsed.
