# Hurrian Religion

## Historical context

Hurrian-speaking peoples lived across a wide zone from northern Mesopotamia to Syria and Anatolia during the third and second millennia BCE. Their traditions are reconstructed from archives at Hattusa, Ugarit, Nuzi, and other sites, often through texts copied by non-Hurrian scribes. Political formations such as Mittani mattered, but no single Hurrian state or priesthood defined all practice.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The storm god Teššub, Ḫebat, Šauška, Kumarbi, and deities grouped as ancient “former gods” feature in surviving myth and ritual. Offerings, purification, music, divination, and oath-making traveled through multilingual courts. Hurrian compositions shaped Hittite archives and may stand within longer routes toward Greek myth, but missing intermediaries caution against simple one-to-one descent. Local Syrian and Anatolian traditions also transformed what they received.
