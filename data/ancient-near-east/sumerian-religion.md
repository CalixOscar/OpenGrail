# Sumerian Religion

## Historical context

Sumerian-speaking communities of southern Mesopotamia developed temple institutions by the fourth millennium BCE. Cities such as Uruk, Ur, Nippur, Eridu, and Lagash honored overlapping but locally ordered pantheons. Evidence comes from temples, offerings, administrative tablets, hymns, laments, myths, and later scribal copies; it does not reveal a single creed shared unchanged across every city or period.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Divine authority, civic prosperity, irrigation, kingship, and ritual labor were closely connected. Deities including An, Enlil, Enki, Inanna, Nanna, and Utu occupied changing constellations of power. Sumerian compositions profoundly shaped later Akkadian-language Mesopotamian literature, yet “Sumerian religion” is a scholarly umbrella. Similar flood or creation motifs elsewhere require contextual comparison and do not by themselves prove direct borrowing.
