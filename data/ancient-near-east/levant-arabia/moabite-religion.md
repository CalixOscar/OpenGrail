# Moabite Religion

## Historical setting

Moabite religion is known through archaeology, neighboring literary witnesses, and above all the ninth-century BCE Mesha Stele. That inscription credits Chemosh with land, victory, anger, and restoration, binding divine patronage to the Moabite kingdom. Local sanctuaries, figurines, offerings, and names invoking several deities show a religious field broader than one royal monument.

## Scope and interpretation

Chemosh’s national prominence resembles other Iron Age Levantine patron-god traditions without making them identical. Biblical portrayals are important but often polemical external witnesses. The category “Moabite religion” therefore describes a regional and political configuration within a related West Semitic environment, reconstructed from sources of sharply unequal purpose and preservation.
