# Nabataean Religion

## Historical setting

The Nabataean kingdom connected northwestern Arabia, southern Syria, and the Levant through caravan trade and urban centers such as Petra and Hegra. Inscriptions and sanctuaries attest Dushara, al-‘Uzza, Allat, Manat, and many local divine titles. Aniconic blocks and betyls were important, while figural imagery and monumental architecture expanded under Hellenistic and Roman interaction.

## Scope and interpretation

Nabataean practice combined Arabian, Aramaic-speaking, Levantine, Greek, and Roman forms without becoming a uniform synthetic creed. Divine identifications varied by site and patron. Petra’s spectacular remains should not obscure rural, household, and funerary religion or assume that every Nabataean shared the same ritual repertoire.
