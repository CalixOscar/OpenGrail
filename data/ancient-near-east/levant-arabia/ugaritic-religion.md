# Ugaritic Religion

## Historical setting

Texts excavated at Late Bronze Age Ugarit on the Syrian coast preserve myths, offering lists, divination, correspondence, and ritual instructions in Ugaritic and other languages. The Baal Cycle portrays conflict among Baal, Yamm, Mot, Anat, El, and Athirat, while administrative evidence embeds divine cult in palace, temple, household, and international life.

## Scope and interpretation

Ugarit supplies the richest textual window onto a northern Canaanite religious world, but it cannot stand unchanged for every Levantine city or later century. Literary myths are not straightforward transcripts of ritual. Comparisons with biblical language and Phoenician evidence are often illuminating when shared Northwest Semitic vocabulary is distinguished from claims of direct dependence.
