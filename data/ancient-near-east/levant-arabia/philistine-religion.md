# Philistine Religion

## Historical setting

Philistine communities appeared in the southern coastal Levant during the upheavals around 1200 BCE. Early material culture shows Aegean and eastern Mediterranean connections, followed by increasingly local forms. Temples at sites such as Ekron, inscriptions, figurines, animal remains, and external texts attest deities including Ptgyh, Dagon, and Baal-zebub, though their interpretation remains debated.

## Scope and interpretation

Philistine religion was neither a preserved package of Mycenaean belief nor simply Canaanite religion under a new ethnic label. Generations of migration, intermarriage, trade, and political competition produced hybrid local traditions. The branch placement marks its Iron Age Levantine setting; the cross-link records plausible Aegean contribution without claiming a transparent pantheon-to-pantheon transfer.
