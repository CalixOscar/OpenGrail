# Ammonite Religion

## Historical setting

The Ammonite kingdom occupied territory around modern Amman during the Iron Age. Inscriptions, personal names, figurines, monumental remains, and external accounts indicate worship of Milkom and other divine powers. Royal language joined divine favor to dynasty and territory, while household materials point to practices not exhausted by official state cult.

## Scope and interpretation

Evidence for Ammon is much thinner than the archives from Ugarit or Mesopotamia, so reconstructions must remain modest. Even the identities and spellings of major divine names are debated in particular inscriptions. Ammonite religion belongs in a related Canaanite and broader West Semitic setting, but political proximity does not imply one standardized Levantine pantheon.
