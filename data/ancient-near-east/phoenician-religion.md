# Phoenician Religion

## Historical context

Phoenician religion refers to the Iron Age traditions of coastal cities such as Tyre, Sidon, Byblos, and Arwad and to related Punic communities overseas. There was no unified Phoenician church or canonical mythology. Evidence is unevenly preserved in inscriptions, sanctuaries, votive objects, classical witnesses, and archaeology, with each city maintaining distinctive divine patrons and civic ritual calendars.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Melqart, Eshmun, Astarte, Baal Hammon, and Tanit became prominent in different places and periods. Maritime mobility carried cult practices through Cyprus, North Africa, Iberia, and other Mediterranean networks, where exchange produced local forms. The branch relation signals linguistic and historical continuity with broader Canaanite worlds. Greek and Roman descriptions can preserve useful information but also impose foreign categories and polemical assumptions.
