# Assyrian Religion

## Historical context

Assyrian religious life formed around the city of Aššur and expanded with Old, Middle, and Neo-Assyrian political power. Its archives share much Akkadian scholarly and ritual literature with Babylonia, while local institutions and the god Aššur gave the northern tradition a distinctive civic focus. Royal inscriptions portray conquest through divine commission, but court ideology cannot stand for every household or province.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Aššur's changing representation, Ishtar's major sanctuaries, prophetic consultation, treaties, divination, purification, and temple service structured state religion. Assyrian scribes copied Babylonian works while also developing their own recensions and archives. The branch edge reflects a common Akkadian-language matrix rather than dependence on a single source. Modern Assyrian Christian identity should not be collapsed into ancient imperial religion despite geographic and naming continuities.
