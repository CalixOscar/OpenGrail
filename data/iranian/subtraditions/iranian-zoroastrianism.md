# Iranian Zoroastrianism

## Historical formation

Zoroastrian communities persisted in Iran after the Islamic conquests despite legal disadvantage, demographic decline, migration, and periodic persecution. Yazd and Kerman became especially important centers. Exchanges of questions and answers with Parsi priests produced the Persian Rivayats, while nineteenth- and twentieth-century reform, urbanization, and diaspora reshaped communal institutions.

## Community and distinction

Iranian Zoroastrians share scripture, ritual lineages, and modern networks with Parsis but possess distinct language histories, calendars, customs, and social experiences. Older outsider labels can be derogatory and should not replace community self-designations such as Zartoshti. Contemporary Iranian practice includes both priestly ritual continuity and diverse modern reinterpretations.
