# Gathic Zoroastrianism

## Textual horizon

The Gāthās are Old Avestan hymns embedded in the Yasna and attributed by tradition to Zarathustra. Their archaic language is closely related to Vedic Sanskrit, but their historical setting and the prophet’s date remain disputed. They address Ahura Mazda, truth or right order, moral choice, ritual community, and beneficent powers in compressed poetic language.

## Scope and interpretation

“Gathic Zoroastrianism” names a textual-historical layer, not a separately organized denomination. Later Avestan and Middle Persian traditions interpret the hymns through institutions and cosmologies that cannot simply be read back into every verse. Modern reformers sometimes privilege the Gāthās, but their readings are contemporary theological positions distinct from academic reconstruction of Old Avestan religion.
