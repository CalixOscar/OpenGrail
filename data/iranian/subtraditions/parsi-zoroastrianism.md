# Parsi Zoroastrianism

## Historical formation

Zoroastrian migrants from Iran established enduring communities in western India, conventionally narrated in the later *Qissa-i Sanjan*. Precise dates and details of migration remain debated. Under Gujarati, Mughal, and British rule, Parsis developed distinctive institutions, occupational networks, charitable trusts, priestly lineages, and major urban communities, especially in Bombay.

## Community and distinction

Parsis preserve Avestan liturgy, fire temples, initiation, purity disciplines, and funerary customs while reflecting a specifically South Asian history. Debates over conversion, intermarriage, calendar, reform, and identity are internal and ongoing. “Parsi” is an ethnoreligious community name; it is not interchangeable with all Zoroastrians, including the related Iranian community.
