# Zurvanism

## Historical context

Zurvanism is a scholarly name for doctrines reported especially by late antique Armenian, Syriac, Greek, and Middle Persian witnesses. These accounts make Zurvan, often understood as infinite Time, the parent or ground of the opposed spirits Ohrmazd and Ahriman. Some evidence points to important Sasanian adherents, but no independent Zurvanite canon, church, or complete self-description survives.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The category may represent several speculative tendencies rather than one denomination, and scholars dispute its antiquity, coherence, and place within Zoroastrian history. Hostile outsiders sometimes supplied the fullest narratives, so their symmetry should not be projected onto every Iranian source. The minority-tier branch edge makes that uncertainty explicit: Zurvanite ideas are best studied as contested developments inside Zoroastrian discourse, not as an securely bounded rival religion.
