# Chinese Salvationist Religions (Comparative)

## Historical context

Scholars use categories such as “salvationist religions,” “redemptive societies,” or formerly “sectarian religions” for a changing range of Chinese lay movements from the late imperial through modern periods. Many circulated through congregational halls, spirit-writing groups, vegetarian communities, charitable societies, and household networks despite alternating patronage and suppression.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Recurring features include revelation, moral cultivation, initiation, millennial renewal, the Unborn or Eternal Mother, Maitreya, and claims to unite the Three Teachings. No feature applies to every organization. This node is a comparative aid, not a self-identifying super-church; labels such as “White Lotus” were often imposed broadly by officials and can obscure movements’ own names and genealogies.
