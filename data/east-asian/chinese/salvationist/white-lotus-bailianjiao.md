# White Lotus

## Historical context

The White Lotus movement began during the Southern Song dynasty around 1133 CE, founded by the Buddhist monk Mao Ziyuan (1086–1166) as the White Lotus Society (*Bailian hui*). Drawing upon the 5th-century Pure Land fellowship of Huiyuan on Mount Lu, Mao established a lay Buddhist movement where men and women participated equally in vegetarian fellowship, Amitābha Buddha recitation, and repentance rituals without taking monastic vows.

Over subsequent centuries, particularly during the Mongol Yuan (1271–1368), Ming (1368–1644), and Qing (1644–1912) dynasties, the movement absorbed elements of Manichaean dualism, Daoist internal alchemy, and popular Maitreyan millenarianism. As imperial authorities banned unapproved religious gatherings under blanket anti-heresy statutes, groups associated with the White Lotus operated underground. The name became closely linked to major uprisings, including the Red Turban Rebellion that overthrew the Yuan, the Wang Lun uprising of 1774, and the massive White Lotus Rebellion (1796–1804) across central and southwestern China. By the late 19th century, the historical networks had fragmented into distinct redemptive societies such as the Xiantiandao and Yiguandao.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Modern scholarship emphasizes that "White Lotus" was frequently an administrative and polemical label deployed by Ming and Qing imperial officials to categorize diverse, decentralized folk religious sects, rather than a single unified institutional hierarchy. Historical continuity across seven centuries is therefore a matter of academic nuance: while specific lineages were local and ephemeral, a shared mythic and ritual matrix endured across northern and central China.

The core theological innovation of late White Lotus groups was the worship of the *Wusheng Laomu* (Eternal Venerable Mother), the primordial goddess who birthed the ninety-six myriad original souls (*yuanzi*) into the mortal world of red dust (*hongchen*). Human existence is understood as an exile from one's true cosmic mother. Salvation occurs across three great cosmic kalpas: the Green Yang Kalpa governed by Dīpankara Buddha, the Red Yang Kalpa governed by Śākyamuni Buddha, and the impending White Yang Kalpa governed by Maitreya Buddha. Devotees met in domestic halls to chant *baojuan* (precious scrolls), practice breath cultivation, and prepare for the gathering of the faithful into the Mother's primordial Pure Land (*Zhenkong Jiaxiang*).
