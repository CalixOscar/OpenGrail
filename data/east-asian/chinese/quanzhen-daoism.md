# Quanzhen Daoism

## Historical context

Quanzhen, “Complete Perfection,” arose in twelfth-century north China around Wang Chongyang and disciples later remembered as the Seven Perfected. The movement established monastic communities and expanded under Mongol rule. The Longmen lineage became particularly prominent, though Quanzhen history contains multiple regional transmissions and later reinventions.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Quanzhen discipline joins celibate monastic life, meditation, internal alchemy, ethical precepts, liturgy, and study. Early writings drew selectively on Buddhist and Confucian vocabulary while maintaining Daoist goals and lineages. Its frequent description as a “Three Teachings synthesis” should not erase its institutional identity as Daoism or imply that all three traditions became doctrinally identical.
