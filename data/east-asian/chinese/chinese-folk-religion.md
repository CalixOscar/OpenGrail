# Chinese Folk Religion

## Historical context

Chinese local religion has no single founder or canon. Household ancestor rites, village temples, territorial gods, pilgrimage networks, spirit mediums, opera, and calendrical festivals developed through imperial and modern change. Daoist priests, Buddhist monastics, Confucian ritual ideals, and local specialists often serve the same communities without erasing their institutional distinctions.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Practice sustains reciprocal relations among living families, ancestors, gods, ghosts, and place through offerings, vows, divination, healing, and communal celebration. “Folk” should not imply primitive or unorganized: temples manage property, charities, ritual knowledge, and transregional networks. Many participants do not claim exclusive religious membership, so census categories capture this field imperfectly.
