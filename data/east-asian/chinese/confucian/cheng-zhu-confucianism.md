# Cheng–Zhu Confucianism

## Historical context

Northern Song thinkers Cheng Hao and Cheng Yi developed influential accounts of moral principle, human nature, and cultivation. Zhu Xi synthesized their work with other Song scholars, edited the Four Books, and produced commentaries that became authoritative in civil examinations across East Asia. Later Korean, Japanese, Vietnamese, and Chinese thinkers adopted and contested this inheritance in locally specific ways.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The school treats *li*, coherent principle or pattern, and *qi*, psychophysical material force, as inseparable dimensions of concrete reality. Moral cultivation involves reverent attentiveness, learning, ritual, and the investigation of things. The label “rationalist” can mislead if it suggests a modern secular philosophy: Cheng–Zhu thought integrates cosmology, ancestral rites, embodied practice, and ethical-political order.
