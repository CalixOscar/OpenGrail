# Korean Confucian Traditions

## Historical context

Confucian texts and institutions reached the Korean peninsula through long exchange with China and entered education and government in the Three Kingdoms and Goryeo periods. Joseon rulers made Neo-Confucian learning central to state examinations, academies, family rites, and political debate. Thinkers such as Yi Hwang and Yi I developed sophisticated positions rather than merely repeating Chinese authorities.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Korean traditions give exceptional attention to Zhu Xi learning, ritual propriety, filial relationships, and debates over the moral emotions and the relation of principle to material force. Lineage rites and academies shaped social order but also became sites of faction and reform. “Korean Confucianism” is a regional intellectual-ritual field, not a separate church or a description of every Korean’s personal religion.
