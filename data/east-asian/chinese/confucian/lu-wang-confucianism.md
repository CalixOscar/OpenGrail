# Lu–Wang Confucianism

## Historical context

Lu Jiuyuan challenged approaches he considered overly dependent on external textual investigation, arguing that the moral mind already participates in universal principle. Ming thinker Wang Yangming renewed and expanded this current after a transformative experience in exile. His teachings spread through academies and disciples in China, Japan, Korea, and Vietnam, often provoking orthodox criticism.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Lu–Wang teaching emphasizes the unity of knowledge and action, recovery of innate moral awareness, and disciplined attention to selfish obstruction. “Innate knowing” does not mean acting on every spontaneous preference; it requires ethical cultivation and correction in concrete affairs. Comparisons with Chan are historically relevant because critics and practitioners recognized shared vocabulary, yet the school frames awakening through Confucian relationships and public responsibility.
