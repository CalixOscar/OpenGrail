# New Confucianism

## Historical context

New Confucianism emerged among twentieth-century Chinese thinkers confronting imperial collapse, iconoclastic modernism, war, Marxism, and Western philosophy. Xiong Shili, Mou Zongsan, Tang Junyi, and later scholars defended the creative resources of Confucian learning while disagreeing about metaphysics, democracy, science, and cultural identity.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The movement treats moral self-cultivation and relational personhood as resources for modern life rather than antiquarian survivals. Some authors reconstruct mind-and-nature metaphysics; others emphasize civic ethics or dialogue with Kant and contemporary philosophy. New Confucianism is primarily an intellectual current, though it participates in a broader revival of ritual, education, and Confucian public identity.
