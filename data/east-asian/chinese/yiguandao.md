# Yiguandao

## Historical context

Yiguandao developed from Chinese popular sectarian and salvationist networks and expanded rapidly under Zhang Tianran and Sun Suzhen during the Republican era. Suppressed in mainland China after 1949, it grew in Taiwan and later through global Chinese diasporas. Its decentralized temple networks have produced organizational and regional diversity.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Teachings center on the Unborn or Eternal Mother, a final age of cosmic crisis, initiation into the Dao, vegetarian ethics, ancestor care, and missionary merit. Yiguandao presents Confucianism, Buddhism, and Daoism as expressions of one truth, while also incorporating figures associated with Christianity and Islam. This universalism is its own theological claim, not an academic conclusion that the traditions are equivalent.
