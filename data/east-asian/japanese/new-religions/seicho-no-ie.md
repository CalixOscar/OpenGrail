# Seichō-no-Ie

## Historical context

Seichō-no-Ie ("House of Growth") was founded in 1930 by Taniguchi Masaharu (1893–1985). Originally an English translator and literary intellectual, Taniguchi explored various religious movements in Taishō-era Japan, serving as an editor for the millenarian movement Ōmoto and studying Western New Thought and Christian Science literature (notably the writings of Fenwicke Holmes and Mary Baker Eddy). In 1929, he experienced a decisive mystical realization that matter is non-existent and that humanity is inherently divine, perfect, and sinless.

In March 1930, Taniguchi began publishing the magazine *Seichō-no-Ie*, which quickly developed into a nationwide publishing and spiritual movement. His magnum opus, the 40-volume *Seimei no Jissō* (*The Truth of Life*), became one of Japan's most widely distributed religious texts. During the post-war era, Seichō-no-Ie grew into one of Japan's largest new religions with millions of adherents across Japan, the Americas (notably Brazil), and Europe. Under later leadership, particularly under Masanobu Taniguchi, the movement pivoted strongly toward environmental activism, zero-carbon temple operations, and global ecological ethics.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Seichō-no-Ie presents a metaphysical synthesis known as the "Threefold Teaching," integrating Shinto reverence for the divine life principle (*Ame-no-Minakanushi* and *kami*), Mahayana Buddhist teachings on emptiness and Buddha-nature, and Western idealism's focus on the creative power of the mind. The foundational distinction is between the *Jissō* (the Real Image or True State), which is eternally perfect, healthy, and abundant, and the *Genshō* (the Phenomenal World), which is merely a shadow projected by human consciousness.

The central spiritual practice is *Shinsōkan* (Meditation on the Divine Image), a structured daily contemplation in which practitioners sit in formal posture, adjust their breathing, and affirm: "I am a child of God; the divine life fills my entire being." Practitioners also recite the sacred scripture *Kanro no Hōu* (*Nectar of Truth*) for the spiritual pacification of ancestral souls and the healing of bodily ailments. Seichō-no-Ie emphasizes that reconciliation and deep gratitude toward one's parents and forebears are essential prerequisites for personal health, prosperity, and spiritual awakening.
