# Happy Science

## Historical context

Happy Science (Kōfuku-no-Kagaku) was established in Tokyo in October 1986 by Ōkawa Ryūhō (1956–2023, born Nakagawa Takashi), a graduate of the University of Tokyo who worked for a major Japanese trading corporation before launching his religious career. In 1981, Ōkawa began receiving what he described as spiritual communications from high spirits, including Nikko (a Nichiren disciple), Jesus Christ, Confucius, and Socrates. He published these as bestselling books, turning the movement into a mass-media phenomenon across Japan in the late 1980s and early 1990s.

The movement achieved official status as a religious corporation (*shūkyō hōjin*) in 1991 and expanded internationally, founding temples and missionary centers in North America, Europe, Asia, and Africa. In 2009, Happy Science entered electoral politics by founding the Happiness Realization Party (*Kōfuku Jitsugen-tō*), which fields candidates advocating conservative, national defense, and free-market policies. Following Ōkawa's death in March 2023, leadership shifted to the organization's board of directors and senior executives amidst discussions over spiritual succession.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The theological core of Happy Science centers on El Cantare, whom the movement identifies as the supreme primordial Buddha and God of the Earth. According to the movement's self-understanding—classified epistemically as a theological claim rather than an empirical or historical consensus—El Cantare has dispatched aspects of his spiritual consciousness into human history, including Alpha in ancient South America, Elohim in the Middle East, Hermes in ancient Greece, and Gautama Buddha in India, culminating in his complete earthly incarnation in Ryūhō Ōkawa.

Happy Science’s doctrine synthesizes Mahayana Buddhist ethics, Western New Thought positive thinking, and twentieth-century spiritualism. Its core spiritual discipline is the "Exploration of the Right Mind" through the Fourfold Path: the Love that Gives (unconditional service), Wisdom (spiritual study), Self-Reflection (correcting selfish karmic tendencies), and Progress (spiritual and worldly success). The movement has produced hundreds of books purporting to channel the spirits of religious leaders, philosophers, and contemporary politicians, and operates publishing houses, movie studios producing anime and live-action films, and private educational institutions.
