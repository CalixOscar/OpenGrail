# PL Kyōdan

## Historical context

The roots of PL Kyōdan (Church of Perfect Liberty) trace to the early twentieth century. In 1916, Zen Buddhist priest Kanada Tokumitsu founded the Tokuharukai, which was reorganized in 1924 by his disciple Miki Tokuharu (1871–1938) as Hito-no-Michi Kyōdan ("Way of Humanity"). The movement expanded rapidly during the interwar era through faith healing and moral teachings. In 1936, the militarist government suppressed Hito-no-Michi under the Peace Preservation Law, dissolving its organization and imprisoning its leaders on charges of lèse-majesté.

Following World War II and the postwar implementation of religious freedom, Tokuharu's son, Miki Tokuchika (1900–1983), revived the movement on September 29, 1946, in Tosu, Saga Prefecture, under the name Perfect Liberty (PL) Kyōdan. Under Tokuchika's leadership, the church established a vast international headquarters in Tondabayashi, Osaka Prefecture, constructing the iconic 180-meter Daiheiwa Kinen Tō (Great Peace Prayer Tower) in 1970 and sponsoring international schools, golf courses, and cultural festivals.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

PL Kyōdan's philosophy is encapsulated in its first principle: "Life is Art" (*Jinsei wa Geijutsu de aru*). The human being is understood as a child of the supreme cosmic parent deity, Mioya-Ōkami, endowed with creative agency. Illness, financial distress, and interpersonal conflict are not viewed as arbitrary misfortunes or divine punishments, but as *kokoro no kuse* (distortions of the ego) that manifest as physical and psychological "warnings" (*mishirase*).

To address these warnings, followers consult church ministers who transmit personalized divine directives (*mioshie*) explaining which selfish mental attitude needs correction. Once the individual adjusts their mindset and expresses their authentic self in harmony with others, the problem is understood to resolve naturally. The organization places high spiritual value on artistic performance, sportsmanship, and grand spectacles—notably the annual PL Art Fireworks, among the largest displays in the world, offered as a communal prayer for universal world peace.
