# Tenshō Kōtai Jingūkyō

## Historical context

Tenshō Kōtai Jingūkyō was founded by Kitamura Sayo (1900–1967), a farmer’s wife from Tabuse in Yamaguchi Prefecture. Following severe domestic hardship and prolonged ascetic water-purification practices (*mizugori*), Kitamura began experiencing spirit communications in 1944. On July 22, 1945, she proclaimed that the absolute cosmic deity Tenshō Kōtai Jin had permanently descended into her body, designating her as the living mouth and mediator of God, whom followers addressed as *Ogamisama* (the Great Deity).

In the immediate aftermath of Japan's defeat in World War II, Kitamura traveled across the country preaching fearless moral repentance, lambasting political corruption and societal hypocrisy, and calling people to build a spiritual kingdom of peace. The movement attracted intense public and media attention due to the exuberant, uninhibited dancing of its converts in public squares, earning the popular moniker *Odoru Shūkyō* ("the Dancing Religion"). Following Kitamura's death in 1967, leadership passed to her granddaughter Kitamura Kiyokazu, and the community maintains its international headquarters and sacred precinct in Tabuse.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

At the heart of Tenshō Kōtai Jingūkyō is the doctrine that humanity must cleanse itself of accumulated spiritual impurities (*meimō*) and six negative emotions—regret, greed, hatred, attachment, craving for affection, and egotistical ambition—to allow the divine light to dwell within. Salvation is not achieved through ritual commodities, talismans, or paid priestly intercession, which Ogamisama vehemently rejected, but through rigorous daily self-examination (*hansei*).

The most distinctive ritual manifestation is *muga-no-mai* (the Dance of Selflessness or Non-Ego). Devotees chant a continuous prayer for world peace and the purification of six root evils while moving their limbs and bodies spontaneously in rhythm with inner divine promptings. This ecstatic dance serves as both a physical release of ego-centric tension and a communal affirmation of spiritual rebirth into a selfless, divine life.
