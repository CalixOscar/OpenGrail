# Agon Shū

## Historical context

Agon Shū ("The Āgama School") was founded in 1978 by Kiriyama Seiyū (1921–2016, born Tsutsumi Masao). After overcoming severe illness and poverty in his early adult life, Kiriyama studied Shingon esoteric Buddhism and established the Kannon Ebisu-kai in 1954. In the 1970s, after extensive research into the *Āgama Sūtras* (the Chinese translations of early Buddhist Nikāyas, long neglected in traditional East Asian Mahayana curricula), Kiriyama declared that these early scriptures contained the authentic meditation practices and "karmic severing" methods taught directly by the historical Buddha.

In 1978, Kiriyama reorganized his following into Agon Shū, establishing its international headquarters in Yamashina, Kyoto. The movement expanded rapidly during the late 1970s and 1980s through prolific publishing, high-profile televised fire rituals, and the acquisition of sacred Buddhist relics from Sri Lanka and Bhutan. Following Kiriyama's death in 2016, the organization continues under its ordained council and monastic leadership.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Agon Shū presents a distinctive synthesis in modern Japanese religion, often categorized by scholars as a "shin-shinshūkyō" (new new religion). While traditional Japanese Buddhism favored Mahayana texts such as the *Lotus Sutra* or *Nirvana Sutra*, Agon Shū claims that the *Āgamas* provide the only complete systematic manual for liberating the human spirit from ancestral distress and negative karmic inheritance (*innen*).

Central to Agon Shū’s religious practice is the *goma* (esoteric fire rite), culminating in the spectacular annual *Hoshi Matsuri* (Star Festival) held every February in the Kyoto hills. Hundreds of thousands of pilgrims assemble as two immense sacred pyres—one for the pacification of the dead (Buddha’s Realm) and one for worldly blessings of the living (Vajra Realm)—are ignited using traditional Shugendō and Shingon liturgies. Devotees offer millions of wooden prayer sticks (*gomagi*) inscribed with personal wishes and ancestral names, believing the sacred flames instantly purify spiritual defilements and avert natural and geopolitical disasters.
