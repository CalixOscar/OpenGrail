# Shinnyo-en

## Historical context

Shinnyo-en ("Garden of Absolute Reality") was founded in Tachikawa, Tokyo, on February 8, 1936, by Itō Shinjō (1906–1989), an aeronautical engineer turned Buddhist master, and his wife Itō Tomoji (1912–1967). In 1936, Shinjō entered the historic Daigo-ji monastery in Kyoto—the head temple of the Sanbō-in lineage of Shingon esoteric Buddhism—where he underwent rigorous monastic training and attained the rank of *Dai-Ajari* (Great Acharya).

In 1951, the movement adopted the name Shinnyo-en and established its central temple, the Shōjōshin-in, in Tachikawa. Following the death of Tomoji in 1967 and their two sons who passed away in childhood (venerated as spiritual guides, the *Ryōdōji*), the spiritual leadership of the international sangha passed to their daughter, Itō Shinsō (born 1942), also an ordained Great Acharya. Shinnyo-en has expanded into a worldwide fellowship with temple centers and membership across North America, Europe, Asia, and Oceania.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Shinnyo-en occupies a unique position in Japanese Buddhism by uniting the ritual orthodoxy of traditional Shingon esoteric Buddhism (*Mikkyō*) with the inclusive egalitarianism of the *Mahāyāna Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra* (Nirvana Sutra). The Nirvana Sutra's core proclamation—that Buddha-nature is permanent, joyful, self-sustaining, and pure (*jō, raku, ga, jō*), and inherent in all sentient beings without exception—forms the doctrinal foundation of all Shinnyo-en teachings.

The signature practice of Shinnyo-en is *sesshin*, a structured meditative practice in which practitioners sit in quiet contemplation while a certified spiritual guide (*reidō*) provides brief, individualized verbal reflections. These reflections are intended to help practitioners observe subconscious ego patterns, heal psychological wounds, and discover practical ways to express compassion in daily life. Shinnyo-en is also widely recognized for its large-scale public ceremonies, such as the *Saitō Goma* (outdoor fire rite) and the Floating Lantern Ceremonies (such as the annual Lantern Floating Hawaii), which gather diverse religious communities to commemorate lost loved ones and pray for global peace.
