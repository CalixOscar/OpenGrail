# State Shinto

## Historical context

After the Meiji Restoration, the government separated kami and buddhas, reorganized shrines, ranked priesthoods, and connected imperial rites with national education. Officials often designated shrine observance as nonreligious civic ceremony, enabling its public support while other religious bodies were regulated separately. The system was dismantled under the 1945 Shinto Directive.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

State Shinto was an administrative and ideological formation, not all Shinto practice between 1868 and 1945. Participation varied, and shrines retained local functions even as national symbolism intensified. Scholars debate the term’s precise scope, but agree that modern institutions transformed older rites. Distinguishing State Shinto from contemporary shrine practice avoids treating nationalism as Shinto’s timeless essence.
