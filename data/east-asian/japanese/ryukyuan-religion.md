# Ryukyuan Religion

## Historical context

The Ryukyu Islands developed ritual systems through island communities, the Ryukyu Kingdom, maritime exchange, and later Japanese incorporation. Royal priestesses headed by the *kikoe-ōgimi* once coordinated state rites, while village priestesses, household ritualists, and spirit mediums sustained local practice. War and modernization disrupted many sacred landscapes.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Ancestors, hearth powers, sea and land presences, and sacred groves called *utaki* remain central in varied combinations. Women have often held prominent ritual roles, though practice differs by island and period. Similarities with Shinto do not make Ryukyuan religion a regional subtype; Chinese, Japanese, and Southeast Asian contacts entered a distinct island history.
