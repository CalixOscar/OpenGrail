# Shinto

## Historical context

Practices concerning *kami* long preceded the label Shinto as a bounded religion. Court myth, local shrines, clan rites, pilgrimage, and agricultural festivals developed together with imported writing and institutions. For more than a millennium kami worship was frequently organized through Buddhist temples and theories until modern governments imposed administrative separation.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Shrine rites cultivate proper relations with kami through purification, offerings, prayer, festival, and care for sacred places. Shinto has no single founder or universal creed; priestly organizations, local communities, imperial rites, and new movements differ. Describing kami simply as “gods” can mislead, since the category includes powerful presences associated with landscape, ancestors, events, and extraordinary persons.
