# Jinja Shinto

## Historical context

After Japan’s 1945 separation of religion and state, shrines formerly embedded in government administration reorganized as private religious corporations. Jinja Honchō, commonly translated as the Association of Shinto Shrines, formed in 1946 and today affiliates many—but not all—shrines. Local shrines retain their own histories, priestly families, kami, parish relations, and festivals.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Shrine practice centers offerings, purification, prayer, seasonal festivals, amulets, and care for kami tied to places, communities, occupations, or national mythology. “Jinja Shinto” is an institutional and scholarly category, not a creed to which every visitor formally subscribes. It must also be distinguished from prewar State Shinto and from independent Sect Shinto organizations.
