# Konkōkyō

## Historical context

Farmer Kawate Bunjirō, later known as Konkō Daijin, understood a series of experiences culminating in 1859 as a call from Tenchi Kane no Kami to serve people through spiritual mediation. A network of worship halls grew around his teaching and was recognized within the Meiji Sect Shinto system. Communities later spread beyond Japan, especially through migration.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Konkōkyō teaches a mutually dependent relationship between the divine parent of the universe and human beings, gratitude amid ordinary life, and *toritsugi*, mediation through listening and counsel. It lacks a rigid conversion boundary and draws on the ritual world of Japanese kami while developing a distinct revealed theology. Its historical legal classification does not reduce it to state shrine practice.
