# Shugendō

## Historical context

Shugendō took shape around sacred mountains, itinerant practitioners called *yamabushi*, Buddhist temples, and local kami cults. Traditions associate the path with the legendary En no Gyōja. Esoteric Buddhist institutions systematized many rites, while regional pilgrimage circuits retained distinctive lineages. Meiji separation policies prohibited Shugendō temporarily, but communities later revived.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Mountain entry, fasting, waterfall austerity, mantra, fire ritual, and guided pilgrimage transform the practitioner through direct encounter with a sacral landscape. Shugendō is paradigmatically combinatory, yet “syncretism” should not imply a random mixture: initiations, ranks, routes, and temple affiliations organize practice. Contemporary groups vary in accessibility and institutional connection.
