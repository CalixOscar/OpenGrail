# Sect Shinto

## Historical context

Meiji authorities classified thirteen organizations as Sect Shinto, including mountain-worship, purification, healing, and revival movements. Some drew on older confraternities; others formed around charismatic founders. The category distinguished them from government-administered shrines and subjected them to the legal framework for religious teaching organizations.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The recognized sects never shared one theology: they venerated different kami, mountains, founders, and ritual disciplines. “Sect Shinto” is therefore best understood as a historical legal umbrella, not a coherent denomination. Some later movements, including Tenrikyo, passed through related classification while developing strong independent identities. Modern descendants may prefer their own names and histories to the inherited category.
