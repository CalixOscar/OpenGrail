# Dangun Veneration (Comparative)

## Historical context

Medieval Korean texts narrate Dangun as founder of the first Korean polity, while the story drew on older sacred-geographical and ancestral themes. From the late nineteenth century, intellectuals and new religions reworked Dangun as a focus of national origin under imperial pressure and Japanese colonization. Public commemorations, scholarship, and religious organizations continue to interpret the figure differently.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

This node groups a motif and ritual field, not one self-identifying denomination. Dangun may appear as divine ancestor, culture hero, historical symbol, or national emblem depending on the community. Its visual placement near Korean shamanic traditions marks one set of mythic and ritual affinities, not a claim that every Dangun movement is simply a branch of shamanism.
