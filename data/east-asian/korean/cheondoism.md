# Cheondoism

## Historical context

Choe Je-u founded Donghak, “Eastern Learning,” in 1860 amid foreign pressure and domestic crisis. The movement joined revelation, ethical discipline, and critique of corrupt hierarchy; followers participated in the 1894 Donghak uprising. Renamed Cheondoism under Son Byeong-hui, it became influential in education, publishing, and Korea’s 1919 independence movement.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Cheondoism teaches that humans bear or serve Heaven within, grounding radical dignity and moral responsibility. Incantation, reverence, disciplined sincerity, and social reform connect inward cultivation with a renewed world. Confucian, Daoist, Buddhist, Catholic, and Korean ritual contexts all mattered, but the tradition’s revelatory lineage and national history make it more than an eclectic mixture.
