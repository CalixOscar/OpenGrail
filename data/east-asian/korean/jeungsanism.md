# Jeungsanism

## Historical context

Jeungsanist movements arose in Korea around Kang Il-sun, known as Kang Jeungsan, whose followers understood his ritual work and teaching as a response to the crises of the late Joseon and colonial periods. After his death in 1909, several organizations developed distinct successions, scriptures, and institutions; Jeungsanism is therefore a family label rather than one centralized church.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Jeungsanist traditions commonly anticipate a transformed Later World brought through the resolution of accumulated grievance and the reordering of cosmic relationships. Healing, ethical cultivation, ritual, and reverence for Kang vary by movement. Their vocabulary draws on Korean popular religion, Confucian cosmology, Daoist techniques, Buddhism, and older millenarian currents without reducing cleanly to any one source.
