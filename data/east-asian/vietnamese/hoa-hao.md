# Hòa Hảo Buddhism

## Historical context

Huỳnh Phú Sổ began preaching in 1939 in the western Mekong Delta, building on the earlier Bửu Sơn Kỳ Hương reform environment. The movement grew rapidly among rural communities and became entangled in anticolonial conflict, armed politics, and the founder’s disappearance in 1947. Hòa Hảo institutions survived warfare, diaspora, and changing state regulation.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Hòa Hảo teaching encourages Buddhist moral reform, simple home altars, remembrance of ancestors, charity, and reduced dependence on costly ceremonies or monastic intermediaries. Its plain brown altar cloth and emphasis on inward sincerity express a rural reform ethic. It is a self-identifying Buddhist tradition with a particular Vietnamese history, not merely “folk Buddhism” or a political faction.
