# Tứ Ân Hiếu Nghĩa

## Historical context

Tứ Ân Hiếu Nghĩa ("Four Debts of Gratitude and Filial Righteousness") was founded in 1867 by Ngô Lợi (1831–1890, reverently known by disciples as *Đức Bổn Sư*). Believing himself entrusted with carrying forward the spiritual mission of Đoàn Minh Huyên (Phật Thầy Tây An), Ngô Lợi led hundreds of peasant followers into the remote, dense jungle at the base of Tượng Mountain in Ba Chúc, An Giang Province, near the Cambodian border.

In Ba Chúc, the community cleared virgin land, established village communes, built the Tam Bửu and Phi Lai pagodas, and organized communal farming enterprises. French colonial authorities viewed the settlement with deep suspicion, launching repeated military raids between 1879 and 1888 that burned the pagodas and scattered the villagers. Despite continuous repression, the community repeatedly rebuilt its holy village. Tứ Ân Hiếu Nghĩa remains an officially recognized and active religious community in southwestern Vietnam today, with Ba Chúc as its sacred center.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Tứ Ân Hiếu Nghĩa organizes its teachings around the *Tam Giáo* (Three Teachings)—Buddhism for personal salvation, Confucianism for social and familial morality, and Daoist cosmology for harmonious living with nature and healing. Like Bửu Sơn Kỳ Hương, the tradition does not maintain a monastic sangha of tonsured, celibate monks; instead, all leaders and members are married householders who wear traditional black or brown robes and maintain neat topknots.

Ritual life centers on the *trần điều* (red altar cloth) and the daily chanting of the *Bá gia truyền pháp*, invoking Amitābha Buddha while dedicating merit to the four pillars of life: parents/ancestors, the homeland, the spiritual teachings, and humanity at large. The community places exceptional emphasis on mutual assistance (*tương trợ*), communal burial services, and free traditional medicine dispensaries (*phòng thuốc nam*), modeling an enduring agrarian moral community in the Mekong Delta.
