# Subud

## Historical context

Subud originated in Central Java during the 1920s through the spiritual experiences of Muhammad Subuh Sumohadiwidjojo (1901–1987), an Indonesian municipal clerk known respectfully to his followers as "Bapak" (Father). In 1924, in the city of Semarang, Subuh experienced a series of spontaneous mystical awakenings, described as an intense descending radiance of light that initiated involuntary physical movements, chants, and inner purification. Subuh understood this not as a personal revelation or the founding of a new religion, but as the awakening of a latent spiritual capacity inherent in all humanity.

Throughout the 1930s and 1940s, Subuh shared this practice privately across Yogyakarta and Central Java within the context of *kebatinan* (Javanese mystical movements). The movement expanded internationally beginning in 1957 when J. G. Bennett, a prominent British mathematician and spiritual seeker formerly associated with G. I. Gurdjieff and P. D. Ouspensky, met Bapak in England and introduced the *latihan* to hundreds of Western seekers at Coombe Springs. Rapid global growth followed, leading to the formation of the World Subud Association (WSA), which today coordinates autonomous national associations in over eighty countries.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Subud explicitly maintains that it is **not** a religion, has no creed, sacred dogma, priesthood, or rituals of excommunication. Members are encouraged to remain active within their respective faiths—whether Islam, Christianity, Judaism, Buddhism, Hinduism, or secular humanism—using Subud's spiritual practice to deepen their personal faith and ethical conduct.

The central pillar of Subud is the *latihan kejiwaan* (Javanese: "spiritual training" or "exercise of the soul"). Practiced twice weekly in simple hall settings with men and women gathered in separate rooms, the *latihan* involves no meditation techniques, breathing exercises, visualization, or mental mantras. Practitioners simply stand in quiet stillness and surrender personal will, intellect, and desire to God (*pasrah*). This state of receptivity allows the *Daya Jiwa Agung* (Great Life Force) to manifest as spontaneous physical movements, laughter, weeping, vocal singing, or deep contemplative silence, which practitioners understand as a process of emotional, psychological, and spiritual purification.

Subud also sponsors humanitarian and social initiatives worldwide, notably *Susila Dharma International*, a non-governmental organization recognized by the United Nations that funds medical clinics, schools, and sustainable development projects globally.
