# Philippine Benevolent Missionaries Association

## Historical context

The Philippine Benevolent Missionaries Association (PBMA) was established in 1965 on the isolated island of Dinagat, off the northeastern tip of Mindanao, by Ruben Edera Ecleo Sr. (1934–1987). Ecleo Sr., who had gained widespread fame throughout the Visayas and Mindanao as a charismatic healer and mystic endowed with miraculous powers, registered PBMA as a non-sectarian, fraternal, and charitable organization. Known to his followers as the "Divine Master," Ecleo Sr. taught that divine power could be channeled to heal physical ailments, cast out malevolent spirits, and uplift the marginalized.

Under Ecleo Sr.'s leadership, PBMA grew into a massive grassroots movement claiming millions of registered members across chapters throughout the Philippines and overseas Filipino worker communities. The town of San Jose on Dinagat Island became the movement's spiritual capital, transforming the island's local economy and paving the way for Dinagat's eventual designation as an independent province, with the Ecleo family establishing a enduring political leadership in the region.

Following Ecleo Sr.'s death in 1987, his eldest son Ruben B. Ecleo Jr. succeeded him as Supreme Master. In the early 2000s, the leadership faced severe criminal controversies, culminating in Ecleo Jr.'s 2012 conviction for the 2002 murder of his wife and subsequent convictions for graft and corruption. Despite these high-profile legal crises, PBMA remains active as a widespread fraternal and religious network governed by family members and chapter leaders, with thousands of loyal devotees maintaining regular chapter assemblies, pilgrimage visits to Dinagat, and mutual-aid missions.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

PBMA represents a distinct 20th-century development within Philippine folk religiosity, uniting elements of Allan Kardec's Spiritism (*Espiritismo*), traditional Visayan folk medicine (*panambal*), Catholic sacramentalism, and Rizalista nationalism. Although members are encouraged to remain active participants in their respective churches (primarily Roman Catholicism), PBMA functions as an autonomous esoteric brotherhood.

A central identifier for PBMA members is the wearing of a gold or brass signet ring inscribed with the association's emblem and symbolic lettering, which devotees believe serves as a protective talisman and a conduit for spiritual healing energy. Healing rituals involve the laying on of hands, the whisper of sacred formulas (*orasyon*), and the application of consecrated oil or saliva. The movement is distinguished by strong internal solidarity and mutual aid (*bayanihan*), providing its predominantly working-class and peasant membership with communal labor networks, emergency financial assistance, and housing support.
