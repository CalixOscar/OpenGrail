# Gelug

## Historical context

Tsongkhapa and his disciples founded monasteries including Ganden, Drepung, and Sera in the fifteenth century, drawing on Kadam path teaching and multiple tantric transmissions. The Gelug scholastic curriculum became highly influential. From the seventeenth century, Dalai Lama governments linked Gelug authority with Tibetan state power, though the school was never institutionally uniform.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Gelug training combines monastic discipline, debate, Madhyamaka analysis, graded-path contemplation, and tantra. Its presentation of emptiness emphasizes dependent designation while defending conventional knowledge. The Dalai Lama is one important incarnation lineage, not the head of every Tibetan Buddhist school. Political prominence should likewise not obscure diverse Gelug monasteries, lay communities, and regional histories.
