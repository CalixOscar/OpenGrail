# Chan Buddhism

## Historical context

Chan took shape in medieval China through meditation communities, lineage narratives, monastic institutions, and encounter-dialogue literature. Stories traced transmission from the Buddha through Bodhidharma, while historically documented schools such as Hongzhou, Linji, and Caodong emerged within Chinese Buddhist networks. Chan later became a major component of institutional Buddhism.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Chan stresses direct realization of buddha-nature through seated meditation, ethical discipline, work, ritual, and engagement with a teacher. Claims of being “outside scripture” function rhetorically; Chan monasteries studied and produced abundant texts. Chinese language and Daoist vocabulary shaped expression, but the common claim that Chan simply fuses Buddhism and Daoism overstates derivation and understates Indian Buddhist foundations.
