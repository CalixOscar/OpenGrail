# Jogye Order

## Historical context

The modern Jogye Order emerged through twentieth-century efforts to unify and reform Korean monastic Buddhism after Japanese colonial rule, while invoking older Seon lineages and the name of China’s Caoxi or Jogye tradition. Its institutional consolidation involved disputes over clerical marriage, property, and the restoration of celibate monastic leadership. It now administers many major temples in South Korea and supports international centers.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Jogye training gives prominence to *ganhwa Seon*, meditation focused on a critical phrase or *hwadu*, together with Vinaya, chanting, doctrinal education, repentance, and devotional ritual. The order draws heavily on Jinul’s integration of sudden awakening with gradual cultivation. It is not synonymous with all Korean Buddhism: Taego, Cheontae, and other orders preserve distinct institutions and emphases.
