# Linji Chan

## Historical context

The lineage takes its name from Linji Yixuan, a ninth-century teacher remembered through a later compiled record of sermons and encounters. During the Song dynasty, Linji transmission houses became institutionally dominant and developed influential kōan collections and lineage histories. Monks carried related transmissions to Korea, Vietnam, and Japan, where the Japanese Rinzai name uses another reading of the same characters.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Linji literature favors abrupt exchanges, shouts, paradoxical language, and criticism of attachment to religious status, while its monasteries also maintained ritual, discipline, study, and seated meditation. Later teachers organized encounter stories into curricula for contemplation and commentary. The dramatic rhetoric should not be mistaken for hostility to all scripture, nor should every lineage descended from Linji be assumed to use identical kōan methods.
