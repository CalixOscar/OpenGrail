# Jōdo-shū

## Historical context

Hōnen taught an independent Pure Land path in late Heian and early Kamakura Japan, conventionally dating his school’s founding to 1175. Drawing on Chinese Pure Land masters and Genshin, he selected vocal *nembutsu* as the practice suited to an age of declining Dharma. His diverse disciples formed several lineages; some survived as Jōdo-shū while others contributed to separate movements.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Jōdo-shū encourages trusting Amitābha’s vow and reciting *Namu Amida Butsu*, alongside ethical conduct, liturgy, memorial practice, and study. Hōnen emphasized the selected practice without necessarily denying all value to other Buddhist disciplines. Differences from Jōdo Shinshū concern interpretation of faith, practice, clerical form, and institutional history, not devotion to a different Buddha or Pure Land canon.
