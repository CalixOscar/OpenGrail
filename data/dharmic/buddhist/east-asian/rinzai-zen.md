# Rinzai Zen

## Historical context

Japanese monks including Eisai transmitted Linji-lineage teachings from Song China beginning in the late twelfth century. Rinzai monasteries developed multiple temple networks and became closely connected with warrior governments and elite literary culture. In the eighteenth century, Hakuin Ekaku and his successors revitalized training and shaped the kōan curricula used by most surviving Japanese Rinzai lines.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Rinzai training commonly integrates zazen, interviews with a teacher, liturgy, manual work, and sequential engagement with kōans. Institutional branches differ, and some emphasize scripture, arts, or pastoral ritual as much as formal kōan study. Describing Rinzai solely through sudden enlightenment overlooks long discipline and post-awakening cultivation, while its historical elite associations do not encompass all of its communities.
