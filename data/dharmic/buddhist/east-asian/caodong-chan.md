# Caodong Chan

## Historical context

Caodong emerged among the “five houses” retrospectively used to map late Tang and Five Dynasties Chan. Its name combines lineage associations with Dongshan Liangjie and Caoshan Benji, though historical transmission was more complex than a simple two-founder chain. The house declined institutionally in parts of China and was later renewed; a transmission carried to Japan by Dōgen became foundational for Sōtō Zen.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Texts linked to Caodong explore relational patterns such as the Five Ranks and later emphasize “silent illumination,” a term especially connected with Hongzhi Zhengjue. Practice joins seated meditation with liturgy, monastic work, ethical discipline, and doctrinal reflection. Contrasts with Linji’s supposed sudden dynamism are useful historically but easily become caricatures, since both houses used encounter literature and multiple contemplative techniques.
