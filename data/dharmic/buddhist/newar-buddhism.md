# Newar Buddhism

## Historical context

Newar Buddhism preserves Sanskrit Mahāyāna and Vajrayāna texts and rituals in the Kathmandu Valley through hereditary ritual specialists, monasteries, festivals, and lay associations. Its institutions developed through exchange with Indian and Tibetan Buddhism while remaining embedded in Newar urban society. The disappearance of celibate monastic ordination did not end its complex priestly and initiatory traditions.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Vajrācārya and Śākya lineages conduct initiations, life-cycle rites, deity practices, and communal observances. Buddhist and Hindu deities, festivals, and spaces overlap extensively, yet participants maintain meaningful identities and ritual jurisdictions. Describing the tradition as “corrupted monasticism” imposes a narrow model; it is a durable householder Vajrayāna formation with its own authoritative transmissions.
