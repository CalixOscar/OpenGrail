# Engaged Buddhism

## Historical context

The English label “Engaged Buddhism” gained currency through Thích Nhất Hạnh during the Vietnam War, but related Buddhist activism developed independently across Asia and beyond. Movements have addressed colonialism, caste, war, dictatorship, poverty, prisons, gender, and ecological crisis through different institutions and political strategies.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Engaged Buddhists interpret compassion, interdependence, non-harming, and the bodhisattva path as mandates to confront social structures as well as individual distress. Approaches range from relief work and reconciliation to protest and community organizing. The category can hide regional histories if treated as a Western innovation; it can also romanticize activism by overlooking disagreement and political compromise within Buddhist communities.
