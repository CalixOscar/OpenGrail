# Early Buddhist Schools

## Historical context

In the centuries after the Buddha, monastic communities differentiated through ordination lineages, disciplinary codes, geography, patronage, and doctrinal analysis. Traditional lists speak of eighteen schools, but names, dates, and genealogies vary by source. Archaeology and manuscripts reveal a more fluid landscape than a single clean schism tree.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Schools produced distinct Vinayas and Abhidharma systems while sharing teachings on dependent arising, ethical discipline, meditation, and liberation. Theravāda preserves one continuing ordination and canonical lineage; Sarvāstivāda and Mahāsāṃghika writings survive partly in Chinese, Tibetan, and Sanskrit witnesses. “Early Buddhism” is a historical reconstruction from overlapping evidence, not a modern denomination or a perfectly uniform original community.
