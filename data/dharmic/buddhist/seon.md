# Seon Buddhism

## Historical context

Meditation lineages reached Korea from Tang China and were remembered as the Nine Mountain Schools. During Goryeo, Jinul developed an influential synthesis of Seon practice and doctrinal learning at the retreat community of Suseonsa. Later suppression and revival, colonial rule, and postwar institutional divisions shaped modern Korean Seon.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Seon often combines sudden awakening with gradual cultivation and uses *hwadu* inquiry focused on a critical phrase. Monastic meditation retreats coexist with liturgy, scripture, funerals, and lay devotion. Korean Buddhist temples also accommodated mountain-spirit and indigenous ritual imagery, but “syncretism” should describe particular practices rather than reduce Seon to shamanism.
