# Cambodian Buddhism

## Historical context

Theravāda became dominant after centuries in which Khmer courts supported Hindu and Mahāyāna institutions, especially during the post-Angkor period. Village monasteries served as centers of learning, ritual, and social life under both the majority Mohanikay and the royal-connected Thommayut order. The Khmer Rouge killed or displaced monastics and destroyed temples; ordination and education were rebuilt after 1979 in Cambodia and the diaspora.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Cambodian Buddhist life includes merit transfer, ancestor rites, chanting, temporary ordination, festivals, ethical precepts, and protective practices as well as meditation. Monks participate in education, memorialization, and peace work, but their political roles remain contested. Local spirit and Brahmanical elements reflect Cambodia’s long religious history and should not be treated as peripheral contamination of an otherwise “pure” Theravāda.
