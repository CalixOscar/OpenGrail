# Burmese Buddhism

## Historical context

Buddhism was present in the region long before Pagan, but royal support under Anawrahta and later dynasties made Theravāda institutions increasingly prominent from the eleventh century. Burmese courts sponsored pagodas, textual recensions, ordination reforms, and monastic examinations. Colonial disruption and twentieth-century lay meditation movements transformed these systems without eliminating the village monastery’s educational and ritual roles.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Burmese Buddhism combines Pāli scholarship, strict Vinaya currents, merit-making, pagoda devotion, spirit-related practices, and intensive meditation. Several state-recognized monastic orders coexist, while methods associated with Ledi Sayadaw, Mahāsī Sayadaw, U Ba Khin, and Pa-Auk became globally influential. The regional label includes ethnic and institutional diversity and should not be reduced to one meditation technique or political position.
