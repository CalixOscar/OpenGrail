# Sri Lankan Buddhism

## Historical context

Buddhist chronicles connect the island’s first durable monastic establishment with missions during Aśoka’s reign in the third century BCE. Anurādhapura monasteries developed distinct fraternities, and the Mahāvihāra lineage eventually supplied the institutional ancestry claimed by modern Theravāda. Sri Lankan monks, manuscripts, relics, and ordination missions repeatedly circulated through mainland Southeast Asia, even as warfare and colonial rule disrupted local institutions.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Sri Lankan practice joins Pāli learning and monastic discipline with pilgrimage, relic and bodhi-tree devotion, protective chanting, merit-making, and worship of regional deities. Modern reformers promoted lay meditation, print culture, education, and Buddhist nationalism in response to Christian missions and colonial government. “Sri Lankan Buddhism” is therefore a regional tradition containing multiple nikāyas and political outlooks, not a single centralized church.
