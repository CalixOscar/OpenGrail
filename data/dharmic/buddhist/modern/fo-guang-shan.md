# Fo Guang Shan

## Historical context

The monk Hsing Yun founded Fo Guang Shan in southern Taiwan in 1967. It grew into an international network of temples, schools, museums, publishing programs, social services, and the lay Buddha’s Light International Association. The organization emerged from Chinese monastic lineages reshaped by migration to Taiwan and later developed culturally adaptive communities across several continents.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Fo Guang Shan teaches Humanistic Buddhism through the integration of Chan, Pure Land devotion, precepts, study, ritual, culture, and public service. Its polished institutions and large events are part of a deliberate effort to make Buddhist life accessible and socially engaged. It is one influential Humanistic Buddhist organization rather than an authority over every group using that broader label.
