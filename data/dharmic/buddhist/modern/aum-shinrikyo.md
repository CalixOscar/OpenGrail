# Aum Shinrikyo (Aleph / Hikari no Wa)

## Historical context

Aum Shinrikyo ("Aum Supreme Truth") was established in Tokyo, Japan, in 1984 by Chizuo Matsumoto (1955–2018), who adopted the religious name Shoko Asahara. The organization originated as a small commercial yoga and meditation school called Aum Shinsen no Kai before reorganizing in 1987 under the name Aum Shinrikyo, obtaining legal status as a recognized religious corporation from the Tokyo metropolitan government in 1989. While based geographically in Japan, the movement is classified within the Dharmic cluster in comparative scholarship and this atlas because its core theological framework, nomenclature, initiatory ladder, and soteriological vocabulary were directly derived from esoteric Vajrayāna Buddhism, Hindu Śaivism, and classical yogic asceticism, rather than native Japanese Shinto.

In February 1990, Asahara and twenty-four senior disciples ran for the Japanese House of Representatives under the banner of the Shinrītō ("Truth Party"), suffering an overwhelming electoral defeat. Interpreting this rejection as evidence that the outside world was irredeemably corrupted, the leadership embraced an aggressive, conspiratorial millenarianism, predicting that global nuclear and chemical Armageddon would occur by the late 1990s. The group embarked on a massive, covert weapons development program across laboratories in Japan and a pastoral property in Western Australia, producing industrial quantities of the chemical nerve agents sarin, tabun, and VX, biological agents including anthrax and botulinum toxin, and an automated assembly line for Kalashnikov assault rifles.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Aum's theology represented a lethal fusion of esoteric Buddhism, yogic subtle anatomy, Hindu iconography (especially the worship of Śiva as the destroyer god), the prophecies of Nostradamus, and the Christian Book of Revelation. Asahara claimed to possess the power to transfer spiritual energy directly (*śaktipāta*) and claimed to have achieved final enlightenment, identifying himself simultaneously as the Buddha, the Lamb of God, and the only savior capable of taking on the negative karma of humanity.

Central to Aum's moral rationalization for violence was the distortion of the Tibetan Buddhist tantric concept of *phowa* (transference of consciousness at the moment of death), rendered in Japanese as *poa*. Asahara taught that enlightened masters had the spiritual authority and moral obligation to "poa" individuals who were accumulating heavy negative karma—meaning to terminate their physical lives so their souls could be redirected to a higher celestial realm before descending into hell. This doctrine was invoked to justify the execution of defectors, political opponents, and the general public.

## Documented harms and controversies

Between 1989 and 1995, Aum Shinrikyo carried out numerous assassinations, murders, and mass-casualty chemical attacks:
- **Sakamoto Family Murders (November 1989)**: Sect members broke into the Yokohama apartment of anti-cult attorney Tsutsumi Sakamoto, who was preparing a civil class-action lawsuit against Aum, strangling Sakamoto, his wife Satoko, and their one-year-old son Tatsuhiko to death, burying their bodies in remote mountain forests.
- **Matsumoto Sarin Attack (27 June 1994)**: Operatives used a converted refrigerator truck to disperse aerosolized sarin gas in a residential neighborhood of Matsumoto, Nagano Prefecture, aiming to assassinate three judges presiding over a real-estate dispute involving the sect. The attack killed eight people and injured more than five hundred.
- **Tokyo Subway Sarin Attack (20 March 1995)**: Five coordinated teams carried double-layered plastic pouches containing liquid sarin onto five trains across the Tokyo subway system during morning rush hour, puncturing the bags with sharpened umbrella tips. The attack killed thirteen people, severely injured dozens, and caused temporary vision damage and respiratory distress to more than 5,800 commuters.

Following nationwide raids on Aum facilities, Japanese police arrested over four hundred members. Shoko Asahara and twelve senior disciples were convicted of multiple counts of murder and conspiracy; all thirteen were executed by hanging at Japanese detention centers in July 2018.

The movement retains living adherents in Japan and Russia under close statutory surveillance by Japan's Public Security Intelligence Agency. It operates primarily as two splinter groups:
1. **Aleph**: Formed in 2000 under Fumihiro Joyu before he departed; continues to maintain clandestine reverence for Asahara's teachings and remains subject to strict regulatory restrictions under anti-Aum legislation.
2. **Hikari no Wa** ("Circle of Rainbow Light"): Founded in 2007 by Fumihiro Joyu; has formally repudiated Asahara, removed all his portraits and texts, and reframed itself as an open philosophical association, though it also remains under regular governmental inspection.
