# Jonang

## Historical context

Jonang institutions developed in Tibet from the thirteenth century, with Dolpopa Sherab Gyaltsen and Tāranātha among their major authors. Seventeenth-century political changes converted many central Tibetan monasteries to Gelug control, leading outsiders to presume the school extinct. Jonang communities nevertheless continued in eastern Tibet and have gained wider recognition in recent decades.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Jonang preserves a complete Kālacakra system and teaches *shentong*, or “other-emptiness,” describing ultimate awakened qualities as empty of adventitious defilement rather than empty of their own nature. Critics and Jonang authors interpret this language differently. The debate belongs within Tibetan Madhyamaka and buddha-nature discourse, not a simple contrast between belief and nihilism.
