# Mahāyāna Buddhism

## Historical context

Mahāyāna texts and communities became visible in India around the beginning of the common era, developing within existing monasteries rather than immediately forming a separate ordination. New sūtras circulated alongside Madhyamaka, Yogācāra, devotional, ritual, and cosmological traditions. Translation carried these materials throughout Central and East Asia.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The bodhisattva vow joins liberation with commitment to all beings. Emptiness, skillful means, buddha-fields, compassion, and multiple celestial buddhas receive varying emphasis. Mahāyāna is not one school: Chan, Pure Land, Tiantai, Nichiren, and Tibetan traditions select different texts and practices. The label “greater vehicle” is an internal ideal and should not be used to demean non-Mahāyāna traditions.
