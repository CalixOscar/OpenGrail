# Sarvāstivāda

## Historical context

Sarvāstivāda monastic and scholastic centers flourished across northern India, Kashmir, Gandhāra, and Central Asia. The Vaibhāṣika synthesis associated with the *Mahāvibhāṣā* developed one of Buddhism’s most detailed Abhidharma systems. Many texts survive through Chinese and Tibetan translation, while related Mūlasarvāstivāda discipline continues in Tibetan ordination.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The school is named for the thesis that conditioned factors in past, present, and future “exist,” a claim designed to explain karma, memory, and temporal continuity without a permanent self. Internal interpretations differed, and critics such as Vasubandhu sharpened later Buddhist philosophy. Sarvāstivāda is institutionally extinct, but its categories remain deeply embedded in East Asian and Tibetan scholastic curricula.
