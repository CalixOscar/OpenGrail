# Vajrayāna Buddhism

## Historical context

Esoteric Buddhist scriptures and ritual systems expanded in India from about the sixth century CE and traveled to Tibet, Nepal, China, Japan, and Southeast Asia. Monasteries and itinerant specialists developed initiatory lineages, mandalas, mantras, and yogic methods within Mahāyāna ethical and philosophical frameworks.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Vajrayāna aims to transform body, speech, and mind by ritually inhabiting awakened form under a qualified teacher. Practices are graduated and traditionally require initiation; public imagery should not be detached from those commitments. Buddhist and Śaiva-Śākta tantric communities shared vocabulary, sites, and techniques, but borrowing was multidirectional and each tradition reworked materials through distinct views of self, deity, and liberation.
