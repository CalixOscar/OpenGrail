# Secular Buddhism

## Historical context

Secular Buddhist networks emerged prominently in Europe, North America, and Australasia from late twentieth-century convert Buddhism, mindfulness culture, historical-critical study, and humanist ethics. Writers and communities differ substantially, so the label describes a family of experiments rather than an institution with a common creed.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Many participants treat rebirth, karma across lives, supernatural beings, and cosmology as optional, symbolic, or historically conditioned while retaining meditation, ethical precepts, community, and dependent arising. Critics argue that this selectively reflects modern Western assumptions; supporters see responsible adaptation. Academic classification can describe these changes without deciding whether they constitute authentic Buddhism, a question communities answer differently.
