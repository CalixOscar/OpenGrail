# Rimé Movement

## Historical context

Rimé, meaning “without boundaries” or nonsectarian, arose especially in nineteenth-century Kham through figures such as Jamgön Kongtrul, Jamyang Khyentse Wangpo, and Chokgyur Dechen Lingpa. They gathered transmissions and texts at risk of disappearing and created large teaching collections. Their work influenced later Tibetan masters in Tibet, exile communities, and global Dharma networks.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Rimé encourages receiving and preserving teachings from multiple schools while practicing each according to its own view and lineage requirements. It was not a project to dissolve Nyingma, Kagyu, Sakya, Gelug, or Bön into a new synthetic church. Modern use of “nonsectarian” sometimes implies generic eclecticism, whereas classical Rimé scholarship often insists on mastering precise differences as a condition for mutual respect.
