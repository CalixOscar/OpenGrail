# Tibetan Buddhism

## Historical context

Buddhism entered the Tibetan Empire through Indian, Nepalese, Chinese, and Central Asian connections. Translation projects produced the Kangyur and Tengyur, while later transmission waves supported Nyingma, Kagyu, Sakya, Gelug, and other lineages. Tibetan institutions also spread through Mongolia, the Himalayas, and Inner Asia.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Monastic discipline, Madhyamaka philosophy, bodhisattva ethics, tantric initiation, deity yoga, debate, and graded-path teaching coexist with lay ritual and pilgrimage. Lineages differ in contemplative systems and authority but share much of their canon. Relations with Bön include competition, exchange, and parallel institutional development; Bön is not accurately described as merely a Buddhist sect.
