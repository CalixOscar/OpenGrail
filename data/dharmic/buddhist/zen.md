# Zen Buddhism

## Historical context

Japanese monks transmitted Chan teachings and institutions in several waves, with Rinzai and Sōtō becoming prominent from the Kamakura period and Ōbaku arriving in the seventeenth century. Zen participated in temple administration, funerary practice, elite culture, local devotion, and modern lay movements; its history extends far beyond meditation halls.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Sōtō commonly emphasizes *shikantaza*, or “just sitting,” while Rinzai curricula often use kōan training, though both encompass ritual, scripture, ethics, and teacher authorization. Premodern temples interacted deeply with kami worship before modern state separation policies. Global “Zen” often foregrounds minimalist aesthetics and personal experience, selectively representing complex Japanese institutions.
