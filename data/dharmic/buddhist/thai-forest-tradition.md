# Thai Forest Tradition

## Historical context

The Kammaṭṭhāna or Thai Forest tradition formed around Ajahn Sao and Ajahn Mun in late nineteenth- and early twentieth-century northeastern Thailand. Its teachers renewed wandering ascetic observances and intensive meditation during a period when state-backed monastic administration was consolidating. Disciples later founded forest monasteries across Thailand and internationally.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Practice emphasizes the Vinaya, selected ascetic observances, mindfulness of the body, concentration, and investigation of impermanence. Charismatic teacher narratives and relic devotion coexist with claims of experiential verification. “Forest” names an ideal and lineage, not a guarantee that every monastery is remote. The tradition is one modern Theravāda formation rather than an unchanged survival from the Buddha’s lifetime.
