# Nichiren Buddhism

## Historical context

Nichiren taught in thirteenth-century Japan amid political instability and competing Buddhist institutions. Drawing on Japanese Tendai training, he identified the *Lotus Sūtra* as the definitive teaching for his age and endured exile and persecution. After his death, disciples formed multiple temple lineages that continue to differ over authority and practice.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Chanting *Namu Myōhō Renge Kyō*, devotion to the *Lotus Sūtra*, and use of a calligraphic *gohonzon* are central. Nichiren connected personal awakening with the welfare of society and offered sharp criticism of rival schools. Modern Nichiren communities range from priestly denominations to lay movements; they should not be treated as one centrally governed organization.
