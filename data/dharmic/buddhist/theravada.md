# Theravāda Buddhism

## Historical context

Theravāda descends from the Sri Lankan Mahāvihāra lineage and became established across Myanmar, Thailand, Laos, and Cambodia through changing networks of ordination and royal patronage. The Pāli canon was transmitted orally before being written, while commentaries associated with Buddhaghosa systematized interpretation. Modern reform and colonial encounter reshaped education and meditation.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Theravāda communities center the Buddha’s teaching, monastic discipline, merit, generosity, ethical conduct, and cultivation of calm and insight. The arhat is a prominent ideal, but devotion, relics, protective rites, and aspirations concerning future buddhas also matter. Calling Theravāda “original Buddhism” erases centuries of development; calling it merely monastic overlooks extensive lay ritual and practice.
