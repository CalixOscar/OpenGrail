# Tiantai Buddhism

## Historical context

Tiantai crystallized around Zhiyi in sixth-century China and took its name from Mount Tiantai. Zhiyi organized the varied Buddhist scriptures translated into Chinese through a comprehensive classification of teachings and practices. The school declined and revived several times while deeply influencing Chan, Pure Land, and Japanese Buddhist formations.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Tiantai gives special interpretive authority to the *Lotus Sūtra* and teaches the mutual inclusion of all phenomena through the “three truths” of emptiness, provisional existence, and the middle. Its *zhiguan* system integrates calming and insight rather than opposing study to meditation. Claims of a uniquely Chinese synthesis describe creative systematization, not a clean break from Indian Mahāyāna sources.
