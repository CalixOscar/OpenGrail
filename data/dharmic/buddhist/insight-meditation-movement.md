# Insight Meditation Movement

## Historical context

Lay-oriented vipassanā methods expanded in colonial and postcolonial Burma through teachers such as Ledi Sayadaw, Mahasi Sayadaw, and U Ba Khin. Asian and Western students carried related techniques into global retreat centers during the later twentieth century. Distinct lineages use different instructions and should not be collapsed into one standardized method.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Programs commonly emphasize systematic mindfulness of changing bodily and mental events, intensive retreat, and practical instruction. Some retain chanting, merit, rebirth, and monastic authority; others translate practice into psychological language. Modern accessibility is a real innovation built from Theravāda sources, not proof that meditation was always the principal practice of most Buddhist laypeople.
