# Kagyu

## Historical context

Kagyu traditions arose from Tibetan reception of Indian tantric transmissions, remembered through the teacher sequence Tilopa, Nāropa, Marpa, Milarepa, and Gampopa. Gampopa joined yogic instruction with Kadam monastic path teaching. Multiple “major” and “minor” Kagyu branches followed; Karma Kagyu became especially prominent through the Karmapa incarnation lineage.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Mahāmudrā meditation and the Six Dharmas of Nāropa are characteristic, alongside monastic study, ritual, and bodhisattva training. Popular images of Kagyu as purely experiential can obscure its substantial textual and institutional learning. Lineage histories authorize practice but are not simple organizational charts: teachers exchanged instructions across Tibetan schools, and sublineages frequently overlap.
