# Dharmaguptaka

## Historical context

The Dharmaguptakas were one of the early Indian Buddhist monastic schools and were active along trade routes into Central Asia. Their scriptures survive largely in Chinese translation and in Gāndhārī manuscript discoveries. Although the school ceased as an independent doctrinal institution, its ordination lineage endured.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Dharmaguptaka collections organize discipline and doctrine somewhat differently from Theravāda and Sarvāstivāda sources. The four-part Vinaya remains authoritative for monastics in Chinese, Korean, and Vietnamese Buddhism. Continuity of this Vinaya does not mean modern East Asian schools preserve every ancient Dharmaguptaka doctrine.
