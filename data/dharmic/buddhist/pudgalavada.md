# Pudgalavāda Schools

## Historical context

Vātsīputrīya and Sāṃmitīya communities became widespread in India and were collectively criticized as Pudgalavādins by rival Buddhists. Few of their own texts survive, so much reconstruction depends on opponents and Chinese translations.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Pudgalavādins posited a “person” that could not be described as either identical with or wholly different from the five aggregates. This was intended to explain moral responsibility, rebirth, and liberation while rejecting an eternal ātman. Rival schools viewed the doctrine as compromising non-self, but modern labels such as “personalist” can overstate resemblance to Western concepts of personhood.
