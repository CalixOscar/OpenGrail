# Won Buddhism

## Historical context

Pak Chungbin, known as Sotaesan, experienced awakening in 1916 and organized a community during Japanese colonial rule. The movement adopted the name Won Buddhism after liberation and developed temples, schools, welfare institutions, and international missions. Its reforms responded to rapid modernization while drawing on Korean Buddhist and broader East Asian ethics.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The *Il-Won-Sang*, a single circle, symbolizes dharma-body Buddha and the interdependent source of all things. Practice joins seated meditation, inquiry, mindful choice in daily activity, and gratitude toward four encompassing beneficences. Won Buddhism identifies as Buddhist while using modern organization and a concise new canon; its Confucian ethical resonances do not make it merely a blended derivative.
