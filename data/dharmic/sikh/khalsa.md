# Khalsa

## Historical context

Guru Gobind Singh inaugurated the Khalsa at Anandpur in 1699 through initiation with *amrit*, amid conflict and community consolidation. Initiates adopted the names Singh or Kaur and a shared discipline. Eighteenth-century Khalsa bands, later Sikh polities, colonial military categories, and modern reform all shaped its public form.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The Khalsa ideal joins devotion with readiness to defend justice and the vulnerable. Initiated Sikhs maintain the five Ks and a code governing worship, conduct, and communal responsibility. Not all Sikhs are formally initiated, but the Khalsa profoundly shapes Sikh identity. Reducing it to a warrior order misses its devotional, ethical, and egalitarian dimensions.
