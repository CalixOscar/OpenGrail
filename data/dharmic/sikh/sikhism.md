# Sikh Tradition

## Historical context

Guru Nanak taught in late fifteenth- and early sixteenth-century Punjab, and nine succeeding human Gurus developed a community, scripture, institutions, and public ethics. Guru Arjan compiled the Ādi Granth; Guru Gobind Singh founded the Khalsa and affirmed the scripture and community as enduring Guru. Sikh history later unfolded through empire, colonialism, reform, partition, and diaspora.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Sikhs worship one ineffable God through remembrance of the divine Name, sung scripture, honest work, sharing, service, and resistance to oppression. The *gurdwara* and *langar* enact congregational equality. Sikh teaching arose amid Hindu bhakti and Islamic-Sufi worlds yet constitutes a self-defined tradition, not a hybrid or branch of either.
