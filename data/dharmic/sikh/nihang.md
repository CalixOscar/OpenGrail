# Nihang Sikhs

## Historical context

Nihang lineages associate their origins with Guru Gobind Singh’s Khalsa and became prominent in the eighteenth-century Dal Khalsa and Sikh misls. They maintained mobile armed orders, guarded shrines, and retained autonomy under changing Sikh, colonial, and postcolonial authorities. Major groupings include the Buddha Dal and Taruna Dal.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Nihangs are recognized by blue dress, high turbans, weapons, equestrian skill, and distinctive recitation and festival practice. Some preserve texts and customs contested by other Sikh institutions, including particular preparations of cannabis and older martial rites. Such differences represent internal Sikh diversity; exoticized “warrior monk” imagery obscures learned, devotional, and custodial roles.
