# Namdhari Sikhs

## Historical context

The Namdhari, also called Kuka, movement took shape in nineteenth-century Punjab under Balak Singh and Ram Singh. It combined religious reform with boycott of some colonial institutions and suffered violent repression in 1872. Community memory holds that Ram Singh survived exile, a claim historians distinguish from documentary conclusions.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Namdharis wear white homespun clothing, practice rigorous vegetarianism, emphasize congregational singing and repetition of the divine Name, and maintain a line of living gurus after Guru Gobind Singh. This distinguishes them from mainstream Sikh teaching that guruship rests eternally in scripture and community. Their anti-colonial role remains important without making the movement solely political.
