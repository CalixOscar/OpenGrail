# Śramaṇa Movements

## Historical context

During the first millennium BCE, renouncers, teachers, and communities in northern India developed alternatives to Vedic sacrifice and householder status. Buddhist and Jain sources preserve debates with Ājīvikas, materialists, skeptics, and other groups, although accounts written by rivals must be used critically.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Śramaṇa is a broad social and historical category, not one religion. Its communities disagreed over whether karma exists, whether action changes destiny, what survives death, and whether knowledge or austerity liberates. Buddhism and Jainism became durable institutional traditions, while several contemporaries disappeared or survive mainly through hostile reports.
