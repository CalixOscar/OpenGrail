# Jainism

## Historical context

Jain communities trace a succession of twenty-four Jinas, with Pārśva and Mahāvīra historically associated with the eastern Gangetic world before and during early Buddhism. Monastic and lay networks spread across India, supported literary cultures in Prakrit, Sanskrit, and regional languages, and developed Digambara and Śvetāmbara formations.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Jain teaching describes living souls bound by karmic matter and liberated through right faith, knowledge, conduct, and severe nonviolence. Ascetics model renunciation; laypeople pursue adapted vows, generosity, pilgrimage, and temple or non-image-based worship. Jainism shares karma and rebirth vocabulary with Buddhist and Hindu traditions but offers a distinct pluralist ontology and should not be treated as a branch of either.
