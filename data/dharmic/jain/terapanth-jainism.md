# Terāpanth Jainism

## Historical context

Ācārya Bhikṣu founded the Śvetāmbara Terāpanth in Rajasthan in 1760 after disagreements over mendicant authority and conduct. The order developed an unusually centralized structure under one ācārya. In the twentieth century Ācārya Tulsi and Ācārya Mahāprajña expanded education, lay ethics, meditation, and international outreach.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Terāpanth rejects image worship in continuity with Sthānakavāsī practice and stresses unified discipline, study, and obedience within the mendicant order. The Anuvrat movement invites people beyond Jainism to undertake small ethical vows, while Preksha meditation presents contemplative training in accessible form. These modern programs adapt Jain nonviolence without replacing the community’s canonical and ascetic foundations.
