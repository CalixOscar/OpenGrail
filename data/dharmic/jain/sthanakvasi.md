# Sthānakavāsī Jainism

## Historical context

Sthānakavāsī formation followed late medieval western Indian critiques associated with Loṅkā Śāh, who argued that the received Āgamas did not authorize image worship. Distinct mendicant lineages emerged over time, gathering in simple halls rather than temple sanctuaries. The movement became influential in Rajasthan, Gujarat, Punjab, and diasporic communities.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Sthānakavāsīs emphasize meditation, preaching, ethical vows, canonical study, and careful nonviolence while rejecting temple images and related offerings. Mendicants may wear a mouth covering as a visible precaution against harming minute life. “Reform” does not mean reduced ritual: disciplined confession, fasting, calendrical observance, and teacher-lineage practice remain substantial.
