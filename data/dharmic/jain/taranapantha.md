# Tāraṇapantha

## Historical context

Tāraṇ Svāmī taught in central India around the late fifteenth and early sixteenth centuries. Communities gathered around his writings and built *chaityālayas* that enshrine books rather than Jina images. The tradition remains regionally concentrated but historically important in Digambara reform.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Tāraṇapanth worship emphasizes knowledge, meditation, ethical discipline, and reverence for texts. Its rejection of image ritual differentiates it from many Digambara temple traditions while leaving it within Jain commitments to the Jinas, karma theory, nonviolence, and liberation.
