# Śvetāmbara Jainism

## Historical context

Śvetāmbara institutions developed principally in western and northern India through mendicant orders, lay patronage, pilgrimage centers, manuscript culture, and scholastic debate. A council remembered at Valabhī is traditionally associated with reducing the transmitted Āgamas to writing, although historians treat details of the canon’s formation cautiously.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Śvetāmbara monks and nuns wear white robes and the tradition accepts that women can attain liberation. Image-worshiping temple communities coexist with Sthānakavāsī and Terāpanth non-image lineages. Differences from Digambara Jainism include scripture, mendicant discipline, and narratives about liberated beings, while core commitments to Jinas, nonviolence, karma, and vows remain shared.
