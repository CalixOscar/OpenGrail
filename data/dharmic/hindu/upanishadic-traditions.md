# Upanishadic Traditions

## Historical context

The oldest Upaniṣads were composed within late Vedic learned communities, beginning roughly in the first millennium BCE. They extend rather than simply reject sacrificial culture, recasting ritual correspondences as inquiry into breath, consciousness, death, rebirth, and the relation between *ātman* and *brahman*. Later Upaniṣads developed sectarian and yogic themes.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

These texts preserve multiple positions rather than one settled doctrine: some emphasize identity between self and ultimate reality, while others retain difference or devotional hierarchy. Their vocabulary became foundational for Vedānta. Parallels with Buddhist debates reflect a shared North Indian intellectual environment, but Buddhism famously contests any permanent self; similarity does not establish doctrinal identity.
