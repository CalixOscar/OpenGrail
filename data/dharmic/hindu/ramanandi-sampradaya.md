# Rāmānandī Sampradāya

## Historical context

Rāmānandī communities emerged in late medieval North India around memories of the teacher Rāmānanda and devotion to Rāma. Monastic orders, renouncers, temples, pilgrimage centers, and lay devotees made it one of the region's largest Vaiṣṇava formations. Later genealogies connect Rāmānanda to Rāmānuja, though historians debate the exact institutional continuity.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Rāma, Sītā, and Hanumān anchor worship, recitation, pilgrimage, and ascetic discipline. Rāmānandīs include both armed *nāgā* renouncers and household communities, and they have negotiated caste inclusion in different ways. The broad network should not be treated as a single centralized denomination.
