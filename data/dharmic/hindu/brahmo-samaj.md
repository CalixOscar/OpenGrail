# Brahmo Samaj

## Historical context

Rammohun Roy established the Brahmo Sabha in Calcutta in 1828; Debendranath Tagore, Keshab Chandra Sen, and later leaders reshaped it through successive organizations and schisms. The movement arose amid Bengali intellectual change, colonial institutions, Christian and Islamic encounter, and debates over Hindu scripture and social practice.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Brahmos advocate worship of one formless God, ethical reform, and reasoned engagement with scripture, especially the Upaniṣads. Many rejected image worship, caste restrictions, and practices they regarded as harmful, while supporting women’s education and legal reform. Christian Unitarian contact mattered, but Brahmo theology was not simply imported Christianity; participants creatively reframed South Asian sources within a modern global religious conversation.
