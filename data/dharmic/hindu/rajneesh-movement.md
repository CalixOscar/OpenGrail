# Rajneesh Movement (Osho)

## Historical context

The Rajneesh movement was initiated by Chandra Mohan Jain (1931–1990), a former professor of philosophy at the University of Jabalpur who gained national attention across India during the 1960s as Acharya Rajneesh. Lecturing against religious dogmatism, political corruption, and sexual puritanism (most notably in his provocative 1968 lectures compiled as *From Sex to Superconsciousness*), Rajneesh attracted both passionate followers and fierce public criticism. In September 1970, he initiated his first disciples into *neo-sannyas* in Bombay, transforming the classical Hindu ascetic ideal of world-renunciation into a life-affirming path where disciples wore ochre or red garments and a wooden-bead *mālā* with his portrait while remaining fully engaged in worldly careers.

In 1974, the movement relocated to the Koregaon Park district in Pune (Poona), establishing an ashram that quickly evolved into a global destination for hundreds of thousands of Western seekers, therapists, and intellectuals. The ashram became renowned for synthesizing traditional Eastern meditation with Western humanistic psychology, gestalt therapy, bioenergetics, and Encounter groups. In 1981, seeking to build a self-sustaining communal society and facing mounting tax disputes in India, the movement purchased the 64,000-acre Big Muddy Ranch in Wasco County, Oregon, constructing the intentional city of Rajneeshpuram. After the dramatic collapse of the Oregon commune in 1985 and his deportation from the United States, Rajneesh returned to Pune in 1987. In 1989, he adopted the name "Osho" (derived from the Japanese term *oshō*, denoting a spiritual master, as well as William James's term "oceanic"). Following his death in 1990, the Pune property was reorganized as the Osho International Meditation Resort, which continues to operate as an international center for active meditation and personal growth.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Rajneesh's central philosophy was embodied in his concept of "Zorba the Buddha"—an ideal human being who rejects the false dichotomy between earthly pleasure and spiritual transcendence, combining the sensual joy, dance, and earthiness of Nikos Kazantzakis's Zorba the Greek with the silent stillness and enlightenment of Gautama Buddha.

Because modern people carry deep psychological tensions and inhibitions, Rajneesh argued that classical silent meditation techniques (such as Vipassanā or Zazen) were ineffective without prior emotional purgation. He developed several "Active Meditations," most famously *Osho Dynamic Meditation*, a five-stage morning practice consisting of chaotic breathing, cathartic emotional release (shouting, weeping, laughing), jumping with arms raised while chanting the mantra *Hoo*, a sudden freeze in complete stillness, and celebratory dance.

## Documented harms and controversies

The rapid expansion of Rajneeshpuram in rural Oregon triggered intense political, legal, and cultural confrontations with local ranchers, county commissioners, and state officials over land-use regulations and municipal incorporation. To influence the outcome of the November 1984 Wasco County elections, the commune's leadership circle—commanded by Rajneesh's personal secretary, Ma Anand Sheela (Sheela Silverman)—orchestrated the 1984 Rajneeshee bioterror attack.

In September 1984, commune operatives deliberately sprayed liquid cultures of *Salmonella enterica* Typhimurium onto salad bars in ten restaurants in The Dalles, Oregon. The attack sickened 751 people, resulting in forty-five hospitalizations, and remains the largest biological terror attack in United States history. Subsequent state and federal investigations uncovered that the inner circle had also installed a vast illegal wiretapping network intercepting thousands of phone calls at the commune and public agencies, established a secret toxicology lab, and conspired to assassinate Charles Turner, the United States Attorney for the District of Oregon.

In 1985, Sheela and several top lieutenants fled to Europe, were extradited to the United States, and in 1986 entered guilty pleas to charges of conspiracy, attempted murder, first-degree assault, unauthorized wiretapping, and immigration fraud; Sheela served twenty-nine months in federal prison. Bhagwan Shree Rajneesh was arrested in North Carolina aboard a chartered jet in October 1985, indicted on thirty-five federal counts of immigration fraud (involving orchestrated sham marriages between foreign disciples and American citizens), pleaded guilty to two felony counts of making false statements to federal officials, paid a $400,000 fine, and agreed to leave the United States under an order of deportation.
