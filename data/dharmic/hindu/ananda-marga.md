# Ananda Marga

## Historical context

Ananda Marga ("Path of Bliss," officially *Ananda Marga Pracaraka Samgha* or AMPS) was founded on 9 January 1955 in Jamalpur, Bihar, India, by Prabhat Ranjan Sarkar (1921–1990), known to his disciples as Shrii Shrii Anandamurti. Sarkar, then an employee of the Indian Railways accounts division, began initiating disciples into a systematic regime of Tantric meditation and moral discipline. In 1959, Sarkar formulated PROUT (Progressive Utilization Theory), a comprehensive socio-economic doctrine intended to restructure human civilization around universal humanism, economic self-reliance, and spiritual values.

During the 1960s and 1970s, the movement expanded rapidly throughout India and internationally, establishing an orange-robed monastic order of whole-time spiritual teachers (*ācāryas* and *avadhūtas*) and founding AMURT (Ananda Marga Universal Relief Team) for global disaster relief and humanitarian assistance. Ananda Marga encountered severe political conflict with the government of India; during the national Emergency declared by Prime Minister Indira Gandhi in 1975, the organization was banned and Sarkar was imprisoned on murder conspiracy charges. Sarkar conducted a five-year liquid-only fast in prison protesting conditions before being fully acquitted of all charges by the Patna High Court in 1978. In West Bengal, intense ideological friction with the governing Communist Party of India (Marxist) culminated in the 1982 Bijon Setu massacre, in which seventeen Ananda Marga monks and nuns were lynched by a mob in Kolkata. Today, Ananda Marga operates schools, medical clinics, orphanages, and meditation centers in more than 120 countries.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Ananda Marga's philosophy uniquely binds individual mystical transcendence with collective social duty. Its spiritual practice is classified as *Rājadhi-rāja Yoga*, an advanced system of Tantric meditation comprised of six distinct lessons taught privately by ordained *ācāryas*, emphasizing withdrawal of the mind (*pratyāhāra*), concentration (*dhāraṇā*), and cosmic ideation (*dhyāna*). 

The movement is characterized by distinctive ritual and physical practices:
- **Bābā Nām Kevalam**: An eight-syllable universal mantra ("Love is the essence of all things") sung during *kīrtana* chanting with arms raised above the head.
- **Tāṇḍava**: An energetic, ancient warrior dance performed exclusively by initiated practitioners holding a dagger or skull, symbolizing the conquest of fear, weakness, and physical mortality.
- **Kaoshikii**: A rhythmic, twenty-two-step psycho-physical dance designed to stimulate the glandular and endocrine systems, develop mental agility, and overcome lethargy.

Sarkar's socio-economic philosophy, **PROUT**, rejects both state-controlled communism and unregulated capitalism, advocating for a decentralized three-tiered economy: small private enterprises for non-essential goods, worker-owned producer cooperatives for consumer and agricultural goods, and public utilities for key heavy industries. PROUT guarantees the basic necessities of life (food, clothing, shelter, education, and medical care) to all citizens as a constitutional right, demanding that political and moral direction be guided by *sadvipras*—morally dedicated, spiritually integrated individuals whose lives are devoted to selfless service.
