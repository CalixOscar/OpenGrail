# Śuddhādvaita Vedānta

## Historical context

Vallabha articulated Śuddhādvaita in fifteenth- and sixteenth-century North India through commentaries on the *Brahma Sūtras* and *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*. His teaching supplied the theological foundation for Puṣṭimārga and continued through hereditary teachers and scholastic interpretation.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Śuddhādvaita identifies Brahman with the personal, blissful Kṛṣṇa while affirming the reality of the universe as divine manifestation. Bondage results from obscured awareness rather than an unreal world, and grace-filled devotion restores participation in divine play. It differs from Śaṅkara's Advaita and from dualist Vedānta without fitting a simple midpoint between them.
