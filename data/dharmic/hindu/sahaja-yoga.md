# Sahaja Yoga

## Historical context

Sahaja Yoga was founded on 5 May 1970 in the coastal village of Nargol, Gujarat, India, by Nirmala Srivastava (1923–2011), known to her followers as H.H. Shri Mataji Nirmala Devi. Born into a Christian family in Madhya Pradesh and active in her youth in the Indian independence movement alongside Mahatma Gandhi, Srivastava sought to develop a universal spiritual technique that would enable ordinary householders to achieve spontaneous spiritual awakening without rigorous asceticism, severe austerities, or financial payment.

Beginning in Mumbai and Delhi, Srivastava traveled extensively throughout the 1970s and 1980s across India, the United Kingdom, Europe, North America, and Australia, conferring *en masse* "Self-Realization" upon public audiences. The movement established international non-profit organizations, including the Vishwa Nirmala Dharma, and built educational institutions, medical research clinics, and cultural centers worldwide. Sahaja Yoga maintains a strict policy that instruction in its meditation and energetic techniques must be provided entirely free of charge, rejecting the commercialization of spiritual knowledge.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The word *sahaja* (Sanskrit for "spontaneous," "natural," or "born together") indicates the movement's central premise: that the potential for spiritual realization is an inborn biological and spiritual capacity within every individual. 

The movement's core practice revolves around the awakening of the *Kuṇḍalinī*, understood as a maternal, primordial spiritual energy residing in three-and-a-half coils within the triangular sacrum bone. When awakened through the presence or photograph of Shri Mataji, the Kuṇḍalinī ascends through the central energy channel (*suṣumṇā nāḍī*), balancing the left sympathetic channel (associated with emotion and past desires) and the right sympathetic channel (associated with physical action and mental planning). As the energy crosses the crown *cakra* (*sahasrāra*), practitioners report feeling a cool breeze (*caitanya*) emitting from the palms of their hands and the fontanelle area, entering a state termed *nirvicāra samādhi* (thoughtless awareness)—a meditative condition in which inner mental chatter ceases while alert, witness consciousness is maintained.

Sahaja Yoga integrates elements from multiple religious traditions, interpreting Christ, the Buddha, Krishna, Rama, and Muhammad as divine incarnations governing specific *cakras* within the universal human subtle body.
