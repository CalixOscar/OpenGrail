# Śāktism

## Historical context

Goddess worship has deep and varied South Asian histories. A recognizably Śākta synthesis appears in the *Devī Māhātmya* and later Purāṇas and Tantras, where multiple regional goddesses are identified with an encompassing Devī. Court, village, temple, household, and ascetic settings supported distinct forms across the subcontinent.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Śakti is divine power and may be worshiped as Durgā, Kālī, Lalitā, Lakṣmī, or other forms. Ritual ranges from public festivals and domestic offerings to initiated mantra, visualization, and subtle-body practice. Śākta and Śaiva tantras frequently share cosmology and liturgy, while differing in emphasis. Sensational portrayals of tantra obscure the ethical, contemplative, and highly regulated character of most lineages.
