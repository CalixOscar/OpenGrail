# Sathya Sai Baba Movement

## Historical context

The Sathya Sai Baba Movement originated in the rural village of Puttaparthi in the Anantapur district of Andhra Pradesh, India. On 20 October 1940, at the age of fourteen, Sathyanarayana Raju (1926–2011) announced to his family that he was no longer their son but the reincarnation of Shirdi Sai Baba (d. 1918), a revered Maharashtrian holy man whose eclectic teachings had bridged Hindu and Muslim devotionalism. Adopting the name Sathya Sai Baba, he gathered a dedicated following that expanded over several decades from a local rural following into a worldwide devotional network with millions of adherents.

His ashram in Puttaparthi, *Prasanthi Nilayam* ("Abode of Supreme Peace"), established in 1950, grew into a self-contained city featuring an international airport, educational campuses, and research institutions. Through the Sri Sathya Sai Central Trust and the Sri Sathya Sai Seva Organisation, the movement funded major philanthropic enterprises, including massive rural drinking-water projects serving millions across Andhra Pradesh, multiple state-of-the-art super-specialty hospitals offering free surgical care, and a nationwide system of tuition-free schools and universities. Following his death in April 2011, Sathya Sai Baba was accorded a full state funeral by the Government of India, attended by senior political leaders and millions of mourners.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The movement's theology combines orthodox Bhakti devotionalism with Advaitic philosophical concepts, teaching that all religions are valid paths leading to the same singular divine truth. Devotional gatherings across thousands of global Sai Centers focus on *bhajans* (call-and-response devotional singing in multiple languages), study circles, and community service projects (*sevā*).

A defining characteristic of Sathya Sai Baba's public career was the daily performance of miracles, most notably the materialization of sacred ash (*vibhūti*), jewelry, watches, and holy amulets from his empty hands, as well as the manifestation of *lingams* from his mouth during the festival of Mahashivaratri. Within the movement, these phenomena are regarded as theological claims of divine omnipotence (*līlā*) intended to inspire spiritual faith. Conversely, rationalists and skeptical investigators (such as B. Premanand and the Committee for the Scientific Investigation of Claims of the Paranormal) characterized the demonstrations as stage magic and sleight of hand.

During the late 1990s and 2000s, several former devotees published allegations of financial impropriety and sexual misconduct against Sathya Sai Baba, which were reported in international media and documentary broadcasts. These allegations were never tested in a court of law, no criminal charges were ever filed by Indian or international law enforcement authorities, and Sathya Sai Baba consistently dismissed the claims as false attacks on his charitable mission.
