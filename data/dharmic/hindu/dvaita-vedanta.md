# Dvaita Vedānta

## Historical context

Madhva established Dvaita in thirteenth-century Karnataka, grounding his interpretation of the Vedānta canon in devotion to Viṣṇu. Monastic centers at Udupi and a substantial commentarial tradition preserved and elaborated his teachings. Later Haridāsa poet-saints helped carry related devotional ideas into Kannada religious culture.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Dvaita treats God, individual selves, and matter as genuinely and eternally distinct, while maintaining their complete dependence on Viṣṇu. Liberation is communion with God rather than identity with an impersonal absolute. Its account of graded souls and divine grace differs from both Advaita nonduality and Rāmānuja’s qualified unity. “Dualist” here describes a sophisticated plural realism, not a simple opposition between spirit and matter.
