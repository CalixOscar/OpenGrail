# Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta

## Historical context

Viśiṣṭādvaita was systematized by Rāmānuja in the eleventh and twelfth centuries within the Śrīvaiṣṇava world of South India. His Sanskrit commentaries interpreted Vedānta through worship of Nārāyaṇa and interacted with Tamil Āḻvār devotion, temple institutions, and earlier theologians such as Yāmuna.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The school describes reality as a qualified unity: conscious selves and matter are real, distinct, and wholly dependent on brahman, often likened to God's body. Loving devotion and divine grace accompany knowledge and disciplined action. Unlike Advaita, liberation does not erase personal distinction; unlike Dvaita, all beings form an organic unity in God. Later Śrīvaiṣṇava sublineages debated how grace and human response cooperate.
