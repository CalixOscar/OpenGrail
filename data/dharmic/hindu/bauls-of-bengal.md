# Bauls of Bengal

## Historical context

The Bauls (from Sanskrit *vātula*, "mad" or "wind-struck," or *vyākula*, "restless with divine longing") are an itinerant mystical and musical community indigenous to the Bengal region, encompassing present-day Bangladesh and the Indian state of West Bengal. The tradition emerged in its recognizable form during the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries CE as an organic rural synthesis of earlier Buddhist Sahajiyā esotericism, Tantric Śāktism, Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava devotionalism, and Chishti and Qadiri Sufi mysticism.

The most celebrated figure in the tradition is Lalon Shah (Lalon Fakir, c. 1774–1890) of Chheuriya in the Kushtia district (now in Bangladesh), whose thousands of composed oral songs crystallized Baul philosophical humanism. Lalon systematically rejected caste labels, sectarian religious boundaries, and scriptural literalism, famously singing: "Everyone asks, 'What is Lalon's faith?' / Lalon says, 'I have never seen what religion looks like with my eyes.'" In the twentieth century, Rabindranath Tagore brought international attention to the Bauls, citing their poetry as a primary inspiration for his own literary and spiritual vision. In 2005, UNESCO inscribed Baul songs on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, recognizing the tradition as a masterpiece of oral and intangible cultural heritage.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Baul philosophy is defined by *dehatattva* ("the truth of the body"), the conviction that the physical human body is a microcosm containing the entire universe and the seat of all divine reality. The divine is personified not as an omnipotent transcendent ruler, but as the intimate *Maner Mănuṣ* ("The Man of the Heart") or *Sahaja Mănuṣ* ("The Natural Man"). Consequently, Bauls reject institutionalized places of worship, idol worship, pilgrimages, and the authority of orthodox clerics—both Brahmanical priests and Islamic qadis.

The inner spiritual discipline of initiated Baul couples involves an esoteric yogic and tantric practice known as *sādhanā*. Rather than advocating world-denying asceticism, the tradition works directly with physical embodiment, emotion, and vital energies. Under the strict guidance of a realized guru or *murshid*, practitioners engage in specialized breath control (*prāṇāyāma*), mental concentration, and controlled ritual union directed toward redirecting and sublimating sexual and emotional energies inward and upward toward the crown of the head. This practice is pursued not for mundane pleasure, but to attain a state of effortless equilibrium (*sahaja*) and direct mystical union with the divine dwelling within the practitioner.
