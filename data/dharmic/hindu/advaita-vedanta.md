# Advaita Vedānta

## Historical context

Advaita emerged through early Vedānta interpretation and became especially influential through Śaṅkara and later monastic teaching lineages. Śaṅkara’s eighth-century commentaries defended a nondual reading of the shared Vedānta canon while disputing Buddhist, Mīmāṃsā, Sāṃkhya, and realist accounts. Later authors refined its theories of error, appearance, and contemplative discipline.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Advaita holds that *brahman* alone is ultimately nondual and that ignorance makes the self appear limited and separate. Liberation arises through disciplined hearing, reflection, and realization, not merely intellectual assent. Comparisons with Mahāyāna emptiness are historically important but contested: Advaita affirms brahman and self in ways Buddhist philosophers generally reject.
