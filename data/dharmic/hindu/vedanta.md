# Vedānta

## Historical context

Vedānta, literally the “end of the Veda,” grew from systematic interpretation of the Upaniṣads. Bādarāyaṇa’s *Brahma Sūtras* organized disputed teachings, and later commentators read this terse work alongside the Upaniṣads and *Bhagavad Gītā*. Monastic, temple, and scholastic lineages developed sharply different accounts of their shared canon.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Vedānta asks how ultimate reality, individual selves, the world, action, devotion, and liberation are related. Advaita teaches nonduality; Viśiṣṭādvaita qualified nonduality; Dvaita enduring difference. “Vedānta” therefore names a field of argument, not a single metaphysics. Its thinkers debated Buddhists, Mīmāṃsakas, and other Hindu schools while also absorbing devotional practices and sectarian theologies.
