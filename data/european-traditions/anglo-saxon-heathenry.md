# Anglo-Saxon Heathenry

## Modern formation

Anglo-Saxon-focused Heathen practice developed within the wider twentieth-century Germanic pagan revival. Practitioners work with archaeology, Old English, place-names, charms, poetry, comparative Germanic material, and contemporary ritual experimentation. Some prefer the term *Fyrnsidu*, “old custom,” while terminology and organization vary widely.

## Reconstruction and distinction

The surviving Anglo-Saxon archive was mostly written by Christians and does not provide a complete pagan liturgy. Comparisons with Norse sources help fill patterns but cannot make Anglo-Saxon gods and practices identical to later Icelandic material. This branch is a modern reconstruction within Heathenry, not a claim that a continuous institutional religion survived England’s conversion.
