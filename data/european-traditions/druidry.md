# Modern Druidry

## Historical context

Modern Druidry grew from eighteenth-century British antiquarianism, fraternal societies, Welsh cultural revival, Romantic nature spirituality, and later Pagan movements. Early revivalists had limited archaeological knowledge and sometimes treated creative literary constructions as ancient survivals. Twentieth- and twenty-first-century orders developed distinct initiatory, ceremonial, ecological, and devotional paths in Britain and internationally.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Seasonal ceremony, poetry, meditation, sacred landscape, ancestry, and service to community or environment often matter more than fixed theology. Some Druids are polytheists, others pantheists, Christians, or religious naturalists. Ancient Celtic sources inspire the movement, but no evidence supports unbroken institutional descent from Iron Age Druids. The influence edge preserves real reception while distinguishing modern creativity from ancient reconstruction.
