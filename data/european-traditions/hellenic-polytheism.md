# Hellenic Polytheism

## Historical context

Contemporary Hellenic polytheism developed through twentieth-century pagan revival, Greek cultural organizations, diaspora communities, and online networks. Practitioners reconstruct or renew devotion to Greek gods using ancient literature, archaeology, inscriptions, and modern ritual judgment. Organizations differ over terminology, historical reconstruction, ethnicity, and relations with the Greek state and Orthodox majority.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Offerings, hymns, household worship, festival calendars, reciprocity, and virtues such as hospitality commonly shape practice. No modern group can reproduce an ancient polis with its civic institutions, slavery, or social hierarchy; renewal necessarily selects and transforms. The branch edge denotes explicit source orientation, not uninterrupted organizational survival. Hellenic polytheism overlaps with broader Neopaganism but some practitioners reject that label in favor of specifically Hellenic identity.
