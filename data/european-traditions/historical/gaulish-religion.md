# Gaulish Religions

## Historical setting

Celtic-speaking communities across Iron Age Gaul worshiped through sanctuaries, feasts, vows, deposits, processions, and local political institutions. Roman conquest produced abundant bilingual or Latin inscriptions pairing indigenous divine names with Roman titles. Gods such as Epona, Cernunnos, and regional forms of Lugus or healing powers appear unevenly rather than as one national pantheon.

## Scope and interpretation

The plural “religions” emphasizes local variation across centuries and territories. Interpretatio Romana was an active process of translation, patronage, and institutional change, not proof that paired deities were originally identical. Later Irish and Welsh literature can aid comparison only when its different language, date, and Christian transmission are kept in view.
