# Rodnovery

## Historical context

Rodnovery, or Slavic Native Faith, comprises modern movements that expanded especially after the late Soviet period across Russia, Ukraine, Poland, and other Slavic countries and diasporas. Practitioners draw on folklore, archaeology, chronicles, linguistic reconstruction, ecological thought, and new revelation. Organizations vary widely, and no common council determines authentic doctrine or ritual.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Seasonal festivals, ancestors, land, household practice, and gods such as Perun or Veles may be central. The modern archive is fragmentary, so reconstruction and innovation are inseparable. Some communities promote pluralist or ecological identities; others fuse religion with exclusionary ethnonationalism. Scholarship should neither generalize the latter to every practitioner nor ignore it. The influence relation avoids claiming a continuous pre-Christian institution.
