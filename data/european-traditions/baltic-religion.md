# Baltic Religion

## Historical context

Baltic religion is reconstructed for Lithuanian, Latvian, Old Prussian, and related communities from archaeology, medieval chronicles, early modern reports, language, songs, and later folklore. Christianization unfolded unevenly and did not freeze earlier practice in place. Sources often describe opponents through missionary categories, while folklore reflects both older patterns and centuries of Christian coexistence.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Sacred groves, household and agricultural rites, fire, seasonal songs, ancestors, and powers associated with thunder, sun, fate, and the land recur in regional evidence. Names such as Perkūnas or Saule do not imply one standardized Baltic pantheon. Indo-European comparison can clarify linguistic relationships but cannot reconstruct whole rituals automatically. Modern Romuva and Dievturība draw selectively on these archives and living folk culture.
