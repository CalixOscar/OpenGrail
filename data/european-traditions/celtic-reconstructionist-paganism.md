# Celtic Reconstructionist Paganism

## Modern formation

Celtic Reconstructionist approaches emerged in the late twentieth century among polytheists dissatisfied with generalized “Celtic” spirituality. Practitioners emphasize particular languages and cultures, historical criticism, folklore, community ethics, and reciprocal relationships with deities, ancestors, and places. The movement is decentralized and contains Gaelic, Brythonic, and other regional orientations.

## Reconstruction and distinction

Reconstruction means building viable present practice from incomplete archives; it is not proof of an unbroken pre-Christian priesthood. Celtic Reconstructionism overlaps with some forms of modern Druidry but is not identical to them, often placing more weight on polytheism and cultural specificity. Communities debate accessibility, diaspora identity, language learning, and responsible engagement with living Celtic cultures.
