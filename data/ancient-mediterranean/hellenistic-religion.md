# Hellenistic Religion

## Historical context

After Alexander's conquests, Greek-speaking kingdoms connected Egypt, western Asia, and the eastern Mediterranean through new cities, courts, armies, and trade. Religious change did not erase local traditions. Instead, cults traveled, divine names were translated, rulers received honors, and sanctuaries negotiated several cultural vocabularies. “Hellenistic religion” therefore names a historical environment rather than one faith.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Associations, healing sanctuaries, household cult, oracles, royal ritual, and initiatory groups offered overlapping forms of belonging. Deities such as Sarapis demonstrate institutional synthesis, while Isis retained multiple Egyptian and Mediterranean meanings. Astrological and philosophical cosmologies also circulated widely. Syncretism should describe specific acts of identification and adaptation, not assume that all gods became interchangeable or that local communities passively accepted Greek forms.
