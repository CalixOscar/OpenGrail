# Ancient Greek Religion

## Historical context

Ancient Greek religion encompassed changing practices from the post-Mycenaean world through the Hellenistic and Roman periods. Independent poleis organized distinctive calendars, priesthoods, sacrifices, festivals, and patron deities, while pan-Hellenic sanctuaries connected wider networks. Epic and drama preserve influential divine narratives but were never a binding creed, and local inscriptions often reveal practices missing from literary sources.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Reciprocity with gods, ritual purity, household observance, divination, hero cult, and care for the dead structured religious life. Zeus, Athena, Apollo, Demeter, Dionysus, and other gods appeared under many local epithets. Philosophy could criticize or reinterpret myth without simply replacing cult. Modern “Greek mythology” collections can obscure this lived variety and should not be treated as an ancient canonical system.
