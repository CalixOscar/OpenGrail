# Neoplatonism

## Historical context

“Neoplatonism” names several late antique philosophical lineages beginning conventionally with Plotinus and continuing through Porphyry, Iamblichus, Proclus, Damascius, and others. These thinkers understood themselves as Platonists, not founders of a new religion. Their schools operated amid Greek, Roman, Egyptian, Jewish, and Christian religious worlds and differed over ritual, textual authority, and the structure of reality.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Many systems trace all reality to a first principle, often “the One,” through ordered levels of intellect and soul, with return through virtue and contemplation. Iamblichean and later currents also defended theurgy as divinely given ritual. Neoplatonists criticized some Gnostic myths even while sharing late antique philosophical vocabulary. Later Christian, Islamic, Jewish, and esoteric readers transformed these ideas rather than receiving an unchanged system.
