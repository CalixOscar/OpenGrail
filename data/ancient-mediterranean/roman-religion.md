# Roman Religion

## Historical context

Roman religion developed through household rites, neighborhood associations, civic priesthoods, military practice, and relationships with communities across Italy and the empire. Greek narratives and images became deeply influential, but Roman cult was not simply Greek religion under Latin names. Republican and imperial institutions continually adopted, regulated, translated, or rejected divine powers encountered through conquest and migration.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Correct performance, vows, auspices, sacrifice, festival calendars, and negotiated peace with the gods mattered more than assent to a universal creed. Lares, Penates, ancestors, and household genius stood beside Jupiter, Juno, Mars, and countless local gods. Emperor cult expressed political belonging differently across provinces. “Paganism” was a later umbrella; practitioners normally identified by city, cult, office, or inherited custom rather than one Roman religious membership.
