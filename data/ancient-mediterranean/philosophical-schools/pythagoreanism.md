# Pythagoreanism

## Historical setting

Pythagoras established a community in Croton in southern Italy during the sixth century BCE, but almost no securely contemporary writing survives. Later Pythagoreans developed mathematical, cosmological, musical, ethical, and political teachings, while ancient biographies surrounded the founder with wonder stories. Several revivals make “Pythagoreanism” a family of traditions rather than one unchanged school.

## Religious and philosophical scope

Communal rules, purification, dietary disciplines, reverence for number, and teachings about the soul gave Pythagorean life a religious dimension alongside philosophical inquiry. Similarities with Orphic material are real but their direction and institutional relationship remain disputed. This node does not imply that mathematics itself functioned as a cult or that every doctrine later attributed to Pythagoras is early.
