# Stoicism

## Historical setting

Zeno of Citium taught at the Painted Stoa in Athens around 300 BCE. Cleanthes, Chrysippus, and later Greek and Roman thinkers developed a systematic tradition of logic, physics, and ethics. Most early works survive only in fragments, while Seneca, Epictetus, and Marcus Aurelius offer influential but distinct imperial-era forms.

## Religious and philosophical scope

Stoics described a rational, living cosmos pervaded by divine *logos* or pneuma and emphasized virtue, disciplined judgment, providence, and human kinship. They interpreted traditional gods allegorically while participating in surrounding civic cultures. Stoicism is best classified as a philosophical school with religious cosmology and practices, not as a church or a synonym for emotional suppression.
