# Epicureanism

## Historical setting

Epicurus founded his Athenian school, the Garden, in 306 BCE. Communities preserved letters, maxims, commemorative meals, friendship networks, and a philosophical curriculum over centuries. Lucretius’s Latin poem transmitted Epicurean physics to Roman readers, while hostile opponents also shaped its later reputation.

## Religious and philosophical scope

Epicureans accepted blessed and immortal gods but denied that they created or providentially governed the world. Natural explanations of celestial events and death were meant to free people from fear, enabling modest pleasure, friendship, and tranquility. Calling the school simply atheist or hedonist misses both its theology and its disciplined account of desire; it remained a philosophical community within plural ancient religious societies.
