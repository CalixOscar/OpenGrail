# Shamanic Traditions

## Historical context

The word *shaman* entered European scholarship through Tungusic-speaking communities of Siberia and was later extended to ritual specialists around the world. That expansion can enable comparison but has also erased local titles, institutions, and colonial histories. Contemporary “neo-shamanism” is a separate modern phenomenon and should not be silently substituted for Indigenous practice.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Practices called shamanic may include spirit negotiation, healing, divination, soul journey, drumming, possession, or initiation through illness, but no universal checklist applies. Specialists operate within accountable communities and cosmologies. This node represents an analytical family resemblance, not a claim that all ritual mediators descend from one prehistoric religion.
