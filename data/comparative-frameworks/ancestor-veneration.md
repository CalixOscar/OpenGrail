# Ancestor Veneration

## Historical context

Care for the dead appears in archaeological and historical settings across the world, but its meanings differ. Ancestors may protect descendants, authorize land and office, require feeding, judge conduct, or remain present through names, shrines, graves, relics, and collective memory. Not every dead person becomes an ancestor, and not every tradition uses a worship vocabulary.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Offerings, funerary cycles, genealogical recitation, household altars, pilgrimage, and spirit consultation can renew kinship across death. The term is a comparative node rather than a single religion. Parallels between Confucian rites, African lineage traditions, Roman household cults, and Oceanic genealogies do not establish common origin; each must be understood within its own social and theological order.
