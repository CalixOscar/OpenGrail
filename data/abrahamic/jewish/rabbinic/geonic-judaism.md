# Geonic Judaism

## Historical setting

From the early Islamic period into the eleventh century, the heads of the Babylonian academies at Sura and Pumbedita were known as geonim. Jewish communities across North Africa, the Middle East, and Europe sent them questions on law, worship, commerce, and communal governance, creating a transregional responsa network.

## Intellectual legacy

The geonim strengthened the Babylonian Talmud's authority and produced early prayer books, legal monographs, biblical interpretation, and polemics. Saadia Gaon joined rabbinic law with Arabic-language philosophy and grammar. The decline of the academies redistributed learning to regional centers rather than ending the geonic textual legacy.
