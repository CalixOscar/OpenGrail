# Rabbinic Judaism

## Historical context

Rabbinic Judaism developed gradually after the Second Temple's destruction rather than beginning at a single council. Sages in Roman Palestine compiled the Mishnah around 200 CE; later academies produced Palestinian and Babylonian Talmuds through centuries of debate. Rabbinic authority spread unevenly and eventually shaped most Jewish communities, while local customs, philosophy, mysticism, and legal commentary continued to diversify it.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Rabbinic tradition understands written Torah together with an oral interpretive inheritance. Halakhic reasoning, narrative interpretation, prayer, study, household observance, and communal courts translated Temple-centered traditions into diasporic life. Continuity with Pharisaic interpretation is substantial but not a one-to-one institutional succession. Modern Orthodox, Conservative, Reform, and Reconstructionist movements all engage rabbinic sources differently; Karaite Judaism historically disputes the binding authority of oral Torah.
