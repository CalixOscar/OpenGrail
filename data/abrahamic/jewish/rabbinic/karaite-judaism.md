# Karaite Judaism

## Historical context

Karaite movements became visible under the early Islamic caliphates, with important centers in Iraq, Jerusalem, Byzantium, Crimea, and Egypt. Their origins involved multiple scripturalist currents rather than a single uncontested founder. Medieval Karaite scholars produced major works of Hebrew grammar, exegesis, law, and calendar calculation while debating Rabbanite contemporaries. Communities survive principally in Israel and diaspora settings.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Karaites regard the Tanakh as revelation and require interpreters to derive practice from its text rather than accept the rabbinic oral law as intrinsically authoritative. They nonetheless possess inherited interpretive traditions and communal rulings, so “Bible only” can oversimplify their method. Practices concerning calendar, Sabbath, dietary law, and fringes may differ from Rabbanite norms. Karaite identity is fully Jewish and should not be confused with Protestant scripturalism merely because both criticize extra-scriptural authority.
