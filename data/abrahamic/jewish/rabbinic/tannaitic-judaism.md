# Tannaitic Judaism

## Historical setting

The tannaim were rabbinic teachers active mainly in Roman Palestine from the late first through early third centuries CE. In the aftermath of the temple's destruction and the Bar Kokhba revolt, their circles transmitted and debated oral legal traditions concerning ritual, civil life, agriculture, festivals, and purity.

## Textual legacy

The Mishnah, traditionally associated with Judah ha-Nasi around 200 CE, became the foundational collection of tannaitic teaching. The Tosefta and tannaitic midrash preserve parallel and supplementary traditions. Scholars debate how widely early rabbis represented Jewish society, but their literary program became central to later rabbinic Judaism.
