# Ashkenazi Judaism

## Historical development

Ashkenazi Jewish culture formed in medieval communities along the Rhine and later expanded through Central and Eastern Europe. Distinct legal customs, prayer rites, Yiddish culture, educational institutions, and communal structures developed amid migration, persecution, and sustained exchange with surrounding societies.

## Diversity and legacy

Ashkenazi tradition includes pietist, rationalist, kabbalistic, Hasidic, Lithuanian, modern Orthodox, Reform, Conservative, and secular expressions. The term describes a broad historical-cultural complex rather than a single theology. Migration and genocide transformed its centers, while descendants established influential communities in Israel, the Americas, and elsewhere.
