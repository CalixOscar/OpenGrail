# Sephardic Judaism

## Historical development

Sephardic culture flourished among Jews of medieval Iberia through Hebrew poetry, philosophy, law, science, and engagement with Arabic and Romance societies. Expulsions and forced conversions in the late fifteenth century dispersed communities across North Africa, the Ottoman world, Italy, Western Europe, and the Americas.

## Practice and identity

Sephardic communities developed related liturgical rites, legal traditions, and languages including Ladino, while retaining marked regional diversity. “Sephardi” may denote Iberian ancestry, a prayer rite, or broader legal-cultural alignment. It overlaps with but should not simply replace the category Mizrahi, which encompasses many Middle Eastern communities without Iberian descent.
