# Pharisees

## Historical context

Pharisees appear in sources from the Hasmonean and early Roman periods as a movement with influence among parts of the population. Josephus, the New Testament, and later rabbinic literature portray them from different agendas, so reconstruction requires comparison rather than literal harmonization. They survived in memory beyond 70 CE, but their precise organization and social reach remain debated.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Sources associate Pharisees with traditions interpreting written Torah, practices extending purity beyond the Temple, belief in resurrection, and a combination of providence with human responsibility. Gospel disputes reflect close intra-Jewish argument and should not become stereotypes about Judaism as legalistic. Rabbinic Judaism inherited important Pharisaic themes and perhaps personnel, yet historians reject a simple equation between Pharisees and later rabbis.
