# Samaritanism

## Historical context

Samaritans understand themselves as descendants of ancient Israel who preserved worship at Mount Gerizim. Jewish and Samaritan communities developed distinct institutions and competing historical accounts during the Persian and Hellenistic periods, though the chronology of separation is debated. Once numerous, Samaritans survived long periods of imperial rule and persecution; small communities now live chiefly near Nablus and Holon.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The Samaritan Pentateuch is the sole scriptural canon and identifies Gerizim as the chosen sanctuary. Passover sacrifice remains a central communal rite, led with a hereditary priesthood and a calendar distinct from the rabbinic one. Samaritanism is related to Judaism through shared Israelite heritage, but calling it simply a Jewish sect obscures its independent identity.
