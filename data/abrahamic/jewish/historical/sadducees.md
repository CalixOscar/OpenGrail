# Sadducees

## Historical context

Sadducees are known almost entirely through opponents or later observers, including Josephus, New Testament authors, and rabbinic texts. They appear connected with aristocratic and priestly circles during Hasmonean and Roman rule, though neither their membership nor the origin of their name is certain. The destruction of the Jerusalem Temple removed the institution with which they were most closely associated.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Ancient reports say Sadducees rejected resurrection and some Pharisaic traditions, but the claim that they accepted only the Pentateuch cannot be demonstrated securely. Their legal positions survive mainly as contrasts in later literature rather than in their own writings. They should not be portrayed merely as secular collaborators or as a mirror-image denomination. The absence of a surviving Sadducean canon makes the listed texts representative evidence about them, not their authored scriptures.
