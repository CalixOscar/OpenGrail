# Second Temple Judaism

## Historical context

Second Temple Judaism spans Persian, Hellenistic, Hasmonean, and Roman rule from the Temple's restoration to its destruction in 70 CE. Jerusalem sacrifice remained a major focus, but diaspora communities, synagogues, scripture, pilgrimage, legal interpretation, and apocalyptic hope created many forms of Jewish life. Pharisees, Sadducees, Essenes, Jesus movements, revolutionaries, and Jews outside named parties shared some practices while disputing others.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

There was no single Second Temple orthodoxy. Communities differed over calendars, purity, resurrection, priesthood, foreign rule, and which writings carried special authority. The period supplied the immediate matrix for both rabbinic Judaism and Christianity, but neither can be projected backward as its inevitable outcome. “Late Judaism,” once common in older scholarship, misleadingly treats a creative era as merely transitional.
