# Hasidic Judaism

## Historical context

Hasidism arose in eighteenth-century Polish-Lithuanian Jewish society around Israel ben Eliezer, the Baal Shem Tov, and later teachers. It spread through courts led by *tzaddikim* or rebbes and initially faced fierce opposition from other rabbinic elites. The Holocaust destroyed many European centers, but survivors rebuilt dynasties in Israel, North America, and elsewhere.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Hasidic teaching draws heavily on Kabbalah while emphasizing joyful worship, attachment to God, sacred intention, storytelling, and the spiritual leadership of the rebbe. Communities maintain halakhah and distinctive inherited customs, dress, language, and educational structures. Chabad, Breslov, Satmar, Ger, and other groups differ substantially, including over Zionism and outreach. Hasidism is one current within Haredi Orthodoxy, not a synonym for all ultra-Orthodox Jews.
