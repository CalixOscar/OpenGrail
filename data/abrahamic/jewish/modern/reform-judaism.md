# Reform Judaism

## Historical context

Reform Judaism emerged in nineteenth-century German-speaking Europe amid emancipation, Enlightenment thought, and efforts to reshape synagogue worship. It spread especially in North America, where successive platforms revised positions on ritual, peoplehood, Zionism, and tradition. British Liberal and Reform movements developed related but institutionally distinct histories.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Reform communities treat Jewish tradition as historically developing and emphasize ethical obligation, individual informed choice, egalitarian participation, and inclusion. Contemporary Reform has recovered more Hebrew, ritual, and engagement with classical texts than some early reformers favored. It recognizes diverse family structures and ordains women and LGBTQ clergy. Practice remains varied, and describing Reform as simply “less observant” misses its positive theological accounts of autonomy, covenant, justice, and communal responsibility.
