# Haredi Judaism

## Historical development

Haredi communities developed through Orthodox responses to emancipation, secularization, religious reform, and new political movements in nineteenth- and twentieth-century Europe. Hasidic courts, Lithuanian yeshiva traditions, and Sephardic Haredi networks retain distinct institutions while sharing resistance to unbounded religious modernization.

## Diversity and practice

Haredi life commonly centers on halakhic observance, Torah study, dense communal institutions, modest dress, and carefully mediated engagement with wider society. Approaches to employment, technology, general education, and Zionism differ sharply. “Ultra-Orthodox” is common externally, while many communities prefer the Hebrew term *Haredi*.
