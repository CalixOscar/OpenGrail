# Modern Orthodox Judaism

## Historical development

Modern Orthodoxy has roots in nineteenth-century efforts to integrate fidelity to halakha with citizenship and general culture, including Samson Raphael Hirsch's neo-Orthodoxy and later American institutions. Thinkers and schools developed varied syntheses rather than a single founding creed or governing organization.

## Ideas and diversity

Adherents maintain Orthodox law and traditional worship while participating broadly in universities, professions, democratic society, and often religious Zionism. Debates concern biblical scholarship, women's learning and leadership, sexuality, interfaith engagement, and the authority of modern culture. Its boundaries with centrist, open, and Haredi Orthodoxy remain contested.
