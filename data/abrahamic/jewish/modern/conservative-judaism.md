# Conservative Judaism

## Historical context

Conservative Judaism developed from nineteenth-century European Positive-Historical Judaism and took institutional form especially in North America through the Jewish Theological Seminary and Rabbinical Assembly. It positioned itself between Orthodox resistance to some modern changes and Reform's early revisions. The Masorti movement represents related communities outside North America.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The movement treats halakhah as authoritative and evolving through communal interpretation, historical circumstance, and rabbinic decision. Its institutions have adopted changes including egalitarian worship and, in many regions, LGBTQ inclusion, while retaining Hebrew liturgy, Sabbath, festivals, and dietary law as important norms. Congregational practice varies widely. “Conservative” here names conservation of tradition, not a fixed partisan political alignment.
