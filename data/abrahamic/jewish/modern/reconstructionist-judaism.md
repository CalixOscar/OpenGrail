# Reconstructionist Judaism

## Historical context

Reconstructionism began through the thought and institutions of Mordecai Kaplan in twentieth-century American Judaism, first operating within Conservative settings. It established an independent rabbinical college in 1968 and later developed a congregational movement now called Reconstructing Judaism. Its communities have often pioneered egalitarian and participatory practices.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Kaplan described Judaism as the evolving religious civilization of the Jewish people, joining religion with language, art, ethics, memory, and collective life. Reconstructionists generally give tradition “a vote, not a veto,” evaluating inherited practices while creating new liturgy. Views of God range widely, including naturalistic accounts of the power making for salvation. The movement values communal decision and belonging while differing from Humanistic Judaism through its sustained liturgical and textual orientation.
