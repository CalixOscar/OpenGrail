# Islam

## Historical genesis

Islam emerged in western Arabia in the early seventh century CE through revelations Muhammad proclaimed as the speech of God. The Qur'an addresses its audience in conversation with Biblical figures, Arabian religious practices, eschatological preaching, and the wider world of Late Antiquity. Muhammad's migration from Mecca to Medina in 622 marks the beginning of the Islamic calendar and the formation of a community with religious, social, and political dimensions.

After Muhammad's death in 632, disputes over leadership and authority contributed to the eventual formation of Sunni, Shi'a, and other currents. Expanding Muslim-ruled societies brought Arabic-speaking believers into sustained contact with Christians, Jews, Zoroastrians, philosophers, and many local traditions. Qur'anic exegesis, hadith criticism, jurisprudence, theology, philosophy, and Sufism developed distinct methods for negotiating revelation, reason, and inherited practice.

## Ideas and practice

Islam centers the oneness of God (*tawḥīd*), prophecy, revelation, moral accountability, and resurrection. The Qur'an presents Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and other figures as participants in a history of divine guidance while asserting its own corrective and confirmatory role. Common practices include profession of faith, ritual prayer, almsgiving, Ramadan fasting, and pilgrimage to Mecca, though lived expressions vary greatly across societies.
