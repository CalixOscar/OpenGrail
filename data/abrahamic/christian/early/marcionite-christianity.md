# Marcionite Christianity

## Historical development

Marcion of Sinope taught in Rome during the mid-second century and formed communities that spread widely across the Mediterranean and Near East. He distinguished the just creator described in Jewish scripture from the previously unknown good God revealed by Jesus and rejected the Hebrew Bible as Christian scripture.

## Texts and classification

Marcion used an edited Gospel related to Luke and a collection of Pauline letters, making his movement central to debates about early Christian canons. Opponents such as Tertullian preserve much of the evidence, so reconstruction requires caution. Scholars debate whether “Gnostic” is an illuminating label; Marcionite churches were distinctive organizations rather than simply one undifferentiated Gnostic school.
