# Holy Spirit Movement

## Historical context

The Holy Spirit Movement (HSM) was an apocalyptic, spirit-directed military and
religious movement that arose in Acholiland in northern Uganda in August 1986, led by
Alice Auma (1956–2007), a young Acholi Catholic woman. In early 1985, Auma
experienced a spiritual crisis at Paraa in Murchison Falls National Park, where she was
possessed by *Lakwena* ("the Messenger"), who claimed to be the spirit of an Italian
Catholic military officer killed during World War I.

Following the overthrow of the Acholi-dominated military government of Tito Okello by
Yoweri Museveni's National Resistance Army (NRA) in early 1986, northern Uganda was
plunged into severe political alienation, military defeat, and violent reprisals. In
August 1986, Lakwena commanded Auma to cease acting as a traditional healer and
instead form a righteous military force: the Holy Spirit Mobile Forces (HSMF).
Promising to cleanse the Acholi people of the moral pollution and bloodguilt accrued
during decades of civil conflict in the Luwero Triangle, Auma mobilized thousands of
former soldiers, peasants, and youth. Marching south toward Kampala, the HSM won a
series of stunning military engagements against superior government forces. However,
in October and November 1987, after advancing to within fifty miles of Kampala, the
HSM suffered catastrophic artillery defeat in the Magamaga forest near Jinja. Auma
fled on bicycle to Kenya, where she lived in refugee camps until her death in 2007.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The Holy Spirit Movement presented a unique, complex syncretism of Acholi spirit
mediumship (*jok* possession) and Roman Catholic liturgy, morality, and angelology.
Auma was possessed by a pantheon of spirits subordinate to Lakwena, including
"Wrong C.P.O." (an American spirit who directed intelligence), "Ching-Poh" (a Chinese
spirit who controlled logistics), and "Nyaker" (an Acholi maiden who managed
relations with spirits of nature).

To maintain the spiritual purity required for divine invulnerability, combatants had
to adhere strictly to the "Holy Spirit Safety Rules": they were forbidden from taking
cover during battle, looting, smoking, consuming alcohol, engaging in sexual
relations, using traditional magical amulets (*yath*), or holding grudges. Before
battle, soldiers were anointed with consecrated shea butter oil (*moo yaa*), which
Lakwena promised would turn bullets into water if the soldier's heart was pure.
Soldiers marched into combat in cross-shaped formations, singing Catholic Latin and
Acholi hymns, with "wire controllers" and altar boys carrying crucifixes and Holy
Water at the vanguard.

Scholars emphasize the critical historical and theological distinction between Alice
Auma's Holy Spirit Movement and the subsequent Lord's Resistance Army (LRA) led by
Joseph Kony (Auma's spiritual cousin). While Kony co-opted aspects of Acholi spirit
possession and Christian rhetoric, the LRA abandoned the HSM's strict ethical code of
voluntary participation and anti-looting, devolving into a notorious insurgent army
characterized by mass abductions of children, mutilation of civilians, and widespread
terror across northern Uganda and Central Africa.
