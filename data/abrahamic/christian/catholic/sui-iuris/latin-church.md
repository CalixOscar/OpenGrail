# Latin Church

## Historical formation

The Latin Church developed from Christian communities in the western Roman world, with Rome becoming its principal episcopal center. Latin liturgy, canon law, monasticism, scholastic theology, and missionary institutions gave western Catholicism a recognizable form. The Roman Rite became overwhelmingly predominant, although Ambrosian, Mozarabic, and several religious-order usages also survived.

## Church, not merely a rite

The Latin Church is a self-governing *sui iuris* church within the Catholic communion, not a synonym for all Catholicism. “Roman Rite” names its largest liturgical tradition, whereas “Latin Church” names the church that ordinarily celebrates it. Eastern Catholic churches share communion with the pope but retain distinct hierarchies, rites, disciplines, and theological inheritances.
