# Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church

## Historical formation

The church traces its Christian inheritance to medieval Kyiv and its present institutional form especially to the Union of Brest in 1596, when several Orthodox bishops of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth entered communion with Rome. Suppressed by imperial and Soviet authorities in different periods, it survived through diaspora communities and an extensive underground network before legal restoration in 1989.

## Identity and practice

The Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church is a *sui iuris* major archiepiscopal church. It celebrates the Byzantine liturgy, follows eastern spiritual and canonical traditions, and permits the ordination of married men to the priesthood under its discipline. “Greek Catholic” refers to Byzantine rite and heritage, not Greek ethnicity; the church is Catholic in communion while remaining distinct from both the Latin Church and Eastern Orthodox jurisdictions.
