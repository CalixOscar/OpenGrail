# Melkite Greek Catholic Church

## Historical formation

Melkite Christianity descends from Chalcedonian communities of the patriarchates of Antioch, Alexandria, and Jerusalem. A disputed patriarchal election in 1724 produced a durable division between a line in communion with Rome and the hierarchy now associated with the Greek Orthodox Patriarchate of Antioch. Migration later established Melkite communities throughout the Americas, Australia, and Europe.

## Identity and practice

The Melkite church is a patriarchal *sui iuris* Eastern Catholic church using the Byzantine rite, commonly in Arabic and local languages. Its liturgy, iconography, fasting traditions, married parish clergy, and synodal structures reflect its Orthodox inheritance. It is not an ethnic Greek denomination: “Greek” in its name primarily signals Byzantine liturgical tradition, while “Melkite” has a longer and changing historical usage.
