# Syriac Catholic Church

## Historical formation

Pro-Catholic currents appeared within the Syriac Orthodox milieu during the early modern period, aided by contacts with missionaries and Rome. A lasting Catholic patriarchal succession is conventionally dated from the election of Michael Jarweh in 1782. Communities developed in present-day Syria, Lebanon, and Iraq before persecution, war, and migration produced a wide diaspora.

## Identity and practice

This patriarchal *sui iuris* church celebrates the West Syriac rite and preserves Syriac language, hymnody, fasting, and spirituality associated with Antiochene Christianity. It shares this broad ritual family with the Syriac Orthodox Church but belongs to the Catholic communion. It should not be confused with the Chaldean Catholic Church, which follows the distinct East Syriac tradition.
