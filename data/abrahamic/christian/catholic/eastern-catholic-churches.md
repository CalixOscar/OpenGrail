# Eastern Catholic Churches

## Historical context

Eastern Catholic churches arose through several different histories: some maintained communion with Rome, while others entered union through agreements such as Brest in 1596 or through later institutional realignments. They include Byzantine, Alexandrian, Antiochene, Armenian, East Syriac, and West Syriac ritual families. Migration has established eparchies far beyond their original regions.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

These churches recognize papal communion while retaining their own liturgy, canon law, clerical discipline, spirituality, and governance as *sui iuris* churches. Some ordain married men to the priesthood, though bishops are normally celibate. “Uniate” is often regarded as a historically burdened label. Eastern Catholic identity should not be treated as Latin Catholicism with exotic ceremony, nor should shared rites imply institutional membership in Orthodox churches.
