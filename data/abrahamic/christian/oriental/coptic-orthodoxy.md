# Coptic Orthodoxy

## Historical context

Egyptian Christianity produced major schools of theology and the formative desert monastic movement. After Chalcedon, the non-Chalcedonian patriarchal lineage became what is now the Coptic Orthodox Church. Coptic Christians lived through Byzantine pressure, Arab conquest, changing legal conditions, modern Egyptian nationalism, and a wide diaspora while sustaining Arabic and Coptic liturgical cultures.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The church follows the Alexandrian rite, honors figures such as Cyril of Alexandria, and gives fasting, saints, pilgrimage, and monasticism a prominent role. Coptic is preserved liturgically although Arabic is common in community life. Coptic Orthodoxy belongs to Oriental, not Eastern, Orthodoxy. The modern Catholic Coptic Church shares much ritual heritage but is a distinct body in communion with Rome.
