# Armenian Apostolic Church

## Historical context

Armenian tradition associates national conversion with Gregory the Illuminator and King Tiridates in the early fourth century. The creation of the Armenian alphabet by Mesrop Mashtots enabled major translation and literary projects. Political division, regional empires, the Armenian genocide, Soviet rule, and diaspora life have all shaped the church's institutions and memory.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The Armenian rite combines ancient local developments with Syriac, Cappadocian, and later Latin contacts. The church receives the first three ecumenical councils and belongs to Oriental Orthodoxy. It has two catholicosates and patriarchates within one communion. The Armenian Catholic Church shares much of its ritual culture but is institutionally separate and in communion with Rome.
