# Church of Christ, Scientist

## Historical context

Mary Baker Eddy founded the Church of Christ, Scientist in 1879 after developing a system of scriptural interpretation and spiritual healing. The movement established practitioners, reading rooms, periodicals, and a central church in Boston, expanding internationally around 1900. It participated in a wider American environment of metaphysical religion while asserting a distinct Biblical revelation.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Christian Science understands ultimate reality as spiritual and treats sin, sickness, and death as errors overcome through clearer apprehension of God. Worship pairs readings from the Bible with *Science and Health* and has no ordained clergy. Members' approaches to medical care vary, although reliance on prayer is a historic emphasis and has generated public controversy. Its theology differs markedly from Nicene doctrines, but its texts and self-understanding remain explicitly Christian.
