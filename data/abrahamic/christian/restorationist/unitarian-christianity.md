# Unitarian Christianity

## Historical context

Organized anti-Trinitarian churches emerged in the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth and Transylvania during the Reformation. English and American Unitarianism later developed through rational dissent and liberal Congregationalism. Some institutions joined Universalists and became religiously pluralist, while explicitly Christian Unitarian churches continued in Transylvania, Britain, and independent networks.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Unitarian Christians affirm one God without the coequal three-person formulation of the Nicene Creed. Understandings of Jesus range from uniquely inspired human teacher to preexistent subordinate divine agent. They often emphasize reason, conscience, ethical discipleship, and freedom from binding creeds. Unitarian Christianity should not be equated automatically with Unitarian Universalism, whose congregations may draw on many religious and humanist sources rather than maintain a Christian identity.
