# Seventh Day Adventist Reform Movement

## Historical development

The Reform Movement arose among European Seventh-day Adventists who rejected denominational accommodations to military service during World War I. After expulsions and failed reconciliation efforts, representatives organized internationally in the 1920s. A later division created separate organizations with similar historical claims, one retaining the Seventh Day Adventist Reform Movement name.

## Belief and practice

The movement preserves Seventh-day Adventist scripture, Sabbath, eschatology, and esteem for Ellen White while applying stricter teachings on noncombatancy, diet, dress, and church discipline. It regards itself as a reform within the Adventist inheritance, whereas the larger denomination treats it as a separate body. Its history should not be confused with unrelated Adventist reform initiatives.
