# Seventh-day Adventist Church

## Historical development

Seventh-day Adventism formed from Millerite believers who reinterpreted the disappointment of 1844 and adopted the seventh-day Sabbath. Networks associated with James and Ellen White, Joseph Bates, and other leaders organized the denomination in 1863. Publishing, education, medical institutions, and overseas mission made it the largest and most globally distributed Adventist body.

## Belief and practice

The church is Protestant and Trinitarian, observes Saturday as Sabbath, practices baptism by immersion, and teaches conditional immortality and a heavenly investigative judgment beginning in 1844. Ellen White's writings are regarded as a continuing prophetic gift while the Bible is identified as the doctrinal standard. Health practices and a strong educational system are prominent but vary among members and regions.
