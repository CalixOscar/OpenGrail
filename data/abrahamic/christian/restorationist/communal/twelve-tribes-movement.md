# Twelve Tribes Communities

## Historical context

The Twelve Tribes Communities (officially known at various times as the Vine
Christian Community, Northeast Kingdom Community Church, and Messianic Communities)
was founded in 1972 in Chattanooga, Tennessee, by Elbert Eugene Spriggs Jr. (known
within the movement as *Yoneq*, 1937–2021) and his wife Marsha Spriggs. Originating in
the Jesus Movement revival sweeping the United States in the early 1970s, the group
initially opened a small coffeehouse and ministry called the Light Brigade, followed
by the opening of the first Yellow Deli restaurant in Chattanooga in 1973.

The community practiced apostolic communal living based on the description of the
early church in Acts 2 and 4. In 1979, following growing tensions with local churches
and deprogramming attempts by counter-cult activists, Spriggs relocated the core
community to Island Pond in Vermont's Northeast Kingdom. In 1984, Vermont state police
and social workers conducted a massive raid on the Island Pond commune, taking 112
children into temporary custody based on allegations of physical abuse; however, a
district court judge ruled the search warrants unconstitutional and ordered all
children returned to their parents the same evening. Over subsequent decades, the
movement expanded internationally, establishing dozens of self-sustaining agrarian
and urban communities, organic farms, bakeries, craft shops, and Yellow Deli cafes
across the United States, Canada, Europe, South America, and Australia.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Theologically, the Twelve Tribes combines radical restorationist Christianity,
dispensational millenarianism, and Hebraic roots terminology. Believers refer to
Jesus by the Hebrew name *Yahshua* and God as *Yahweh*. They believe that institutional
Christianity fell into terminal apostasy after the apostolic era, and that salvation
requires dwelling in a literal, visible community where the twelve spiritual tribes of
Israel are restored. Only when a unified, perfectly obedient body of believers lives
in total mutual love without sin will Yahshua return to earth.

Members surrender all private possessions, bank accounts, and property upon joining,
living in large collective households and sharing a common purse. Daily life revolves
around morning and evening gatherings featuring circle dancing, Hebrew singing,
personal testimonies, and communal meals. The movement observes the Saturday Sabbath,
maintains strict biblical dietary guidelines, wears modest 19th-century-styled attire
(men with beards and ponytails, women in modest tunics and head coverings), and shuns
television, radio, internet, and secular education in favor of home schooling and
manual apprenticeship. Governance is strictly patriarchal and hierarchical, overseen
by local councils of male elders (*teachers* and *shepherds*) and apostolic overseers.
