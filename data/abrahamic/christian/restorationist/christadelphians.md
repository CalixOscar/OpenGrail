# Christadelphians

## Historical development

John Thomas entered Alexander Campbell's restoration movement after emigrating to the United States but developed independent teachings through debate and biblical study. Congregations formed in Britain and North America, adopting the name Christadelphian during the American Civil War when conscientious objectors needed a recognized designation. The movement has remained a decentralized network of local ecclesias.

## Belief and practice

Christadelphians reject the Trinity, the inherent immortality of the soul, and a supernatural personal devil. They teach that Jesus is God's Son and human Messiah, that the dead await resurrection, and that Christ will establish God's kingdom on earth. Ecclesias practice believer's baptism, weekly breaking of bread, lay ministry, and conscientious objection, with no central clergy or worldwide governing authority.
