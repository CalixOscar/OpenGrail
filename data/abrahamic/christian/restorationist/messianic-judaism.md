# Messianic Judaism

## Historical context

Modern Messianic Judaism developed chiefly from twentieth-century Hebrew Christian missions and the evangelical revival of the 1960s and 1970s. Congregations adopted Jewish liturgical symbols, Hebrew terminology, festivals, and communal identities while affirming New Testament claims about Jesus, often called Yeshua. Participants include Jews and non-Jews, with considerable variation in practice and self-description.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Most Messianic groups affirm evangelical beliefs about Jesus, salvation, and scripture while maintaining some Torah observance and Jewish cultural continuity. Mainstream Jewish denominations generally classify belief in Jesus as incompatible with Judaism and regard missionary activity critically; Messianic communities dispute that boundary. Scholars usually locate the movement historically within evangelical Christianity. The graph records both Christian genealogy and deliberate Jewish ritual synthesis without resolving contested identity claims.
