# Latter Day Saint Fundamentalism

## Historical development

Latter Day Saint fundamentalism arose after the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints ended authorization of new plural marriages around the turn of the twentieth century. Networks of believers maintained that plural marriage and related teachings remained divinely required. These networks later formed several churches, priesthood councils, and independent communities, with no single organization representing all adherents.

## Classification and practice

Fundamentalist groups commonly accept nineteenth-century Latter Day Saint scriptures and temple theology while disputing later institutional changes. Their leadership systems, communal arrangements, and marriage practices differ widely. The historical term “Mormon fundamentalism” remains common in scholarship, but “Latter Day Saint fundamentalism” better distinguishes this diverse family from the contemporary church that formally rejects and excommunicates members for entering plural marriages.
