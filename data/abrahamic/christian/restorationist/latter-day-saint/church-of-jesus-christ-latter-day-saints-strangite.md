# Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints (Strangite)

## Historical development

James J. Strang claimed appointment by Joseph Smith and reported new angelic authority shortly after Smith's death in 1844. His movement established communities in Wisconsin and on Beaver Island in Lake Michigan, where Strang briefly exercised both ecclesiastical and civil power. Membership declined sharply after his assassination in 1856, but a small church continued.

## Belief and practice

Strangites accept the Bible, Book of Mormon, parts of the Doctrine and Covenants, and Strang's *Book of the Law of the Lord*. The tradition maintains Saturday as the Sabbath and recognizes prophetic succession through angelic ordination. “Strangite” is a historical differentiator rather than the church's formal name, and it should not be conflated with Utah-based Latter-day Saint institutions.
