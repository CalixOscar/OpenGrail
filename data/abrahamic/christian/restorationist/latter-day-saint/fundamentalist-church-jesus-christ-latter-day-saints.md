# Fundamentalist Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints

## Historical development

The FLDS Church developed from the Short Creek community and a fundamentalist priesthood council that continued plural marriage after its abandonment by the Utah-based church. A 1950s leadership split helped produce a distinct institutional body, later associated with communities in Arizona, Utah, Texas, and Canada. State interventions and criminal convictions of senior leaders profoundly affected the organization and its members.

## Belief and organization

The church preserves a fundamentalist interpretation of nineteenth-century Latter Day Saint scripture, priesthood, communal discipline, and plural marriage. It is institutionally independent from the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, which rejects both its authority claims and its marriage practices. Scholarship and journalism should distinguish the FLDS Church from other fundamentalist groups and from Latter-day Saints generally.
