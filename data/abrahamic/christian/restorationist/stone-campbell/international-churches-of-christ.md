# International Churches of Christ

## Historical development

The International Churches of Christ developed from a rapidly growing ministry led by Kip McKean in Lexington, Massachusetts, and then Boston from 1979. Its discipling system, missionary expansion, and centralized sector leadership differentiated the Boston Movement from other Churches of Christ. It adopted the International Churches of Christ name in the 1990s and underwent substantial decentralization after a leadership crisis in 2002–2003.

## Organization and practice

Congregations retain Stone-Campbell emphases on the Bible, baptism by immersion, and restoration of early Christian discipleship. Intensive mentoring and a strong expectation of evangelism have been distinctive, although practice changed after public criticism and internal reforms. Present congregations cooperate through regional and international networks rather than the former centralized structure.
