# Independent Christian Churches and Churches of Christ

## Historical development

Independent Christian Churches developed from congregations and institutions that resisted the increasingly denominational structure of the Disciples of Christ during the early and mid-twentieth century. The separation was gradual and was marked by disputes over open membership, missionary agencies, property, and denominational organization. The North American Christian Convention became an important gathering point without governing local churches.

## Organization and practice

These congregations usually practice believer's baptism by immersion, celebrate Communion weekly, use instrumental music, and emphasize local autonomy. Bible colleges and independent mission agencies connect congregations without forming a binding hierarchy. Their name overlaps with the noninstrumental Churches of Christ, but historians normally distinguish the two Stone-Campbell streams by worship practices and institutional histories.
