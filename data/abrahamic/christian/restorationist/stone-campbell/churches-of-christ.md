# Churches of Christ

## Historical development

Churches of Christ emerged from the Stone-Campbell Restoration Movement in the United States. Disputes over missionary societies, instrumental music, biblical interpretation, and the effects of the Civil War gradually separated many congregations from the stream later represented by the Disciples of Christ. A 1906 United States religious census treated the Churches of Christ as a distinct body, although no single act created the separation.

## Organization and practice

Congregations are autonomous and normally governed by local elders rather than a denominational headquarters. Common practices include believer's baptism by immersion, weekly Communion, and a strong appeal to New Testament precedent; most congregations sing without instruments. Considerable diversity exists over cooperation, worship, gender, and biblical interpretation, so the plural name accurately reflects a network rather than one centralized church.
