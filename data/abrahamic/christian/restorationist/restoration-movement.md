# Restoration Movement

## Historical context

The Restoration Movement arose on the early nineteenth-century United States frontier through networks associated especially with Barton W. Stone and Alexander Campbell. Participants sought Christian unity by setting aside inherited creeds and restoring patterns they found in the New Testament. Growth, institutional disputes, and differing approaches to instruments and missions eventually contributed to Churches of Christ, Christian Churches, and the Christian Church (Disciples of Christ).

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Common themes include believer's baptism by immersion, weekly Communion, congregational life, and the slogan that scripture should supply a basis for unity. The movement's branches differ substantially over ministry, worship, ecumenism, and biblical interpretation. “Restorationism” can also name a broader impulse including unrelated movements; this node specifically denotes Stone-Campbell history and is not the parent organization of every nineteenth-century restorationist group.
