# Waldensians

## Historical context

Peter Waldo, a merchant of Lyon, gave away his property around 1173 and commissioned
vernacular translations of Scripture, gathering a preaching brotherhood. Refused
permission to preach at the Third Lateran Council and excommunicated at Verona in
1184, the movement went underground and spread through southern France, Lombardy,
Provence, Bohemia, and the Alpine valleys of Piedmont and Dauphiné, where it endured
inquisition, the 1487 crusade, and the Piedmontese massacres of 1655.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

At the synod of Chanforan in 1532 the Waldensian barbes adopted a Reformed confession,
and the movement became in effect an Italian Protestant church, gaining civil rights
in Piedmont in 1848. It is living, not historical: the Chiesa Evangelica Valdese
remains active in Italy and in the Río de la Plata, and has been in full communion
with the Italian Methodist Church since 1975.

Waldensians were routinely bracketed with Cathars by inquisitors and are still
sometimes confused with them, but the two movements were doctrinally opposed. The
Waldensians were not dualists; their dissent was disciplinary and biblicist, aimed at
the authority claimed by the clergy rather than at the goodness of the created world.
