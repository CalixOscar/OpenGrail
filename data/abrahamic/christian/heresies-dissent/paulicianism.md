# Paulicianism

## Historical context

Paulicianism emerged in Armenia and the eastern Byzantine provinces in the seventh
century and survived repeated imperial persecution, notably under Theodora in the
840s. Driven into the frontier zone, the Paulicians established an armed polity at
Tephrike under Karbeas and Chrysocheir, in alliance with the Abbasid frontier emirates,
until Basil I destroyed it around 872. Substantial populations were deported to Thrace,
where they persisted into the medieval period; related Tondrakian communities continued
in Armenia into the eleventh century.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The doctrinal profile is contested because it is transmitted almost entirely through
hostile Byzantine sources such as Peter of Sicily. The Armenian Key of Truth, known
from a nineteenth-century manuscript, presents a movement that looks adoptionist and
biblicist rather than radically dualist, and scholars disagree about how far that text
can be read back onto the earlier communities.

The Thracian deportations are the conventional explanation for the appearance of
dualist teaching in the Balkans a century later, and thus for the line usually drawn
to Bogomilism. The connection is plausible in geography and chronology but is not
directly documented.
