# Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Church

## Historical formation

Eritrean Orthodox communities share the ancient Aksumite Christian inheritance historically administered within the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church. Following Eritrea’s independence, the Coptic Orthodox pope recognized an autocephalous Eritrean church in 1993, and its first patriarch was consecrated in 1994. Later state intervention and disputes over patriarchal leadership have caused serious internal tensions.

## Identity and practice

The church belongs to Oriental Orthodoxy and uses the Alexandrian-derived Ge’ez rite, with fasting, sacred dance, chant, monasticism, and devotion to the *tabot* prominent in religious life. “Tewahedo,” meaning unity, signals its miaphysite Christological heritage. It is institutionally distinct from Ethiopian Orthodoxy despite deep liturgical, linguistic, and historical connections.
