# Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church

## Historical formation

The Malankara church belongs to the Saint Thomas Christian history of southwestern India. After resistance to Portuguese Latinization in the seventeenth century, part of the community formed closer ties with the Syriac Orthodox patriarchate and adopted the West Syriac rite. The establishment of a Catholicate in India in 1912 became central to later disputes over autocephaly and patriarchal authority.

## Identity and distinctions

The Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church recognizes the catholicos of the East and Malankara metropolitan as its primate and belongs to Oriental Orthodox communion. It is Indian rather than ethnically Syrian; “Syrian” refers to a liturgical and ecclesiastical inheritance. It should be distinguished from the Jacobite Syrian Christian Church, the Syro-Malankara Catholic Church, and other Saint Thomas Christian bodies.
