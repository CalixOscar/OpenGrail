# Greek Orthodox Patriarchate of Jerusalem

## Historical formation

Jerusalem’s Christian community claims direct continuity with the earliest church described in Acts. The Council of Chalcedon elevated the see to patriarchal rank in 451. Persian invasion, Islamic rule, crusader occupation, Ottoman administration, and modern conflict each reshaped its institutions, while guardianship of holy sites made the patriarchate internationally significant.

## Identity and scope

The patriarchate is an autocephalous Eastern Orthodox church with jurisdiction in Israel, Palestine, and Jordan and a central role in the Church of the Holy Sepulchre. Its hierarchy has historically been strongly Greek while much of its laity is Arabic-speaking, producing recurring institutional debates. The church is distinct from other Orthodox and Catholic jurisdictions sharing the same sacred geography.
