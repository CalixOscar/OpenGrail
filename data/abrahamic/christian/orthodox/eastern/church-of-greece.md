# Church of Greece

## Historical formation

Following Greek independence from the Ottoman Empire, church authorities in the new kingdom declared autocephaly in 1833; the Ecumenical Patriarchate recognized it in 1850. Its jurisdiction covers much, but not all, of Greece. Crete, the Dodecanese, Mount Athos, and the so-called New Lands have distinct canonical arrangements involving the Ecumenical Patriarchate.

## Identity and governance

The Church of Greece is governed by a synod headed by the archbishop of Athens. Its worship follows the Byzantine rite in Greek, within the larger theological and sacramental communion of Eastern Orthodoxy. “Greek Orthodox” can describe a broad cultural-liturgical family, whereas the Church of Greece is one specific autocephalous institution and should not be conflated with Constantinople, Cyprus, or Alexandria.
