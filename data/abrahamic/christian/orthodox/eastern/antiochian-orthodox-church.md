# Greek Orthodox Patriarchate of Antioch

## Historical formation

The Christian community of ancient Antioch appears prominently in early Christian sources and became one of the major patriarchal sees. After the Chalcedonian divisions, rival patriarchal successions developed. The Eastern Orthodox patriarchate later operated under Byzantine, Islamic, crusader, Ottoman, and modern states, with its administrative center eventually established in Damascus.

## Identity and scope

Despite the historic title “Greek Orthodox,” most of its contemporary faithful are Arabic-speaking, and the church should not be mistaken for the Church of Greece. It is an autocephalous Byzantine-rite patriarchate with dioceses in the Middle East and a substantial diaspora. Its Antiochene geography and history overlap with several Catholic and Oriental Orthodox churches that nevertheless possess distinct hierarchies.
