# Russian Orthodox Church

## Historical formation

The baptism of Prince Vladimir and the Christianization of Kyivan Rus’ in 988 linked the region to Byzantine Christianity. Ecclesiastical leadership eventually shifted northward, and Moscow’s church became autocephalous in the fifteenth century and a patriarchate in 1589. Imperial patronage, Soviet persecution, post-Soviet revival, and global migration have each transformed its institutions.

## Identity and scope

The Moscow Patriarchate is one autocephalous church within Eastern Orthodoxy, using Church Slavonic and vernacular languages in Byzantine worship. Its claimed canonical territory and relationship to churches in Ukraine and elsewhere are subjects of serious contemporary ecclesiastical conflict. Describing those disputes requires separating documented institutional claims from theological endorsement or modern nationalist narratives.
