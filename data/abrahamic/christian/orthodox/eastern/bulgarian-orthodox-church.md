# Bulgarian Orthodox Church

## Historical formation

The Bulgarian church received autonomous status in the ninth century and later patriarchal dignity, becoming an important center of Slavic Christian literature and worship. Byzantine and Ottoman conquests disrupted its institutions. A nineteenth-century exarchate asserted independence amid national revival; Constantinople recognized autocephaly in 1945, and the patriarchate was restored in 1953.

## Identity and governance

The church is governed by a holy synod under the patriarch of Bulgaria and follows Byzantine liturgy in Church Slavonic and Bulgarian. Medieval centers such as Preslav, Ohrid, and Tarnovo remain central to its cultural memory. Although national history shaped its institutions, its doctrine, sacraments, and episcopal communion belong to the wider Eastern Orthodox tradition.
