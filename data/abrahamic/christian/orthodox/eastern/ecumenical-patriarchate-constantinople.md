# Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople

## Historical formation

The bishopric of Byzantium gained exceptional prominence after Constantine made the city an imperial capital in 330. Ecumenical councils accorded Constantinople senior rank after Rome, and its patriarchate profoundly shaped Byzantine liturgy, theology, monasticism, and Slavic missions. Ottoman conquest transformed the patriarch into a major communal leader, while modern national churches reduced the territory under direct jurisdiction.

## Communion and primacy

The ecumenical patriarch is conventionally described as “first among equals” among Orthodox primates. This primacy is one of honor and coordinating responsibility, not the universal ordinary jurisdiction claimed for the pope in Catholic doctrine. The patriarchate directly includes dioceses and institutions in several regions and the diaspora; it is not simply another name for every Greek-speaking Orthodox church.
