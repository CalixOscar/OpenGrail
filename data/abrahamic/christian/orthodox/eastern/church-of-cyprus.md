# Church of Cyprus

## Historical formation

Christian communities flourished early on Cyprus, which New Testament tradition associates with Barnabas and Paul. The Council of Ephesus in 431 affirmed the Cypriot church’s independence from Antioch, making its autocephaly one of the oldest. Frankish, Venetian, Ottoman, British, and modern Cypriot political orders all affected its property, governance, and public role.

## Identity and governance

The archbishop of Cyprus presides over a synod of diocesan bishops. The church celebrates the Byzantine rite in Greek and participates fully in Eastern Orthodox communion. Its autocephaly names ecclesiastical self-government; it does not mean theological separation from other Orthodox churches. Modern territorial division on the island continues to shape access to churches and pastoral life.
