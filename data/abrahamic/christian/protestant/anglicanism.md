# Anglicanism

## Historical context

Anglicanism took institutional form when the English crown rejected papal jurisdiction, then underwent successive Protestant and Catholicizing reforms. The Elizabethan settlement and Book of Common Prayer gave English Christianity a durable liturgical shape. Empire and mission spread Anglican churches globally; many later became autonomous provinces within the Anglican Communion.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Anglicans commonly appeal to scripture, historic creeds, episcopal order, and liturgical prayer, but they contain evangelical, Anglo-Catholic, charismatic, and liberal currents. This breadth makes “middle way” an attractive but incomplete description. Anglican churches differ over ordination, sexuality, and authority, and the Communion has no pope-like central office. Methodism began as an Anglican renewal before developing separate denominations, while continuing Anglican bodies later separated over liturgical and doctrinal change.
