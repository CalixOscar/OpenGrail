# Lutheranism

## Historical context

Lutheranism emerged from Martin Luther's challenge to indulgences and his disputes with church authorities in the early sixteenth century. Reformers and territorial churches developed institutions in German and Scandinavian lands, while the Augsburg Confession of 1530 articulated a shared public position. Immigration, missions, Pietism, confessional revivals, and ecumenism later produced diverse global Lutheran churches.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Lutheran theology stresses justification by grace through faith, the distinction and interaction of law and gospel, and God's action through preached word and sacraments. Most churches baptize infants and confess Christ's real presence in the Eucharist without adopting the Roman Catholic explanation of transubstantiation. Lutheran bodies vary on liturgy, episcopacy, biblical interpretation, and social ethics. Their confessions distinguish them from Reformed Protestantism even where cooperation is close.
