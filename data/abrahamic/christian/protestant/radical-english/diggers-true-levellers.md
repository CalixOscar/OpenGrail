# Diggers (True Levellers)

## Historical context

In April 1649, in the aftermath of the execution of King Charles I, Gerrard
Winstanley and a small group of impoverished followers occupied and began
cultivating common land on St George's Hill in Surrey. Calling themselves the True
Levellers (distinguishing themselves from the constitutional Leveller movement led
by John Lilburne), they established communal colonies at St George's Hill and
later Cobham. Local landowners, clergy, and parish authorities responded with
harassment, lawsuits, physical assaults, and the destruction of their crops and
shelters. By late 1650, the colonies were forcibly dispersed and the movement
collapsed.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Winstanley's writings developed a theological and social philosophy that
equated God with "Reason" and argued that private property, wage labor, and
monarchical rule originated in the biblical Fall. Unlike the political Levellers
who defended private property and smallholders' rights, the Diggers sought
complete communal ownership of land. While contemporaneous groups such as the
Ranters drew antinomian and individualist conclusions from universal divine
immanence, the Diggers emphasized disciplined agrarian labor, strict moral
conduct, and practical communal solidarity.
