# Muggletonians

## Historical context

In February 1652, London tailor Lodowicke Muggleton announced that his cousin
John Reeve had received a direct divine revelation appointing them as the "Two
Witnesses" prophesied in Revelation 11. They claimed to inaugurate the final
"Commission of the Spirit," succeeding the commissions of Moses (the Law) and
Jesus (the Apostles). After Reeve's death in 1658, Muggleton became the sole
leader, enduring trial and pillory under Charles II. Remarkably, the sect
survived into the twentieth century in small artisan and family circles without a
clergy or active proselytism, until the death of its last known member, Philip
Noakes of Kent, in 1979, after which its extensive archives were transferred to the
British Library.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Muggletonian theology was uniquely idiosyncratic and anti-philosophical. They
held a materialist view of the divine, asserting that God was a corporeal being
who entered the womb of Mary, leaving the governance of the universe temporarily
in the hands of the prophet Elijah. They rejected the immortality of the soul,
teaching mortalism (the soul dies with the body until a general physical
resurrection), and held a "Two Seeds" doctrine: that Cain was the physical
offspring of Eve and the devil, dividing humanity into descendants of faith or
damnation.

Unlike almost all other seventeenth-century sects, Muggletonians practiced no
outward worship, holding that God did not intervene in the world or hear prayers
after Christ's ascension. They were fierce polemical opponents of early Quakers
and Ranters, engaging in reciprocal curses with George Fox and William Penn.
