# Evangelicalism

## Historical context

Evangelicalism took shape through eighteenth-century revivals in Britain and North America, drawing on Pietist, Puritan, Anglican, and Methodist sources. Nineteenth-century missions and reform societies widened its reach. Fundamentalist-modernist conflict and postwar “neo-evangelical” institutions reshaped the label, which now covers culturally and politically varied movements across every inhabited continent.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Historians often describe four recurring traits: conversion, activism, high regard for the Bible, and focus on Christ's atoning death. Evangelicalism crosses Baptist, Anglican, Reformed, Wesleyan, Pentecostal, and independent churches, so it is not a denomination or centralized hierarchy. Views differ on biblical interpretation, sacraments, gender, politics, charismatic gifts, and social justice. In some countries the word carries identities unlike its common United States usage.
