# Anabaptism

## Historical context

Anabaptist movements emerged in several sixteenth-century European settings when reformers rejected infant baptism and established communities of professing believers. Catholic and Protestant governments persecuted them, sometimes violently. Swiss Brethren, Hutterites, Mennonites, and later Amish communities developed different organizations, migration histories, and accommodations to surrounding societies.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Historic Anabaptist themes include discipleship modeled on Jesus, mutual accountability, refusal of coercion, economic sharing in some communities, and often nonviolence. “Anabaptist” was originally an opponents' label meaning rebaptizer; adherents argued that infant rites were not valid baptism. The tradition belongs to the Radical Reformation but should not be conflated with all Baptists. Contemporary Anabaptists vary in dress, technology, worship, and engagement with public life.
