# Münster Anabaptist Rebellion

## Historical context

In 1533–1534, radical reformers and Melchiorite Anabaptist preachers led by Jan
Matthys and Jan van Leiden gained control of the municipal council in the
Prince-Bishopric of Münster. Declaring the city to be the New Jerusalem, they
expelled unbaptized residents, abolished private property, and instituted
communal ownership under religious supervision. Following Matthys's death during
a sortie in April 1534, Jan van Leiden assumed leadership, eventually
proclaiming himself King of the New Jerusalem and ruler of the world.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

While early Swiss Brethren and South German Anabaptists codified strict
pacifism, non-resistance, and complete separation from state authority in the
1527 Schleitheim Confession, the Münster leadership adopted an aggressive, armed
millenarian posture. They held that true believers were commissioned to take up
the sword in active retribution against temporal rulers to cleanse the earth
before the Second Coming. The catastrophic collapse of the Münster kingdom led
surviving Anabaptist movements, notably under Menno Simons, to definitively
reaffirm strict nonviolence and repudiate political insurrection.

## Documented harms and controversies

Between 1534 and 1535, the theocratic regime in Münster exercised authoritarian
control accompanied by severe coercion and public executions. In July 1534, Jan
van Leiden instituted compulsory polygamy under penalty of death, executing
dissenting residents, including his wife Elisabeth Wandscherer when she
challenged his authority. Following a brutal fifteen-month siege that caused
widespread starvation, the city fell on June 24, 1535, to the joint forces of
Catholic Prince-Bishop Franz von Waldeck and Protestant allies. An extensive
massacre ensued during the retaking of the city. Jan van Leiden, Bernhard
Knipperdolling, and Bernhard Krechting were captured, publicly tortured with red-hot
tongs, and executed on January 22, 1536, after which their remains were suspended
in iron cages from the steeple of St. Lambert's Church.
