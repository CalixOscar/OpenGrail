# Eastern Orthodoxy

## Historical context

Eastern Orthodoxy developed through the Greek-speaking and other eastern churches of the Roman and Byzantine worlds. The conventional date 1054 symbolizes a long estrangement from Latin Christianity rather than a single clean break. Constantinople's fall, Ottoman rule, Slavic missions, modern national movements, and global migration all shaped today's network of autocephalous churches.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Orthodox theology emphasizes continuity with the early councils, theosis or participation in divine life, icon veneration, and worship structured by the Divine Liturgy. Authority is conciliar: no bishop has the same universal jurisdiction claimed for the Roman pope. Eastern Orthodoxy should not be confused with Oriental Orthodoxy, which separated after debates surrounding Chalcedon. Modern dialogue has shown that some historic disputes involved language and politics as well as theology.
