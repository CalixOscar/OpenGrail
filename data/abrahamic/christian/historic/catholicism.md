# Catholicism

## Historical context

Catholicism traces its institutions to ancient Christian communities and the developing authority of bishops, councils, and the Roman see. Medieval Latin Christianity grew through monastic, scholastic, liturgical, and missionary networks. The Reformation prompted the Council of Trent, while the First and Second Vatican Councils addressed papal authority, modernity, liturgical renewal, and relations with other religions.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Catholic teaching joins scripture with apostolic tradition and a teaching office exercised by bishops in communion with the pope. Seven sacraments, Eucharistic worship, devotion to saints, and natural-law reasoning are prominent. Eastern Catholic churches retain non-Latin rites and disciplines, so Catholicism is not identical with the Latin rite. Orthodox churches share much early doctrine and sacramental life but dispute later Roman claims of universal jurisdiction.
