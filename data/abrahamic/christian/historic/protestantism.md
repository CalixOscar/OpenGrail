# Protestantism

## Historical context

Protestantism arose through multiple sixteenth-century reformations rather than one organization. Lutheran, Reformed, Anglican, and Radical movements challenged aspects of papal authority, sacramental practice, clerical life, and late-medieval theology. Printing, princely politics, urban networks, and vernacular scripture accelerated change. Later revivals, missions, migration, and colonial histories produced a global and highly diverse family.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Many Protestants emphasize scripture's primary authority, justification by grace through faith, preaching, and the priesthood of believers. Agreements stop short of uniformity: churches differ on baptism, Eucharist, predestination, episcopacy, spiritual gifts, and relations with states. “Protestant” can describe a historical genealogy even when a community prefers another identity. Modern ecumenical agreements have also narrowed some Reformation-era disputes without erasing institutional divisions.
