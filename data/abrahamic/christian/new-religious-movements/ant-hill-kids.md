# Ant Hill Kids

## Historical context

The Ant Hill Kids was a destructive millenarian commune founded in 1977 by Roch
Thériault (1947–2011) in Quebec, Canada. Thériault, a former Seventh-day Adventist who
had conducted unauthorized smoking-cessation seminars and apocalyptic health lectures,
broke away from the church after being expelled. Claiming that God revealed that the
world would end in a catastrophic nuclear war in February 1979, Thériault gathered a
small group of followers—primarily women—and founded a commune on a remote hillside in
Sainte-Marie-de-Beauce, Quebec.

When the prophesied apocalypse failed to occur, Thériault declared that the date had
been extended to test the faith of his flock. He required members to work tirelessly
clearing rocks, felling timber, and constructing cabins, remarking that they must
"labor like ants" in their hill, from which media later derived the name "Ant Hill
Kids." Facing mounting investigations by Quebec child welfare authorities, Thériault
relocated the commune in 1984 to a remote 100-acre property near Burnt River in rural
central Ontario. There, he established total control over roughly twenty adults and
children, cutting them off entirely from communication with relatives and civil
society.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Thériault's ideology combined elements of Seventh-day Adventist vegetarianism,
strict Sabbath keeping, and end-times dispensationalism with extreme narcissistic
megalomania. Adopting the name *Moïse* (Moses), Thériault declared himself God's sole
living prophet on earth and the father of the new humanity. He established a
polygamous household, taking all female members as his spiritual wives and fathering
over twenty children, who were denied formal education and birth registration.

The internal dynamic of the commune became an extreme total institution. Thériault
exercised absolute authority over every aspect of daily life, diet, labor, and
corporal discipline. Members surrendered their financial assets, endured public
criticism and physical punishments, and were subjected to intense sleep deprivation
and alcohol-fueled ideological rants.

## Documented harms and controversies

Throughout the late 1970s and 1980s, Roch Thériault subjected commune members to
systematic torture, physical mutilation, and non-consensual surgeries performed
without anesthesia. Believing that he possessed miraculous healing powers, Thériault
performed amateur surgical procedures on followers using kitchen knives, pliers, and
scissors, including castrations, amputations of fingers and toes, and bowel
resections.

In 1988, member Gabrielle Lavallée escaped after Thériault intentionally pinned her
arm to a table and severed her hand with a meat cleaver; she managed to reach a
hospital and inform police. In 1989, commune member Solange Boilard died in agony
after Thériault performed an unsterilized abdominal operation on her to remove an
alleged demon. Police arrested Thériault in 1989, effectively ending the commune. In
1993, Thériault was convicted of second-degree murder in Boilard's death and sentenced
to life in prison. He remained incarcerated at Dorchester Penitentiary in New
Brunswick until February 26, 2011, when he was killed during an altercation with a
fellow inmate.
