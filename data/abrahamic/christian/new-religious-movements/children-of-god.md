# Children of God (The Family International)

## Historical context

The Children of God originated in 1968 in Huntington Beach, California, founded by
David Brandt Berg (1919–1994), an ordained minister of the Christian and Missionary
Alliance who had also worked with the Pentecostal evangelist Fred Jordan. Initially
calling his youth group "Teens for Christ," Berg gathered disaffected countercultural
youth from the nascent Jesus Movement, preaching radical discipleship, apocalyptic
judgment on America, and total separation from worldly institutions ("the System").
Following widespread publicity and parent protests, the group adopted the name
Children of God in 1969 and began establishing communal "colonies" across North
America, Europe, and Latin America.

Berg went into self-imposed seclusion in 1971, communicating with his followers
exclusively through illustrated epistles known as the "MO Letters" (*Moses David
Letters*). In 1978, facing mounting public controversy and government scrutiny, Berg
disbanded the central organization in the "Reorganization Nationalization
Revolution" (RNR), reorganizing the movement into autonomous, family-based communal
homes under the name "The Family of Love" (later shortened to "The Family"). Following
Berg's death in Portugal in 1994, leadership passed to his widow, Karen Zerby
(known as *Mama Maria*), and Peter Amsterdam. In 2010, the movement underwent a major
restructuring, officially transitioning from a communal living model to an online,
decentralized network of independent missionary members known as The Family
International (TFI).

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The movement synthesized charismatic Pentecostalism, dispensational premillennialism,
and countercultural communalism. Berg taught that God was love and that the "Law of
Love" superseded conventional moral and civil laws. Members surrendered outside
employment to live in shared communal households, devoting their days to prayer, Bible
study, musical performances, tract distribution, and child rearing.

The theological framework was intensely millenarian, viewing international political
events (especially in the Middle East) as precise markers of the rise of the
Antichrist and the countdown to Armageddon. Unlike traditional evangelical groups,
the Children of God developed radical doctrines concerning human sexuality, viewing
sexual expression within the community as a foretaste of heavenly ecstasy and a
legitimate vehicle for divine communication and ministry.

## Documented harms and controversies

Between 1974 and 1987, the Children of God practiced "Flirty Fishing" (or *FFing*),
a method of religious proselytization in which female members used romantic and sexual
intimacy to minister to outsiders ("fishers of men") and solicit financial support.
Internal movement records documented over 200,000 contacts and hundreds of births
resulting from the practice before Berg formally halted it in 1987 due to concerns over
sexually transmitted infections and HIV/AIDS.

During the late 1970s and 1980s, Berg authored several MO Letters promoting doctrines
that rationalized adult-child sexual contact within the communal family. These
writings and practices resulted in documented instances of child sexual abuse,
subsequently corroborated by governmental inquiries in France, Spain, Argentina, the
United Kingdom, and the United States, as well as extensive published testimonies by
second-generation former members who grew up in the communes. Following Berg's death,
the leadership of The Family International formally banned all sexual interactions
between adults and minors in 1989, enacted strict zero-tolerance child protection
charters in the late 1990s, and issued public statements expressing deep regret and
repudiation of past abusive literature and practices.
