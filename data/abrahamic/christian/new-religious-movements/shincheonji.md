# Shincheonji Church of Jesus

## Historical context

Shincheonji Church of Jesus, the Temple of the Tabernacle of the Testimony
(*Shincheonji Yesugyo Jeunggo Jangmak Seongjeon*), was founded on March 14, 1984, in
Gwacheon, Gyeonggi Province, South Korea, by Lee Man-hee (born 1931). Lee had
previously belonged to several Korean millenarian and apocalyptic movements,
including Park Tae-son's Olive Tree movement (*Jeondogwan*) and Yoo Jae-yul's
Tabernacle Temple (*Jangmak Seongjeon*). Lee claimed that in 1980 he received a direct
heavenly revelation commissioning him as the "Counselor" and the "One Who Overcomes"
spoken of in the Book of Revelation.

Operating from its international headquarters in Gwacheon, Shincheonji developed a
highly disciplined, hierarchical organizational model divided into twelve "tribes"
named after the twelve apostles of Jesus. The church established the Zion Christian
Mission Center, an intensive free educational academy where prospective converts
undergo hundreds of hours of biblical instruction before being admitted to formal
church membership. Over four decades, Shincheonji grew into one of the largest new
religious movements in South Korea, claiming over 300,000 members and expanding
branches across Europe, North America, Oceania, and Africa. In 2020, the movement came
under intense global scrutiny during the early COVID-19 pandemic when its Daegu branch
became the epicenter of South Korea's first major viral outbreak.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Shincheonji theology centers on an allegorical and dispensational reading of
biblical prophecy. Lee Man-hee teaches that the Bible is written in spiritual secrets
and parables that remained sealed until the present generation. According to
Shincheonji, biblical history is divided into successive eras of betrayal,
destruction, and salvation: the contemporary mainstream Christian church has fallen
into apostasy (betrayal), allowing demonic theology to corrupt believers
(destruction), while Shincheonji represents the unique remnant gathering the true
saints into the New Heaven and New Earth (*Shin-cheon-ji*).

Members believe that when exactly 144,000 believers are properly sealed with the
revealed word and organized into the twelve tribes, the spirits of the martyrs in
heaven will descend and unite with their living physical bodies, conferring physical
immortality and eternal priestly governance over the nations. Worship services are
characterized by strict uniformity, with congregants dressed in white shirts and dark
slacks, kneeling in tightly packed, perfectly aligned rows without chairs. The church
has drawn controversy from mainstream Korean Protestant denominations due to its
allegorical teachings and its "harvesting" missionary strategy, wherein trained
advocates join conventional churches under cover to recruit existing Christians into
Shincheonji Bible study centers.
