# Ibadi Islam

## Historical context

Ibadi communities emerged from early disputes about righteous leadership and communal judgment. Historians connect their formation to moderate currents that separated from more militant Kharijites, but Ibadis reject the use of “Kharijite” as a sufficient self-description. Ibadi imamates and scholarly networks developed in Oman, North Africa, and parts of East Africa; communities remain in Oman, Algeria, Libya, Tunisia, and Zanzibar.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Ibadi thought teaches that an imam should be selected for knowledge and righteousness rather than hereditary lineage and may be removed for grave failure. Its law, hadith transmission, and theology form an independent tradition while sharing basic worship and scripture with other Muslims. Historical doctrines of affiliation and dissociation are more nuanced than popular portrayals of blanket hostility. Modern Ibadi communities are not continuations of violent extremist Kharijite groups.
