# Sunni Islam

## Historical context

Sunni identity emerged gradually from debates over leadership, civil war, communal authority, law, and hadith after Muhammad's death. The expression *ahl al-sunna wa-l-jama'a* came to mark commitment to prophetic precedent and the broad community. By the medieval period, several legal and theological schools provided durable institutions across a wide geographic range.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Sunni Muslims generally recognize the first four caliphs as legitimate and ground practice in Qur'an, prophetic sunna, scholarly consensus, and disciplined legal reasoning. Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali law coexist, as do Ash'ari, Maturidi, Athari, Sufi, Salafi, and other orientations. Sunni Islam has no single worldwide hierarchy. Its distinction from Shi'a Islam concerns authority and sacred history, not a wholly different scripture or God.
