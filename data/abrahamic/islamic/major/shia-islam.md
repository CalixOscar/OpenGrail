# Shi'a Islam

## Historical context

Shi'a traditions grew from loyalty to Ali ibn Abi Talib and claims that authority after Muhammad belonged within the Prophet's household. The killing of Husayn at Karbala in 680 became a defining memory of injustice and faithful witness. Disagreements over lines of Imams later produced Twelver, Ismaili, Zaydi, and other communities with distinct histories.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Shi'a theology gives the Imams special authority as guides to revelation and law, although the nature and number of Imams differ by branch. Ritual remembrance of Karbala, devotion to the Prophet's family, pilgrimage, and separate hadith corpora are prominent. Shi'a and Sunni Muslims share the Qur'an, core worship, and many legal concepts. The schism edge visualizes a gradual political-theological differentiation, not a single instant of separation.
