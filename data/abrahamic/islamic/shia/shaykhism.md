# Shaykhism

## Historical development

Shaykh Ahmad al-Ahsa'i and his successor Sayyid Kazim Rashti formed a distinctive scholarly network in Qajar Iran and Ottoman Iraq. Their teachings explored the spiritual bodies of resurrection, the metaphysical rank of the imams, and layered worlds of divine manifestation. Leadership disputes after Rashti produced several continuing Shaykhi communities.

## Influence and distinction

Some students from the Shaykhi environment became early followers of the Bab, giving the school an important place in Babi history. Other Shaykhis rejected the Bab's claims and remained within Twelver Shiism. The influence edge therefore records a historical milieu rather than identifying Shaykhism itself as Babi or Bahá'í.
