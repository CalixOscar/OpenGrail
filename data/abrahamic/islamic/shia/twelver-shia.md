# Twelver Shi'a

## Historical context

Twelver Shi'ism formed through communities loyal to a succession of Imams from Ali to Hasan al-Askari. After the eleventh Imam's death in 874, followers developed the doctrine that his son, the twelfth Imam, entered occultation and would return as the Mahdi. Scholarly authority, legal institutions, Safavid state patronage, and seminaries in Najaf and Qom shaped later Twelver life.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Twelvers regard the Imams as divinely guided interpreters and await the hidden Imam's return. Jurists derive law from Qur'an, prophetic and Imamic reports, consensus, and reason; most modern communities follow a living senior jurist. Karbala remembrance and shrine pilgrimage are central devotional forms. Akhbari and Usuli approaches disagree over juristic method. Twelver doctrines should not be projected onto Ismaili or Zaydi branches, whose lines of authority differ.
