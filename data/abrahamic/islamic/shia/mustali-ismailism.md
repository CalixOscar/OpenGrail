# Musta'li Ismailism

## Historical development

After the Fatimid caliph-imam al-Mustansir died in 1094, supporters divided over the succession of his sons Nizar and al-Musta'li. The Fatimid state supported al-Musta'li, while Nizari communities followed Nizar's line. Musta'li history subsequently developed in Egypt, Yemen, and South Asia.

## Later branches

Another succession dispute after al-Amir's death produced Hafizi and Tayyibi lines. The Hafizi community faded after the end of Fatimid rule, while Tayyibi communities continued through a concealed imam and a missionary representative, the *da'i al-mutlaq*. Contemporary Bohra communities descend from this Tayyibi inheritance.
