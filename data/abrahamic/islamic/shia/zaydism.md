# Zaydism

## Historical context

Zaydism traces its name to Zayd ibn Ali, killed in revolt against Umayyad rule in 740. Zaydi communities established imamates around the Caspian and, most enduringly, in Yemen from the late ninth century. The Yemeni imamate lasted in changing forms until the republican revolution of 1962; modern conflict has transformed but not erased Zaydi religious institutions.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Zaydis teach that a qualified descendant of Hasan or Husayn may claim the Imamate through learning and active leadership, rather than affirming a fixed line of infallible Imams. Their classical theology and law sometimes resemble Sunni and Mu'tazili positions more than Twelver formulations. Zaydism should not be equated wholesale with the contemporary Houthi movement, which is a political-military organization arising in a Zaydi context.
