# Akhbari Twelver Shiism

## Historical development

Muhammad Amin al-Astarabadi's seventeenth-century critique of juristic method energized the Akhbari movement. Akhbaris treated the transmitted reports of the Prophet and imams as the secure basis of law and challenged the expansive authority claimed by Usuli jurists. The school was influential in Iran, Iraq, and Bahrain before Usulism regained predominance.

## Method and survival

Akhbari scholars reject or sharply constrain *ijtihad* based on abstract legal principles and do not organize lay emulation in the same manner as Usulis. Communities and scholarly lineages survive especially around the Persian Gulf. The contrast with Usulism concerns authority and method within Twelver Shiism, not recognition of a different line of imams.
