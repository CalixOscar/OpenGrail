# Nizari Ismailism

## Historical context

Nizari Ismailism arose after a Fatimid succession dispute in 1094, when supporters recognized Nizar rather than al-Musta'li. Nizari communities developed centers in Iran and Syria, including Alamut, and survived Mongol conquest through dispersed networks. In the modern period the Imamate relocated from Iran to South Asia and developed transnational institutions under leaders titled Aga Khan.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Nizaris recognize a present hereditary Imam as authoritative interpreter of faith and ethics. Community life includes the *jamatkhana*, service institutions, and regionally varied devotional literatures such as South Asian ginans. Practices have changed through the Imam's guidance across different historical settings. Medieval European “Assassin” legends sensationalize a political conflict and are not reliable descriptions of Nizari teaching or contemporary communities.
