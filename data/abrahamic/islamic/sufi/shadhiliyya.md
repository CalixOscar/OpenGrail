# Shadhiliyya

## Historical development

Abu al-Hasan al-Shadhili taught in the thirteenth-century Maghreb and Egypt, and disciples including Abu al-Abbas al-Mursi and Ibn Ata Allah systematized his path. Numerous branches spread through North and East Africa, the Middle East, the Indian Ocean, and contemporary Western Muslim communities.

## Teaching and practice

Shadhili traditions commonly emphasize remembrance, gratitude, reliance on God, and spiritual realization while remaining engaged in ordinary work and society. Litanies such as *Hizb al-Bahr* circulate beyond formal initiates. Darqawi, Alawi, Yashruti, and other branches differ substantially, so the name denotes a broad lineage family.
