# Shafi'i School

## Historical context

Muhammad ibn Idris al-Shafi'i studied in Arabia and Iraq before teaching in Egypt in the early ninth century. Works attributed to his mature period helped systematize argument about Qur'an, prophetic sunna, consensus, and analogy. Scholarly and trading networks spread the school along Indian Ocean routes, producing strong communities from Yemen and East Africa to Indonesia.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Shafi'i legal theory gives authenticated prophetic reports a central role and seeks disciplined analogical extension when explicit texts do not settle a case. Later jurists refined rather than merely repeated al-Shafi'i's positions. The school coexists with Ash'ari theology in many regions, but legal and theological affiliation remain different categories. Variation among the four Sunni schools represents accepted jurisprudential pluralism, not four separate religions.
