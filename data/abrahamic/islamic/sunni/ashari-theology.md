# Ash'ari Theology

## Historical context

Ash'arism takes its name from Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari, a tenth-century theologian whose followers developed a major tradition of Sunni dialectical theology. Scholars including al-Baqillani, al-Juwayni, al-Ghazali, and al-Razi expanded its methods in conversation with philosophy and rival schools. It spread among many Shafi'i and Maliki institutions while never becoming their exclusive creed.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Ash'ari theologians affirm divine transcendence and attributes, prophecy, resurrection, and God's comprehensive creative agency, often using occasionalist accounts of causation. Their rational *kalam* aims to defend revelation rather than subordinate it to autonomous philosophy. Maturidis share much common ground but differ on several questions of reason, attributes, and human responsibility. Athari and Salafi critics object to some figurative interpretation and theological method; those debates occur within the broad Sunni field.
