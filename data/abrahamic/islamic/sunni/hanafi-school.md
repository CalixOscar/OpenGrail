# Hanafi School

## Historical context

The Hanafi school formed through Abu Hanifa's eighth-century teaching circle in Kufa and the writings and judicial careers of students such as Abu Yusuf and Muhammad al-Shaybani. Abbasid, Ottoman, Mughal, and other states patronized Hanafi jurists, contributing to its broad geographic reach. Later scholars developed regional commentarial and fatwa traditions.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Hanafi jurisprudence is known for systematic analogical reasoning, attention to juristic preference, and a substantial inherited corpus of hypothetical cases. These features operate within scriptural argument, not instead of it. The four Sunni schools recognize one another as legitimate and differ mainly in method and rulings rather than fundamental creed. Many Hanafi communities historically paired legal study with Maturidi theology, though neither affiliation logically requires the other.
