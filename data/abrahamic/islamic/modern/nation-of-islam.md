# Nation of Islam

## Historical context

The Nation of Islam began with Wallace Fard Muhammad in Detroit in 1930 and expanded under Elijah Muhammad through temples, businesses, disciplined communal programs, and critique of white supremacy. Malcolm X became its best-known minister before leaving in 1964. After Elijah Muhammad's death, Warith Deen Mohammed guided many members toward Sunni Islam, while Louis Farrakhan revived a separate Nation organization.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Classic Nation teachings combine Islamic terms and practices with Black nationalist theology, including accounts of Fard's divinity and Elijah Muhammad's messenger role that differ sharply from Sunni and Shi'a doctrine. The movement has promoted self-reliance and prison ministry while drawing justified criticism for antisemitic, anti-LGBTQ, and racialized statements. Historical classification recognizes an Islamic-derived new religious movement; it should not stand in for African American Islam generally, much of which is Sunni.
