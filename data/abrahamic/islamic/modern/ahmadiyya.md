# Ahmadiyya

## Historical context

Mirza Ghulam Ahmad founded the Ahmadiyya movement at Qadian in Punjab in 1889, presenting himself as a divinely appointed reformer, promised messiah, and mahdi. After his death, followers built a caliphal organization and global missionary network; a 1914 division produced the larger Ahmadiyya Muslim Community and the Lahore Ahmadiyya Movement. Ahmadis have faced severe legal restriction and violence, especially in Pakistan.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Ahmadis affirm the Qur'an, Muhammad, prayer, fasting, and other Islamic practices while interpreting the finality of prophecy in ways most Sunni and Shi'a authorities reject. They teach that Jesus survived crucifixion and later died naturally rather than remaining bodily alive in heaven.
