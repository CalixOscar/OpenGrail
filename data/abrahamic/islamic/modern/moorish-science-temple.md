# Moorish Science Temple of America

## Historical context

The Moorish Science Temple of America (MSTA) was established in 1913 in Newark, New
Jersey, by Noble Drew Ali (born Timothy Drew, 1886–1929). Ali, an African American
railroad worker and visionary teacher, claimed to have traveled to Egypt, where he
was initiated into ancient mystical wisdom by an Egyptian high priest and given a
sacred commission to bring Islam and authentic nationality to "fallen" Black people in
America. Initially organized as the Canaanite Temple, Ali moved his headquarters to
Chicago in 1925, legally incorporating the movement in 1926 and re-chartering it as
the Moorish Science Temple of America in 1928.

During the late 1920s, the MSTA grew with extraordinary speed across northern and
midwestern industrial cities, establishing branches in Chicago, Detroit, Pittsburgh,
Philadelphia, and Baltimore with tens of thousands of members. The movement opened
small businesses, manufactured Moorish herbal health products, and provided social
welfare support during the Great Migration.

In 1929, internal factional disputes erupted over leadership and finances, leading to
the violent death of a rival leader, Claude Greene. Noble Drew Ali was arrested by
Chicago police and held in custody; although released on bond, he died under unclear
circumstances shortly afterward in July 1929 at age 43. Following his death, the
movement fractured into several competing factions (such as the Grand Sheik C.
Kirkman-Bey branch and the Edward Mealy El branch), which continue to operate active
temples across the United States. Concurrently, former Moorish Science members in
Detroit—including Wallace Fard Muhammad—went on to establish the Nation of Islam in 1930.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

The central theological and philosophical doctrine of Moorish Science is that human
beings cannot attain true dignity or citizenship without a recognized national and
ancestral identity. Noble Drew Ali taught that African Americans are not "Negroes,"
"colored," or "black" (terms he rejected as European slave labels), but rather
"Asiatics" of Moorish descent from the ancient empire of Morocco. By discovering their
true ancestry and returning to the "religion of their forefathers" (Islam), Moorish
Americans could achieve divine self-mastery and civic equality.

The primary sacred text of the movement is *The Holy Koran of the Moorish Science
Temple of America* (popularly known as the *Circle Seven Koran*), authored and
compiled by Noble Drew Ali. The text draws upon Levi H. Dowling's *Aquarian Gospel of
Jesus Christ*, Rosicrucian mystical treatises (*Unto Thee I Grant*), and Islamic
concepts, presenting Jesus as an Essene master who studied in India and Egypt before
preaching in Palestine. Adherents attach the noble Moorish tribal suffixes "El" or
"Bey" to their European surnames, carry green nationality identity cards, and wear
distinctive regalia—men wearing the red Turkish fez and women wearing head wraps and
modest dresses. Worship services feature the recitation of the Koran, moral lectures,
and the affirmation of the five holy principles: Love, Truth, Peace, Freedom, and
Justice.
