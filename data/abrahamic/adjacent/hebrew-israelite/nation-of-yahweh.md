# Nation of Yahweh

## Historical context

The Nation of Yahweh was established in 1979 in Miami, Florida, by Hulon Mitchell
Jr. (1935–2007), who adopted the title *Yahweh ben Yahweh* (Hebrew for "God, the Son
of God"). Mitchell, a former civil rights activist and preacher who had previously
belonged to the Nation of Islam, established the movement's headquarters at the
"Temple of Love" in Miami's Liberty City neighborhood.

Throughout the 1980s, the Nation of Yahweh experienced rapid growth, drawing
thousands of followers and building an extensive commercial real estate and business
empire in South Florida valued at over $100 million, including apartment complexes,
supermarkets, hotels, and private schools. The organization was publicly praised by
civic and political leaders, including Miami Mayor Xavier Suarez, for revitalizing
blighted urban neighborhoods and rehabilitating drug addicts and ex-convicts.

Behind this public success, however, internal defectors and law enforcement
investigations revealed a violent authoritarian inner circle known as the
"Brotherhood." In 1990, federal authorities indicted Mitchell and fifteen followers
under the Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations (RICO) Act. In 1992, Mitchell
was convicted of conspiracy to commit racketeering involving fourteen murders, an
attempted murder, arson, and extortion, receiving an eighteen-year federal prison
sentence. Following Mitchell's release on parole in 2001 and his death in 2007, the
Nation of Yahweh reorganized under collective administrative leadership,
maintaining an active following focused on publishing Mitchell's theological
writings and operating online study centers.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Theologically, the Nation of Yahweh is rooted in Black Hebrew Israelite ideology,
asserting that Black people are the original biblical Israelites who were stripped of
their divine heritage, language, and moral laws through slavery. Mitchell taught that
God (*Yahweh*) is Black and that he was the literal, physical incarnation of the Son
of God sent to deliver his people from the "white devil" power structure.

Members of the movement were known for wearing flowing white tunics and turbans,
symbolizing moral purity and spiritual light. The group practiced strict dietary
laws, adhered to the Saturday Sabbath, and emphasized fierce economic self-reliance,
pooling resources to purchase real estate and operate self-sustaining agricultural and
retail enterprises. Unlike mainstream Hebrew Israelite congregations that integrate
traditional rabbinic customs and Talmudic scholarship, the Nation of Yahweh's
theology was strictly Mitchell-centric, viewing his words and sermons as the living
scripture for the modern era.
