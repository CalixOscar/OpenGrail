# Mandaeism

## Historical context

Mandaean communities are historically documented in southern Iraq and southwestern Iran, though the precise formation and earlier geography of the tradition remain debated. Their Eastern Aramaic language preserves an extensive religious literature. War, persecution, and displacement since the late twentieth century have scattered much of the small community across Australia, Europe, and North America, challenging a priest-dependent ritual system.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Mandaeans venerate John the Baptist and practice repeated full immersion in flowing “living water,” alongside complex death rites and priestly ceremonies. Texts describe a world of light, the soul's journey, and saving knowledge, while portraying Jesus negatively rather than as divine. Scholars often call Mandaeism the only surviving Gnostic religion, but “Gnosticism” remains an analytical umbrella rather than a historical parent institution. Mandaeism is neither Christian nor Muslim despite long coexistence with both.
