# Babism

## Historical context

In 1844 Sayyid Ali Muhammad of Shiraz declared himself the Bab, first understood as a gate to the hidden Imam and later as an independent messenger inaugurating a new revelation. His movement spread rapidly in Qajar Iran, met intense clerical and state opposition, and experienced armed confrontations and massacres. The Bab was executed in 1850, but his writings anticipated another messenger to come.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Babi scripture reworks Shi'a messianic expectation through new laws, symbolic exegesis, sacred calendars, and an emphasis on progressive revelation. Most surviving Babis later followed Baha'u'llah and became Baha'is; a smaller Azali community followed Subh-i Azal. Babism's branch edge records emergence from a Twelver setting, not continued membership in Twelver Islam. Its own claims explicitly moved beyond Islamic legal authority.
