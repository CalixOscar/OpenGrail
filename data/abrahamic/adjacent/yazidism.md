# Yazidism

## Historical context

Yazidism formed in northern Mesopotamia around the twelfth-century Sufi figure Sheikh Adi and older regional traditions, then developed as a distinct hereditary community with sacred lineages and a pilgrimage center at Lalish. Yazidis endured repeated persecution, culminating in the Islamic State's 2014 genocide, enslavement, and displacement. Community recovery now spans Iraq and a large diaspora.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Yazidis worship one God and honor seven holy beings led by Tawusi Melek, the Peacock Angel. Oral *qewls*, ritual specialists, seasonal festivals, and endogamous social groups structure communal life. The defamatory claim that Yazidis worship the devil misidentifies Tawusi Melek through an external analogy. Proposed derivation from Zoroastrianism is too simple: Iranian parallels exist, but Yazidism also bears Islamic, Mesopotamian, and local Kurdish layers with no single proven source.
