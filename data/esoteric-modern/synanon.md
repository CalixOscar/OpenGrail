# Synanon

## Historical context

Synanon was founded in Santa Monica, California, in 1958 by Charles E. "Chuck" Dederich Sr. (1913–1997), a recovering alcoholic and former member of Alcoholics Anonymous. Initially established as an innovative residential peer-led rehabilitation program for heroin addicts and alcoholics, Synanon achieved widespread praise from politicians, psychiatrists, and Hollywood figures during the 1960s for its success in treating individuals who had failed in conventional psychiatric institutions.

During the late 1960s, Synanon expanded beyond addiction treatment to admit "lifestylers"—wealthy, non-addicted professionals seeking personal growth, communal solidarity, and psychological honesty. The organization acquired massive real estate holdings across California, including major properties in Santa Monica, Oakland, San Francisco, Tomales Bay in Marin County, and Badger in Tulare County, operating multi-million dollar commercial businesses.

In 1974, seeking legal protection and tax exemption following mounting regulatory disputes with the State of California, Dederich formally reorganized the movement as the "Church of Synanon," declaring that communal living and participation in the "Synanon Game" constituted a complete spiritual religion. Following escalating authoritarian control, weapons stockpiling, and violent criminal retaliation against critics, federal and state legal actions dismantled the organization, culminating in the revocation of its tax-exempt status in 1982 and total corporate bankruptcy and dissolution in 1991.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

In the sociology of religion and communal studies (notably the landmark field research of sociologist Richard Ofshe), Synanon is regarded as a prototypical case of organizational transformation from a therapeutic self-help group into a coercive, authoritarian new religious movement.

The centerpiece of Synanon life was "The Game" (originally "The Synanon Trip"), a raw, uninhibited group encounter session where participants gathered in circles to subject one another to intense verbal attacks, profanity, and emotional confrontation. While intended to break down ego defense mechanisms and denial, The Game gradually evolved into a mechanism of social control used by Dederich to enforce obedience and eliminate dissent.

During the mid-1970s, Dederich's dictates became increasingly erratic and totalitarian. Members were ordered to shave their heads completely and wear uniform denim bib overalls. In 1976, Dederich mandated that all married couples who had been together for more than five years divorce and take new spouses chosen by lottery ("Changing Partners"). In 1977, he mandated vasectomies for all adult male residents, followed by forced abortions for pregnant female members, declaring children an unnecessary burden on the collective.

## Documented harms and controversies

In the late 1970s, as investigative journalists and attorneys began exposing Synanon's internal abuses, Dederich established a private, heavily armed paramilitary security force known as the "Imperial Marines":

- **Assaults on Critics and Defectors**: During 1977 and 1978, Synanon security members carried out beatings and physical assaults on former members, neighbors, and critics in Marin and Tulare counties.
- **Rattlesnake Attack on Paul Morantz (October 1978)**: On 10 October 1978, two Synanon members, Lance Kenton and Joe Musico, acting on recorded audio instructions from Dederich urging retaliation against enemies, placed a four-and-a-half-foot de-rattled diamondback rattlesnake into the mailbox of attorney Paul Morantz in Pacific Palisades. Morantz, who had just won a major civil judgment against Synanon on behalf of an abducted woman, was bitten when opening his mailbox, suffering severe poisoning and surviving only after weeks in intensive care.
- **Convictions and Demise**: In December 1978, Dederich was arrested at the Badger compound in a state of severe intoxication. In 1980, Dederich pleaded no contest to conspiracy to commit murder; he was fined, placed on probation, and barred from participating in the management of Synanon.
- **IRS Revocation and Dissolution (1982–1991)**: In 1982, the Internal Revenue Service revoked Synanon's tax-exempt status due to widespread fraud, terrorism, and illegal firearms stockpiling. Following civil litigation and back-tax judgments exceeding $17 million, Synanon formally ceased all operations in 1991.
