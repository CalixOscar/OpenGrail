# Modern Satanism

## Historical context

Self-identified Satanic organizations emerged publicly in the later twentieth century, although Christian accusations of Satan worship are much older. Modern movements differ sharply: some are atheistic and symbolic, others theistic or esoteric, and some primarily political. The “satanic panic” of the 1980s and 1990s produced allegations of organized ritual abuse unsupported by credible evidence.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Satan may represent autonomy, transgression, rational self-interest, resistance to authority, or an actual spiritual being. No single doctrine speaks for all Satanists. Modern Satanism should be studied through its own organizations and texts rather than through polemical stereotypes; it is also distinct from casual occult imagery and from violence committed without an institutional Satanic context.
