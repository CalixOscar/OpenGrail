# Freemasonry

## Historical context

Organized speculative Freemasonry became publicly visible with British grand lodges in the early eighteenth century, drawing symbols and structures from stonemasons' guild traditions while creating a new associational form. It spread through Europe, the Americas, and imperial networks, producing many rites, jurisdictions, political contexts, and appendant bodies.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Lodge ceremonies use staged degrees and the tools of architecture to teach fellowship, mortality, charity, and moral discipline. Most regular bodies require belief in a Supreme Being while declining to define a creed; others are secular or mixed-gender. Freemasonry is usually an initiatory fraternity rather than a religion, but its ritual and mythic symbolism make it relevant to the atlas. Conspiracy narratives should not replace institutional history.
