# Ramtha's School of Enlightenment

## Historical context

Ramtha's School of Enlightenment (RSE) was founded in 1988 in Yelm, Washington, by Judith Darlene Hampton (known professionally as J. Z. Knight, born 1946). Knight stated that in February 1977, in Tacoma, Washington, she was first contacted by Ramtha the Enlightened One, whom she described as a 35,000-year-old warrior from the ancient continent of Lemuria who attained spiritual ascension.

During the late 1970s and 1980s, Knight toured internationally delivering channeled lectures (termed "Dialogues") while entering an altered trance state. The phenomenon attracted celebrity endorsements (including Shirley MacLaine and Linda Evans) and major media coverage, making Knight a central figure in the 1980s American New Age movement. In 1988, Knight established RSE on an eighty-acre ranch in Yelm, converting the property into a year-round campus with large equestrian arenas, dormitories, and teaching fields.

RSE gained further international visibility in 2004 when several long-time students and faculty produced the independent film *What the Bleep Do We Know!?*, which dramatized Ramtha's blend of quantum physics, neurology, and consciousness creation. The school continues to offer global online seminars, live retreats in Yelm, and regional workshops across Europe, Asia, Latin America, and Australasia.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

RSE is analyzed in religious studies scholarship (notably by J. Gordon Melton and Douglas E. Cowan) as a quintessential expression of modern Western esoteric channeling and the sacralization of the self. Unlike traditional religions centered on external deities or sacrificial redemption, Ramtha teaches radical self-sovereignty: God is not an external judge, but the infinite Void (*Zero Point*) manifest within each individual human mind.

The school's curriculum combines lectures with intensive physical disciplines designed to expand mental focus and overcome neurological conditioning. These include "Fieldwork," where blindfolded students walk through large outdoor paddocks guided solely by intuitive resonance to find a specific paper card they previously drew and posted on a perimeter fence; "C&E" (Consciousness and Energy), a rapid, rhythmic breathing technique intended to circulate spinal energy to the brain's upper centers; and "Tank work," an endurance exercise in lightless labyrinths intended to quiet sensory distraction and awaken latent psychic abilities.
