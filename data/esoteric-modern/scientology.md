# Scientology

## Historical context

L. Ron Hubbard introduced Dianetics in 1950 as a therapeutic system and soon reframed it within Scientology, organized through churches from the mid-1950s. The movement spread internationally through courses, missions, celebrity programs, and tightly managed institutions. It has also generated legal conflict, former-member testimony, state scrutiny, and sustained debate over religion, commerce, and organizational control.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Scientology teaches that the person is an immortal *thetan* whose capacities are constrained by traumatic impressions and a vast spiritual history. Auditing with an E-meter and progressive training seek increasing freedom. Academic description of these beliefs does not validate therapeutic or scientific claims. Legal recognition differs by country, and analysis should distinguish the religion's theology from evidence-based mental-health care.
