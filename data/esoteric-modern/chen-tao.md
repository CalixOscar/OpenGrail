# Chen Tao

## Historical context

Chen Tao (Chinese: 臣道 or 真道, meaning "The True Way"), officially registered in the United States as God's Salvation Church, was founded in Taiwan in 1993 by Hon-Ming Chen (born 1955), a former university sociology lecturer and civil servant. Chen broke away from an existing Taiwanese religious group, the Chinese Soul Light Research Association, establishing an independent movement that combined traditional Taiwanese folk religious concepts, Daoist inner alchemy (*neidan*), Buddhist reincarnation, Christian apocalypticism, and extraterrestrial contactee lore.

In 1997, Chen instructed roughly 150 disciples—primarily educated, middle-class Taiwanese families who liquidated their assets—to relocate to the suburban community of Garland, Texas. Chen chose Garland because its name sounded phonetically like "God Land." In Garland, members purchased a cul-de-sac of suburban homes, established a communal lifestyle, and prepared for a series of specific prophetic events.

Chen prophesied that on 25 March 1998, God would appear across North America on Channel 18 television to announce the commencement of salvation, followed on 31 March 1998 by the physical manifestation of God in human form on Chen's front lawn in Garland. When 25 March passed without a television transmission, Chen addressed international media gathered outside his home, stating that if God did not appear on 31 March, his prophecies could be considered false and members would be free to depart. Following the non-event on 31 March, Chen publicly apologized to the community and journalists, peacefully disbanding the Garland congregation. While many members returned to Taiwan, a remnant faction relocated with Chen to Lockport, New York, continuing to practice meditation in reduced numbers.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Chen Tao is examined in the sociology of religion (notably by J. Gordon Melton, James R. Lewis, and Ryan J. Cook) as a classic case study of cognitive dissonance management and peaceful adaptation following a failed prophecy. Unlike destructive apocalyptic groups that turned inward or turned toward violence upon prophetic disconfirmation, Chen Tao's leadership immediately acknowledged the failure of the prediction without recrimination, violence, or hostility toward the surrounding community.

The movement's cosmology asserted that the universe is divided into three distinct realms: the Spiritual Realm, the Energy Realm, and the Material Realm. Humanity was understood as divine energy beings who became trapped in material bodies through karmic descent. Chen taught that God the Father and the Great Mother resided on a planet named "God's Country" and traveled in vast interstellar spaceships (described as the "clouds of heaven" mentioned in Biblical scripture). Adherents wore white suits, white sneakers, and white cowboy hats, which were believed to reflect spiritual light, repel demonic vibrations, and maintain somatic purity for eventual boarding of the divine spacecraft.
