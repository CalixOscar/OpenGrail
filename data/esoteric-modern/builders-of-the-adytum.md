# Builders of the Adytum

## Historical context

Builders of the Adytum (B.O.T.A., from the Greek *adyton*, the inner sanctuary of an ancient temple) was established in 1922 by the American occultist and musical scholar Paul Foster Case (1884–1954). Case had been initiated in 1918 into the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn's American successor, Alpha et Omega (Thoth-Hermes Temple No. 9 in New York), rising rapidly to Praemonstrator. However, doctrinal disagreements with Moina Mathers regarding Case's public lectures on Tarot, his rejection of Enochian magic as spiritually volatile, and debates over sexual esoteric symbolism led to his resignation and expulsion in 1920.

In 1922, Case formed the School of Ageless Wisdom in Boston and New York, relocating to Los Angeles, California, in the late 1920s and re-incorporating the organization as Builders of the Adytum. Collaborating with occultist and artist Jessie E. Steeves, Case revised and standardized the 22 Major Arcana of the Tarot into the definitive BOTA deck, publishing landmark commentaries such as *The Tarot: A Key to the Wisdom of the Ages* (1947).

Following Case's death in 1954, leadership passed to his spiritual successor and wife, Harriet Case, and occultist Ann Davies (1912–1979). Under Davies's administration, BOTA expanded its global correspondence courses, translated curriculum materials into Spanish, French, and German, and established regional study groups and initiatory chapters across North America, Europe, Latin America, and New Zealand, where the organization remains active today.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

BOTA is widely regarded in the history of Western esotericism as the most rigorous and pedagogically accessible modern systematization of Golden Dawn-derived Hermetic Qabalah. Unlike ceremonial orders that emphasize elaborate theatrical lodge rituals or magical operations, Case structured BOTA primarily around psychological and spiritual transformation, defining the Tarot as an exact visual alphabet designed to reprogram the subconscious mind and align human volition with cosmic law.

A central requirement of BOTA training is the personal coloring of the 22 Major Arcana line drawings according to strict chromatic scales (King Scale and Queen Scale). Students meditate upon these cards while studying accompanying correspondence monographs on the Cube of Space, the Tree of Life, and esoteric sound frequencies. BOTA rejects black magic, astral voyeurism, and commercial divination, presenting its work as a meditative path of self-realization aimed at awakening the "True Ego" (the indwelling divine spirit).
