# Wicca

## Historical context

Wicca became public in Britain through Gerald Gardner in the 1950s, drawing on ceremonial magic, folklore, romantic ideas of witchcraft, fraternal ritual, and collaborators such as Doreen Valiente. Initiatory Gardnerian and Alexandrian traditions spread alongside eclectic, solitary, feminist, and regionally adapted forms. Historical scholarship does not support early claims of an intact prehistoric witch religion.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Wiccan ritual commonly uses a consecrated circle, seasonal festivals, lunar observances, magic, initiation, and devotion to a Goddess and God, though theology varies from polytheism to symbolism. Ethical formulations such as the Wiccan Rede are influential but interpreted differently. Wicca is one modern Pagan religion, not a synonym for all witchcraft or all Paganism.
