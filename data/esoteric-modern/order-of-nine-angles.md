# Order of Nine Angles

## Historical context

The Order of Nine Angles (ONA or O9A) emerged in the United Kingdom during the 1970s as a clandestine esoteric network. Academic research by scholars of Western esotericism and political extremism—including Jacob C. Senholt, Connell Monette, Jesper Aagaard Petersen, and Massimo Introvigne—traces the movement's foundational writings to the British neo-Nazi activist David Myatt (operating under the pseudonym Anton Long), though Myatt has repeatedly denied this identification.

Unlike conventional initiatory societies or fraternal lodges, the ONA has never functioned as a centralized membership organization with formal rosters, physical headquarters, or elected officers. Instead, academic analysts describe it as a decentralized subcultural current and ideological brand propagated through underground zines, self-published pamphlets, and internet forums. The network encourages adherents to form independent, autonomous cells known as "nexions" or to operate entirely as solitary practitioners. Because the group's internal literature is heavily pseudonymous, deliberately contradictory, and frequently revised, religious studies scholars emphasize that its self-published historical claims—such as claims of ancient pre-Christian Wiccan or Camlad heritage—are historical fabrications rather than factual lineages.

In the 2010s and 2020s, the network's decentralized texts were increasingly adopted by violent extremist and neo-Nazi accelerationist groups, leading to extensive academic, security, and legislative scrutiny regarding its role in promoting ideological violence.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

In academic comparative typology, the ONA is classified within the extreme Left-Hand Path and political esotericism. The network consciously rejected the symbolic, atheistic, and law-abiding Satanism established by Anton LaVey's Church of Satan, which it dismissed as bourgeois and conformist. Instead, ONA writings advanced a doctrine of "sinister dialectics," asserting that the current global civilization (which it terms the "Magian" or "Galactic" order) is in terminal decline and must be disrupted through transgressive tactics to foster the emergence of a predatory, pagan-imperialist "Homo Galactica."

Scholars note that the group's esoteric framework incorporates eclectic elements of Hermeticism, ceremonial magic, and an abstract cosmological system known as the "Star Game," played on a three-dimensional board of seven distinct boards. The group's literature prescribes a solitary developmental curriculum known as the "Seven-Fold Way" (*Hebdomadry*), intended to harden the practitioner's psyche through severe physical ordeals, hermetic isolation, and deep immersion in adversarial ideological environments. Academic researchers emphasize that this literature is designed to produce psychological detachment, radicalization, and ethical desensitization.

## Documented harms and controversies

Academic researchers, counter-extremism analysts, and law enforcement agencies classify the Order of Nine Angles as a significant security concern due to the direct incitement of violence and terrorism across its ideological corpus:

- **Terrorism and Extremism Designations**: In several jurisdictions, including the United Kingdom, Canada, and Australia, security agencies and parliamentary committees have reviewed the ONA for formal proscription as a terrorist organization. In 2020, the UK advocacy group Hope not Hate published extensive investigations documenting the network's links to international neo-Nazi terrorist networks, such as the Atomwaffen Division, Feuerkrieg Division, and National Action.
- **Criminal Convictions and Infiltration**: Multiple individuals associated with ONA ideology have been prosecuted and convicted of severe criminal offenses. In June 2020, Ethan Melzer, a member of the United States Army who adhered to ONA and Atomwaffen literature, was arrested and subsequently sentenced in 2023 to 45 years in federal prison for attempting to orchestrate a deadly jihadist ambush against his own deployed military unit. In the United Kingdom and Europe, multiple teenage adherents have been convicted on charges relating to the preparation of terrorist acts, dissemination of terrorist publications, and possession of explosive materials.
- **Predatory Doctrines and Abuse**: Academic studies of the movement document that its core literature advocates predatory violence and human sacrifice (referred to in its texts as *culling*). In 2021, an ONA-affiliated neo-fascist network in Europe was dismantled by counter-terrorism authorities, resulting in criminal convictions for the possession and distribution of abusive material and terrorist manifestos.

Religious studies scholars emphasize that the ONA operates primarily as an incubator of violent radicalization rather than a traditional spiritual community, utilizing esoteric mysticism to legitimize predatory behavior and political violence.
