# Dragon Rouge

## Historical context

*Dragon Rouge* (formally *Ordo Draconis et Atri Adamantis*, the Order of the Dragon and the Red Diamond) was founded on New Year's Eve in 1989 in Stockholm, Sweden, by Thomas Karlsson alongside a small circle of young occultists. Karlsson, who later earned a doctorate in the history of religions from Stockholm University, developed the order's extensive ritual and theoretical curriculum throughout the 1990s, synthesizing classical Western esotericism with Scandinavian mythology, Hermetic Kabbalah, and Eastern tantric concepts.

The order expanded internationally during the late 1990s and 2000s, establishing ceremonial lodges, ritual groups, and study circles across Sweden, Germany, Poland, Italy, Greece, Mexico, and North America. It gained notable cultural visibility through Karlsson's long-standing lyrical collaboration with the Swedish symphonic metal band Therion, which incorporated Dragon Rouge's mythological themes, runic lore, and Qliphothic concepts into its albums and international performances.

Dragon Rouge remains an active, international esoteric organization. It maintains a formal headquarters in Sweden and holds regular seasonal gatherings, philosophical seminars, and initiatory convocations across Europe and the Americas.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Dragon Rouge is distinguished in academic studies of contemporary esotericism—particularly the comprehensive sociological field research of scholar Kennet Granholm—as one of the most intellectually developed orders of the modern Left-Hand Path. Unlike secular or hedonistic variants of Satanism, Dragon Rouge is an explicitly magical and religious initiatory order centered on spiritual metaphysics, personal transformation, and the systematic exploration of the unconscious.

The order's initiatory curriculum is structured around eleven degrees corresponding to the eleven spheres of the Qliphoth (the dark or shadow aspect of the Kabbalistic Tree of Life, beginning with Lilith and ascending to Thaumiel). Initiates undergo rigorous astral pathworking and ritual invocations designed to confront and integrate repressed subconscious archetypes. Alongside the Qliphoth, the order teaches the "Uthark" theory of the runic alphabet (pioneered by Swedish philologist Sigurd Agrell), which reads the Elder Futhark as an initiatory cipher of creation and destruction, combined with the awakening of the inner "Dragon" (analogous to the tantric Kundalini).
