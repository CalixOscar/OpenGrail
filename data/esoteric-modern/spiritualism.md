# Modern Spiritualism

## Historical context

Modern Spiritualism emerged in the United States around the 1848 phenomena associated with the Fox sisters and rapidly became a transatlantic movement. Public mediums, home circles, reform networks, churches, investigators, and skeptics all shaped it. Fraud existed alongside sincere practice, and controversy over evidence became part of Spiritualism's public identity.

## Distinctives and comparative notes

Spiritualists seek communication with deceased persons through trance speech, automatic writing, healing, or other forms of mediumship. Many interpret such contact as empirical confirmation of moral progress after death. Institutions range from Christian Spiritualist churches to metaphysically eclectic associations. Mediumship has precedents worldwide, but modern Spiritualism is historically specific and should not be projected onto every ancestral or possession practice.
